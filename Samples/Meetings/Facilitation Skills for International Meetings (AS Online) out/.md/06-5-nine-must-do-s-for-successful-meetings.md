# 5 Nine Must Do’s for Successful Meetings

- **Common problems with meetings…**

> Anyone who has attended a meeting has experienced at least some of these problems.

- Beginning discussion before all background information is presented.

- Discussion wanders from issue to issue without focus.

- The participants introduce ideas unrelated to the agenda.

- Participants don’t speak because there are senior staff members in the meeting.

- Outspoken people tend to dominate the discussion and control the decisions.

- Quieter people tend not to express their ideas.

- People who don’t listen repeat other people’s ideas.

- Unprepared people talk off-issue.

- The meeting is long and boring and ends without clear decisions.

<!-- -->

- **… And nine ways to deal with them**

> The effective facilitator can avoid and overcome these common problems by following the nine must do’s for successful meetings.

1.  **Inform and remind the participants of the purpose of the meeting**

> Knowing the date, time, and place of the meeting is necessary but not enough information.
>
> If the participants know the issues and purpose of the meeting they can prepare for and participate in the meeting.

2.  **Optimize the number of participants**

> Each meeting should have “the right number” of participants.

- Too many or too few participants will limit the success of the meeting.

- The “right number” depends on the purpose of the meeting.

  1.  **Establish and follow an agenda**

> Every meeting must have an agenda. The agenda is the plan for the meeting and helps to keep the participants on track.

- The agenda includes the issues for discussion, the topic leaders, and the time allocated for each item.

- The items on the agenda should be written in order from first to last.

- Everyone in the meeting should have the agenda so they can focus on the issues.

  1.  **State the purpose and goal of the meeting**

> At the beginning of the meeting clearly state the purpose and goal of the meeting, and review the agenda.

- Do not assume that the participants are thinking about or understand the purpose and goal of the meeting.

- Reviewing the purpose and agenda gets the meeting off to a good start and helps to keep it on track.

  1.  **Involve each participant**

> Encourage all the participants to speak.

- Meetings can often get off balance when a few attendees do all the talking and dominate the discussion.

- These individuals naturally tend to control the decision making.

  1.  **Use diagrams, pictures, and graphics**

> There is truth in the old saying, “a picture is worth a thousand words”.

- Pictures can shorten meetings and make them more effective.

- Good diagrams can shorten meeting durations by as much as 25%.

- Whenever practical, encourage participants to use diagrams, pictures, and graphics instead of handing out pages full of text.

  1.  **Set aside important but unrelated topics**

> Keep the meeting on track by following the agenda.

- Do not let unrelated items enter the discussion even if they are important.

- The effective facilitator needs to protect the agenda from unrelated items: unrelated items must be set aside for a separate meeting.

  1.  **Record & review decisions**

> Decisions must be written down.

- If decisions are not recorded they might not be acted on.

- Meetings need an official note taker to record decisions and review them at the close of the meeting.

  1.  **Distribute a meeting report**

> Right after the meeting, prepare and distribute a brief summary of the meeting’s results and decisions to all the participants.

- The official minutes or report with all the details can be prepared later.
