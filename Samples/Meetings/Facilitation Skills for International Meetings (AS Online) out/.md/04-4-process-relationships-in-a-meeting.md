# 4 Process & Relationships in a Meeting

- **Process of a Meeting**

> An important part of the process of a meeting is its structure. In the structure of a meeting there are six stages.

- Just as a building needs a structure, a meeting needs a structure in order to succeed.

- Each stage of the meeting is important. Some stages are brief and can be done quickly while other stages take more time.

- **The Agenda**

> The meeting has one agenda. It is road map for the discussion and decisions.

- The agenda is the list of items to be discussed in the meeting. There are usually three or more items on the agenda.

> **\*Word Note:** The “agenda” is the list. The items on the agenda are called “items”.
>
> In Japanese the word “agenda” has a different meaning. It means the items for discussion. This is acceptable in Japanese however it is not correct in English.

- **The Meeting Report**

> The report is essential because we cannot rely on our memories.
>
> The report should include -

- The date, time, and duration of the meeting.

- Names of participants and their roles.

- Items on the agenda.

- Items on the agenda actually discussed.

- Decisions that are made: who, what, when.

<!-- -->

- **Process and Relationships**

> Two important elements in all meetings are -

- the step-by-step process of the meeting

- the relationships among the participants

> The meeting process and relationships work together to make a successful meeting.
>
> It is the facilitator’s job to balance these two elements.

- **The Meeting: A Dynamic Process**

> A meeting is a dynamic process involving several elements.
