- **The facilitator’s role**

> Discussing an issue can be fairly easy; however, bringing that discussion to a conclusion can be very difficult.
>
> The facilitator needs to be able to guide the participants toward a decision without making the decision for them.

- The true facilitator is neutral in the discussion and decision making.

- He or she has no vested interest in the outcome of the meeting.

  - **Guiding a discussion toward a conclusion**

> The facilitator can guide the discussion in various ways:

- Begin the discussion by clearly stating the issue to be discussed.

<!-- -->

- Keep the discussion focused on the issue.

- Set aside off-issue comments.

<!-- -->

- Make sure that all the background information is presented before the discussion begins.

<!-- -->

- This is a critical first step.

<!-- -->

- Encourage discussion to find common points and points of disagreement.

<!-- -->

- Make sure all the participants have an opportunity to speak.

<!-- -->

- Summarize the points from the discussion.

<!-- -->

- Group the points into common issues.

- Identify the points agreed upon.

- Identify the points of disagreement.

<!-- -->

- When an agreeable proposal seems to appear, restate it to the participants.

<!-- -->

- In your restatement, include all the details and ask for confirmation from all the participants.

- The note taker will record your restatement.

<!-- -->

- If there is no clear agreement, clearly state the two positions.

<!-- -->

- Emphasize the points of agreement and clearly state the differences.

- Ask the participants to focus on these options.

- Allow for new proposals that will overcome the deadlock.

<!-- -->

- If no consensus appears, suggest…

<!-- -->

- making a decision by majority vote, or

- leaving the issue undecided and scheduling a new meeting.

  - **Decisions: What, Who, by When?**

> A decision is the conclusion following thought, and possibly discussion, about a future course of action which includes three key elements:
>
> 1\. <u>What</u> is to be done?
>
> 2\. <u>Who</u> is to do it?
>
> 3\. <u>When</u> is the deadline?
>
> If one of the elements is missing, there is no decision!

- **Coming to a decision**

> There are three basic ways to come to a decision in a meeting.
>
> The facilitator must be aware of which process is required and guide the meeting accordingly.

- **Consensus** All the participants agree on and accept one idea.

<!-- -->

- Consensus is achieved by all the participants expressing their own point of view.

- Discussion continues until consensus is achieved.

- Simply agreeing with the first speaker in order to avoid conflict or save time is not achieving a consensus.

- Achieving consensus takes time and it is not always easy.

- Deciding by consensus maximizes the support for the decision and assures the decision will be carried out.

<!-- -->

- **Majority** The idea that most participants support is accepted as the decision.

<!-- -->

- To establish a majority position a vote must be carried out, usually by a show of hands for and against. The facilitator must announce the number of votes for and against.

- A majority vote is one way of overcoming a deadlock when consensus cannot be achieved.

- Because there was full discussion, compliance with the decision can be expected but not guaranteed. Those who voted against the proposal may hold back their support later.

<!-- -->

- **Authority** One person in authority in the meeting makes the decision.

<!-- -->

- The authority will listen to the discussion of differing ideas and then make a decision.

- The authority can stop the discussion at any time even before all the ideas have been presented.
