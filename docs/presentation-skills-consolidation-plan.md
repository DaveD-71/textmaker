## 4. Design decisions

Per methodology Step 4. Resolved items are marked; open items need your sign-off before drafting.

1. **Framework naming/synthesis** — **Resolved: best-of synthesis.** For each shared framework,
   merge the strongest version across sources rather than picking one book as base or keeping
   variants. E.g. Making Speeches' Theme/Message distinction + Speaking with PowerPoint's fuller
   Logic Tree worked examples + Speaking with PowerPoint's usage-error notes for Flag
   Expressions.

2. **Audience-fork handling** — **Resolved: eliminate the businessperson/government split at
   book level.** Confirmed empirically (Section 3, BPE pair): only 2 of 7–8 units per book carry
   real audience flavor, and in only one case (Government's policy speech) is the content
   substantively different rather than just a relabeled scenario. Audience variants fold into
   paired models/examples within shared units (see Section 5, Units 3, 8, 15) rather than
   separate books or separate units.

3. **Modernization scope** — **Resolved: build new content this pass, treat "online" as a
   cross-cutting consideration rather than a single bolt-on unit.** Online/hybrid delivery is
   now common enough that it needs a presence in nearly every unit — planning, structure,
   delivery, slide design, chairing, Q&A — not just one standalone module. Revised approach:
   every relevant unit in Section 5 gets an explicit "online considerations" component (e.g.
   Structure gets notes on pacing for shorter attention spans on video; Delivery Skills gets
   webcam eye-contact/framing alongside in-room posture; Slide Design gets remote-screen-share
   legibility; Chairing gets virtual hand-raise/mute management). A short dedicated unit (Unit 13,
   Online & Hybrid Delivery Mechanics) remains for online-specific mechanics that don't fit inside
   another unit (platform controls, screen-share setup, recording/backup considerations), but it
   is no longer the sole home for this content. Other modernization items (dated PowerPoint UI
   references, pre-cloud backup advice, infrared pointer warnings, dated dress-code framing,
   accessibility/contrast guidance) remain in-scope fixes within their existing units, not
   deferred.

4. **Model speech placement** — **Resolved: move all model speeches out of the main tiered unit
   list into an Appendices section.** Rather than Units 3, 8, and 15 each being a standalone
   "Model Speech: X" unit, the underlying skill units keep their instructional content and
   exercises, and point to a paired business/government model in the appendix (e.g. "see Appendix
   A: Self-Introduction Models"). This directly serves the businessperson/government
   standardization goal from Decision 2 — one shared unit teaches the skill, the appendix holds
   parallel worked examples per audience, and adding a third audience variant later (or updating
   a dated scenario) doesn't require touching unit structure at all. See Section 5 for the
   revised unit list and the new Appendix structure.

5. **Tier-inclusion granularity** — **Resolved.** Which units belong to which tier is settled by
   the table in Section 5 itself: hard numeric caps (Essentials ≤ 8, Standard ≤ 12, Long = all),
   with the Standard-too-close-to-Long concern specifically addressed by splitting the former
   Units 10–11 into four units (Logic, Persuasion, Analyze, Propose), Persuasion and Propose
   landing Long-only. Current counts: Essentials 8/15, Standard 12/15, Long 15/15. The table *is*
   the decision record — no separate sign-off needed beyond it being correct as written.

   **Section 5b's activity tier-scaling is also now approved as written** (how each individual
   activity's scope/prep time/independence changes between Essentials, Standard, and Long).
   Originally held open pending review of actual drafted activities, rather than the planning-
   stage tier-scaling notes alone — approved at the planning stage on 2026-07-03 without waiting
   for drafts. This is an approval of the *design intent* in Section 5b's table; Stage 2 drafting
   should still follow those notes faithfully, and Stage 6 (Content & pedagogy review, Section 7)
   remains the checkpoint for catching cases where a drafted activity doesn't actually deliver the
   scaling described (e.g. an "abridged" version that reads identically to its full counterpart) —
   approval of the plan doesn't guarantee the execution matches it.

6. **Manuscript structure** — **Resolved: three separate manuscripts, one each for Essentials,
   Standard, and Long.** Not a single tagged/filtered source. Each tier gets its own Markdown
   file(s) built independently through `markdown-to-docx`, sharing unit content by copy/adaptation
   rather than a shared-source-with-tags mechanism. This has a direct consequence for Section 7's
   Stage 2/4: drafting and building happen three times, not once with three filtered outputs, and
   any future content fix must be applied in up to three places (the same unit may exist at
   abridged depth in one manuscript and full depth in another, as separate text, not a shared
   fragment).

7. **Framework naming specifics** — **Resolved after content comparison, not assumption.** Direct
   text comparison of Making Speeches' "Four Keys" (`02-2-keys-to-successful-presentations.md`)
   against Speaking with PowerPoint's "6 Keys" (`01-1-keys-to-successful-presentations.md`) showed
   they are **not two different frameworks** — they cover the same four ideas at different
   granularity, plus one genuinely additional item:

   | Making Speeches (4 keys) | Speaking w/ PPT (6 keys) | Relationship |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1. Purpose & Content | 1. Purpose + 2. Content | Same idea, MS merges what SwPPT splits |
   | 2. Structure | 3. Structure | Identical — near-verbatim wording |
   | 3. Language (Flag Expressions) | 4. Language (Flag Expressions) | Identical — same definition, same two bullets, word-for-word |
   | 4. Delivery Skills | 6. Delivery | Identical — same core sentence |
   | *(not present)* | 5. PowerPoint | **The only genuinely additional item** |

   A combined "10 Keys" would double-count the same three-to-four ideas under six labels — not a
   real option. **Decision: adopt Speaking with PowerPoint's 6-key structure and granularity**
   (Purpose, Content, Structure, Language, Delivery, Visual Aids as six distinct keys, Purpose and
   Content kept separate rather than merged) — since Decision 3 already established visual aids as
   core content at every tier, Visual Aids earns its own key rather than being folded into Content.
   Canonical name: **"Keys to Successful Presentations"** (drop "6"/"Four" — the number is now an
   implementation detail, not part of the brand name, so it doesn't need updating if the key count
   changes later).

   **2026-07-07 revision (supersedes the flat 6-key list above):** during content review, the
   user identified real conceptual problems with the flat six-key checklist: "Language" was too
   vague a label for what the drafted text actually described (a narrow script-editing pass), and
   ordering Visual Aids last implied slides get decided after Delivery, which is backwards — what
   goes on a screen constrains what Delivery can do, so Visual Aids has to be planned first. The
   discussion also surfaced that "Message" (already load-bearing in Unit 2's Theme-vs-Message
   content) had never been formally defined against Purpose or Content, despite the three terms
   sounding related. Resolved: the flat 6-key list is replaced by a **3 Phases of Preparation**
   framework — **Planning** (Purpose → Message → Content → Structure → Visual Aids), **Writing**
   (Drafting → Honing), **Practice** (Delivery) — 8 named elements across 3 sequential phases,
   with an explicit rationale for the ordering (Message distinguished from Purpose/Content;
   Structure requires Content to exist first; Visual Aids precedes Delivery because it constrains
   it; Honing is a distinct pass from Drafting because spoken and written language behave
   differently). Unit 1 (all 3 tiers) has been rewritten accordingly. This is a genuine framework
   change, not a relabeling — "the 6 Keys" and "Language" as a named key should not appear
   anywhere else in the manuscript going forward.

8. **Learner-audience framing** — **Resolved: generalize.** Drop the Japanese-L1-specific framing
   (pronunciation notes tied to "overcoming katakana," etc.) and write for a broader non-native-
   English-speaker audience. Content that is genuinely about common cross-L1 pronunciation/prosody
   challenges can stay; content that only makes sense to a Japanese L1 reader needs rewriting or
   removal during Stage 2 drafting.

9. **New model speech content** — **Resolved: all model speeches will be freshly written**, not
   refreshed/updated versions of the Ventura car pitch (Appendix B) or Cool Biz speech (Appendix
   C). Both need fully current contexts, not a data/date patch on the existing scenarios. Applies
   to every appendix model, business and government variants alike — none of the current source
   speeches carry over as a light edit.

10. **Learning activities per unit** — **Resolved: every unit gets a designed activity, not just
    content delivery.** User flagged that the source books read as instructor/reference guides
    rather than learner-facing textbooks — most units were pure exposition with no built-in
    developmental activity, and where an activity existed it was often a single throwaway line.
    See Section 5b for the full per-unit activity design: 7 of 15 units extend a genuine source
    precedent, 8 of 15 needed a newly designed activity because no source activity existed for
    that content. Every activity is tier-scaled (Essentials/Standard/Long) alongside content
    tiering rather than deferred to drafting.

## 5. Proposed unified unit list and tier mapping

**Tier caps (user-set guideline):** Essentials ≤ 8 taught units, Standard ≤ 12 taught units, Long =
all taught units (no cap — currently 15). These are hard caps on *unit count*, not just depth —
the earlier draft of this table let "abridged" count as "present," which produced a
14-of-15-unit Standard tier barely distinguishable from Long. That approach is retired. A tier
either includes a unit or it doesn't; "abridged" now only describes reduced depth *within* a unit
a tier already includes, never a way to sneak a unit in under the cap.

**Units 10–13 (Logic, Persuasion, Analyze, Propose, in that order) replace the former Units 10–11**
(formerly "Description, Logic & Persuasion" and "Critical Thinking: Analyze & Propose / Argue &
Defend"). The order groups each pair by topic rather than by source book: Logic is immediately
followed by Persuasion (its Long-only extension), then Analyze is immediately followed by Propose
(its Long-only extension) — so a Long-tier learner experiences each skill and its advanced
extension back-to-back rather than encountering all four source-book-grouped units in a block.
Splitting two units into four gives Long genuinely exclusive content — Persuasion and Propose are
Long-only, not deeper versions of material Standard already had — while Standard's unit count is
unaffected: Standard only ever included the Logic and Analyze halves, so it still gets exactly 2
units from this cluster, just as it did before the split (see the counts note after the table).
This makes Standard non-contiguous across this stretch (present at 10 and 12, absent at 11 and
13) rather than a single clean block, which is a deliberate tradeoff for the topic-grouped order.

**Essentials is grounded in the existing BPE Essentials books' actual unit set**, not a fresh
guess: both source books (Businesspeople 8 units, Government 7 units) taught Structure, Delivery
Skills, Flag Expressions ("How to Say It"), Logic Tree, Chairing, and a capstone
(Quick Speech/Introducing the Speaker) — with model speeches now relocated to Section 5a per
Decision 4. That gives 6 direct carry-overs (Units 1–5, 7) plus the capstone (Unit 8) = 7 units,
plus Slide Design (Unit 6, abridged) to reach 8 — since visual aids are now baseline expectation
even at essentials level, unlike when the BPE books were written with no slide content at all.

**Online & Hybrid Delivery Mechanics is cut entirely as a standalone unit**, not just
tier-limited. Decision 3 already committed to weaving online/hybrid considerations into every
relevant unit rather than housing them in one bolt-on module — keeping a dedicated mechanics unit
*in addition to* that was redundant scope creep. Platform-specific mechanics (recording,
connectivity fallback) fold directly into Unit 4 (Delivery Skills) instead of occupying their own
slot.

The Expressions Reference Appendix does **not** count against the unit caps — like the model-speech
appendices in Section 5a, it is reference material that grows in depth per tier rather than a
taught lesson, so it is listed separately at the end of the table rather than occupying a numbered
slot.

### 5b. Learning activities per unit

**Gap identified by user:** the four source books read more as instructor/reference guides than
learner-facing textbooks — most units are pure exposition (a framework explained, a phrase table,
a rules list) with no built-in developmental activity. Where an activity did exist, it was often
a single line ("write your own version") rather than a designed exercise. Every unit in the
unified book needs an actual learning activity, not just content delivery.

**Design approach:** where a source book already had a real activity for a unit's topic (even a
thin one), that activity is the starting point and gets extended/tier-scaled rather than
discarded. Where no source activity exists (true for roughly half the units — pure exposition
units had none), a new activity is designed to match the unit's actual skill, not a generic
template repeated across units. Every activity gets a tier note: what changes in scope, prep
time, or independence between Essentials, Standard, and Long — this is planned alongside content
tiering, not deferred to drafting.

Every activity above either extends a genuine source-book precedent (7 of 15 units: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8,

which activities have prior art to adapt and which need to be built from scratch. Units 11 and 13
are deliberately built to reuse Unit 10's and Unit 12's work respectively (the same evidence

like a continuation of the unit immediately before them, not a bolt-on, and is why Persuasion sits
directly after Logic and Propose sits directly after Analyze rather than being grouped by source
