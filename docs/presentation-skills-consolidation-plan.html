04

Design decisions

Per the consolidation methodology, every decision is logged and signed off explicitly — none are assumed defaults.

01
Framework naming & synthesis Resolved
Best-of synthesis per framework, not single-book-as-base. E.g. Making Speeches’ Theme/Message + Speaking with PowerPoint’s fuller Logic Tree examples and Flag Expressions usage notes.
02
Audience-fork handling Resolved
Eliminate the businessperson / government split at book level. Only 2 of 7–8 units per BPE book carried real audience flavor; variants fold into paired models within shared units (see Units 3, 8, 15 below).
03
Modernization scope Resolved
Online presenting is now common enough to be a cross-cutting consideration, not a single bolt-on module. Revised: nearly every unit gets a woven-in “online consideration” (pacing, webcam framing, screen-share legibility, virtual hand-raise, and so on); a narrower Unit 13 remains for mechanics that don’t fit inside another unit — platform controls, recording, connectivity fallback. Other dated references stay in-scope fixes, not deferred.
04
Model speech placement Resolved
Model speeches move out of the main unit list into Appendices (§05a). Skill units keep their instruction and exercises and point to a paired business/government model instead of hosting it — the direct mechanism for retiring the audience split at book level (Decision 02).
05
Tier-inclusion granularity Resolved
Which units belong to which tier is settled by the §05 table itself — hard caps (E≤8, S≤12, L=all), Standard-too-close-to-Long fixed by splitting former Units 10–11 into four (Logic → Persuasion, Long-only → Analyze → Propose, Long-only). Counts: Essentials 8/15, Standard 12/15, Long 15/15. The table is the decision record — no separate sign-off needed.
§05b’s activity tier-scaling (how each activity’s depth changes between tiers) is now also approved as written Resolved — approved at the planning stage without waiting for drafts. This is approval of design intent; Stage 6 (Content & pedagogy review) remains the checkpoint for verifying drafted activities actually deliver the scaling described.
06
Manuscript structure Resolved
Three separate manuscripts — one each for Essentials, Standard, and Long — not a single tagged/filtered source. Consequence: drafting and building happen three times, and any future content fix propagates to up to three files, not one shared fragment.
07
“Keys” framework naming Resolved
User rejected a bare A-or-B choice, asking whether the frameworks actually overlap. Read both source units word-for-word rather than assuming — found they cover the same four ideas at different granularity, plus one genuinely additional item. A combined “10 Keys” would double-count and was not adopted. Resolved: 6 keys at Speaking with PowerPoint’s granularity (Purpose, Content, Structure, Language, Delivery, Visual Aids); canonical name “Keys to Successful Presentations” (number dropped from the brand name). 08
Learner-audience framing Resolved
Generalize away from Japanese-L1-specific framing (pronunciation notes tied to “overcoming katakana,” etc.) for a broader non-native-English-speaker audience. Genuinely cross-L1 pronunciation/prosody content can stay; content that only makes sense to a Japanese L1 reader needs rewriting or removal during Stage 2 drafting.
09
New model-speech content Resolved
All appendix model speeches will be freshly written, not refreshed/updated versions of the Ventura car pitch (Appendix B) or Cool Biz speech (Appendix C). Both need fully current contexts, not a data/date patch — applies to every appendix model, business and government variants alike.
10
Learning activities per unit Resolved
The source books read as instructor/reference guides, not learner-facing textbooks — most units were pure exposition with no built-in developmental activity. Every one of the 15 taught units now gets a designed, tier-scaled activity (§05b): 7 extend a genuine source precedent, 8 are newly designed where no source activity existed.
05

Proposed unit list & tier map

E — Essentials · S — Standard · L — Long. Hard caps now enforce unit count, not just depth: Essentials ≤ 8 taught units, Standard ≤ 12, Long = all 15. “Abridged” describes reduced depth within a unit a tier already includes — it no longer counts as a way to fit a unit under the cap. Model speeches don’t anchor a unit — see §05a.

Essentials is rebuilt from the existing BPE Essentials books’ actual unit set (Structure, Delivery, Flag Expressions, Logic Tree, Chairing, capstone), not a fresh guess — plus Slide Design, since visual aids are now baseline even at essentials level. The standalone Online & Hybrid Delivery Mechanics unit is cut entirely and folded into Delivery Skills, since Decision 03 already committed to weaving online considerations through every unit rather than housing them in one module.

Units 10–13 (Logic, Persuasion, Analyze, Propose, in that order) replace the former Units 10–11 (“Description, Logic & Persuasion” and “Critical Thinking”). Order groups by topic, not source book: Persuasion sits directly after Logic (its Long-only extension), and Propose sits directly after Analyze (its Long-only extension). Standard keeps exactly the 2 units it always had from this cluster (Logic, Analyze), while Persuasion and Propose are new Long-only units — Standard's count is unaffected, just non-contiguous.

2026‑07‑07 addendum: a 4th appendix (Logic Tree Worked Models, above) was added during Stage 2 drafting to fix a defect — Unit 5’s activity had incorrectly pointed learners to Appendix B, which is paired with Units 10 & 11 and appears later in reading order, not with Unit 5. Because this new appendix’s correct position (right after Self-Introduction Models) sorts before the original Appendix B and C, the A/B/C lettering scheme was retired in favor of referring to every appendix by name only, in both learner-facing text and this table’s own labels going forward.

05b

Learning activities per unit

Gap identified by user: the source books read as instructor/reference guides, not learner-facing textbooks — most units are pure exposition with no built-in developmental activity, and where one existed it was often a single throwaway line. Every unit below gets a designed, tier-scaled activity.

01

Why Presentations Matter

Self-assessmentNew

Learner completes a short “presentation history” inventory (past presentations given, what worked/didn’t) and states one personal goal for the course. No source precedent — keeps Unit 1 from being pure lecture.

E 5-minute written checklist  ·  S/L adds a peer share-out to start the speaking-practice habit immediately
02

Structure of a Speech

Critique → draftExtends source

Extends Making Speeches’ “Deciding the Message” critique (evaluate weak vs. strong candidate messages) into a two-step task: critique first, then draft an Introduction/Body/Conclusion outline for Appendix A’s self-introduction model.

E critique step + 3-line outline  ·  S full critique + outline + theme/message pair  ·  L adds a peer-review swap before Unit 3
03

Flag Expressions

Drill → applicationNew

Fill-in-the-blank drill on the flag-expression table (source books had zero practice activity here — pure reference), then learner inserts flag expressions into the Unit 2 outline already drafted.

E drill only, reduced phrase set  ·  S drill + application to own outline  ·  L adds a timed flag-spotting listening exercise
04

Delivery Skills

Peer observationExtends source

Extends the source books’ teacher-modeled practice into a paired activity: learner delivers 60 seconds of the Unit 2 draft while a partner checks a delivery-skills observation sheet, then swaps. Adds a webcam self-review pass for the online consideration.

E single pass, 3 core skills  ·  S full checklist, peer + self-review  ·  L second round after applying feedback
05

Logic Tree

Build-your-ownExtends source

Makes the blank-template-plus-worked-sample pattern (present in all four source books) explicit: learner completes their own Logic Tree for a topic of choice, using Appendix B’s worked example as a model.

E 3 branches, pre-labeled template  ·  S blank template  ·  L adds a “tree swap” reconstruction exercise
06

Slide Design

Redesign / critiqueNew

No source activity existed (only a pre-presentation checklist). Learner is given a deliberately bad slide and redesigns it against the Rule of 1/Rule of 7 and accessibility guidance.

E single slide, guided checklist  ·  S 3-slide mini-deck  ·  L redesigns twice — projection and remote screen-share thumbnail
07

Chairing a Session

Role-playExtends source

Extends the rotating-chair mechanic already in the source books’ capstone activities into its own drill: small groups rotate chairperson, speaker, and questioner roles using the Unit 3 phrase banks.

E intro one speaker + one question  ·  S full session cycle  ·  L adds virtual hand-raise/mute practice on a live call
08

Capstone Practice Activity

Timed circuitExtends source

Already a full activity in source (“Quick Draw Presentations”, “Quick Speech”) — the one unit where activity and content are the same thing. Only needs tier-scaling.

E single round, ~10 min  ·  S full clocked circuit, ~15 min  ·  L two rounds, second on a self-chosen topic
09

Speaking Clearly

Annotated practiceExtends source

Extends the source books’ annotated-transcript method (mark pause/stress notation on a model, then on your own text) — already real, just needs tier scaling.

E markup on Unit 2 self-intro only  ·  S markup + recorded read-aloud  ·  L adds mic-technique practice for online delivery
10

Logic

Evidence chainNew

No source activity (pure exposition). Learner builds a Statement → Evidence → Analysis → Conclusion chain for a claim in their Appendix B model, mirroring the source’s Ventura worked example — structural/reasoning only, no persuasive-appeal content.

S one chain, guided template  ·  L same, plus the chain feeds directly into Unit 11’s persuasion activity
11

Persuasion

Persuasive reframeNew

Long-exclusive: learner takes the Unit 10 evidence chain for the same Appendix B model and reframes it using the 3 Elements / 5 Stages of Persuasion — same content, now built for persuasive effect, making the Logic/Persuasion distinction concrete.

L only full 5-stage persuasive reframe of the Unit 10 chain, done immediately after Logic
12

Analyze (problem-solving / reporting)

Problem analysis taskExtends source

Extends the source’s point-form sample framing (the Tokyo crowded-trains example) into a full learner task: pick a real problem, apply Background/Situation/Results to explain it and report findings — stops short of recommending action, that’s Unit 13.

S problem from a short list, guided template  ·  L learner sources their own problem
13

Propose (incl. argue & defend)

Recommendation + defenseNew

Long-exclusive, continuing directly from Unit 12: using the same problem analyzed there, learner proposes a recommended action and defends it against a counterargument — the source’s “Argue & Defend” genre folded in as the defense component.

L only recommendation + defense on the learner’s own Unit 12 problem, done immediately after Analyze
14

Delivering with Slides & Handouts

Rehearsal + feedbackNew

No direct source activity (rules and stage directions only, no rehearsal task). Learner delivers their Unit 6 redesigned slides aloud; partner gives feedback on speech/slide synchronization.

S single rehearsal pass  ·  L adds a handout-pacing variant of the same content
15

Team Presentations

Group projectNew

No source activity (language/structure guidance only). Teams of 2–3 divide a topic, each member drafts their section using Units 2–4 skills, then deliver together using the handoff language from the unit.

L only full team-planning-to-delivery cycle, including team-intro and colleague-reference requirements from source content

7 of 15 activities extend a genuine source-book precedent (Units 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12); 8 of 15 are newly designed because no source activity existed for that content (Units 1, 3, 6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15) — flagged per-card above so drafting knows which have prior art to adapt. Units 11 and 13 deliberately reuse Unit 10’s and Unit 12’s work rather than starting fresh.

06

Modernization flags

Raised independently across all four content maps — named here rather than silently fixed or silently dropped.

  • PowerPoint UI references — describes an old template-picker workflow no longer accurate.
  • Pre-cloud backup advice — thumb drives, emailing files to yourself.
  • Infrared pointer warnings — dated hardware concern.
  • Dress-code framing — “check your tie, jacket collar” skews dated and gendered.
  • No accessibility guidance in slide design — contrast, colorblind-safe palettes.
  • No virtual/hybrid content anywhere — addressed by weaving online considerations through every relevant unit, with platform/recording mechanics folded into Delivery Skills rather than a standalone module (Decision 03).
  • Dated set-piece scenarios — Ventura car pitch (2014 hybrid sedan, Appendix B), Cool Biz speech (2005–2011 policy data, Appendix C). See Decision 09.
