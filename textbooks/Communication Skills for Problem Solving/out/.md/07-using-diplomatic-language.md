# Using Diplomatic Language

When communicating with others about problems, we have to be careful that we do not offend other people involved with the problem. The purpose of problem-solving is finding the most effective solution to the problem. The purpose of effective problem-solving is not to blame others for causing the problem. Often the people involved in causing a problem must also be involved in solving it. We need to make sure that are careful about what we say, and how we say it.

## 6.1 Technique 1 – Downplaying Issues

We often add phrases to our language to minimize an issue, or reduce its significance. So, instead of saying, ‘There’s a problem with your report’, we would more likely say ‘There’s a <u>small</u> problem with your report’.

## 6.2 Technique 2 – Becoming Imprecise

Similarly, we often add phrases to our language to introduce uncertainty and a level of imprecision. This softens or ‘takes the edge off’ the impact of communication of the issue. For example, instead of saying ‘There is a problem with your report’, we would more likely say ‘There <u>seems</u> to be a problem with your report’.
