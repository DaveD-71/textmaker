#  8. Notes, Minutes, & Reports

- **Taking Notes**

> During a meeting the note taker records the topics of discussion and the decisions made. These notes are very important because the note taker will use them to make a record of the meeting in “minutes” or in a brief report.

- **About Minutes**

1.  **What are minutes?**

- Minutes are a brief record of a meeting’s key points.

2.  **What is the purpose of minutes?**

- The purpose of minutes is to provide an accurate summary of the issues, proposals, discussion and decisions of a meeting.

- After minutes have been approved, they become a legal document

3.  **Who writes the minutes?**

- Minutes are written by an authorized person, then checked and approved by the chairperson and the other people at the meeting.

<!-- -->

- **Minutes and Reports Include -**

1.  Date of minutes or report.

2.  Meeting date, starting time and ending time.

3.  Place of the meeting.

4.  Type of meeting: weekly, monthly, annual, regular, special.

5.  Officers of the meeting: chairperson, note taker.

6.  Participants and their status; late arrivals & early departures.

7.  Agenda. Attach a copy.

8.  Proposals and Reasoning.

9.  Agreements & Decisions: Be specific. Record – Who will do what by when?

10. Items that were tabled. They were on the agenda but not dealt with.

11. Reports can include the note taker’s personal observations and comments. Minutes do not.

- **  
  Three Steps for Writing Minutes and Reports**

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

#### Before the Meeting

Get a list of the people expected at the meeting.

Get a copy of the agenda.

Know the purpose of the meeting.

#### During the Meeting

Check off attendance as people arrive or pass around a sheet for the participants to write their names.

Note the time the meeting begins.

Write down suggestions, proposals, recommendations and motions (and who made them), and the decisions (Who is doing what by when).

Make note of any proposals for later discussion.

Don't try to write down every comment - just the main ideas.

Note the ending time of the meeting.

#### After the Meeting 

Write the minutes or report as soon as possible while your memory is still fresh.

Write in plain simple English in short sentences.

Follow the format of previous minutes or reports.

Report the discussion and decisions accurately.

In a report, if you express an opinion explain it simply and clearly.

Attach a copy of the agenda.

For big meetings attach a list of those who attended.

Proofread the minutes before you submit them.

The minutes must be checked, corrected if necessary, and approved at the next meeting. When the minutes are signed by the chairperson and note taker they become an official document.

#### Special Terms for Meetings

Adjourn end a meeting

Amend change or correct

Bring to order get people’s attention and start the meeting

Convene to gather people for a meeting or start the meeting

Table postpone a discussion to a later meeting

#### Write well: Notes About Fonts, Punctuation, & Formatting

##### Choice of Font

Choose a formal serif font such as <u>Times New Roman</u>.

Avoid using <u>Century</u> (the default font in MSWord). Some of Century’s punctuation is out of place.

Do not use plain fonts (san serif) such as <u>Arial</u> – they are too informal for minutes and reports.

##### Punctuation 

Be careful to use the correct spacing for punctuation:

There is only <u>one</u> space after punctuation.

There is <u>no space before</u> any punctuation.

##### Common punctuation:

**. period -** for ending sentences.

**, comma** - for separating words.

**; semi-colon** - for separating groups of words.

**: colon** - for replacing words and ending words and sentences before a list (as above).

**( )** **parenthesis** (or brackets) - for extra information, not main points.

##### Formatting 

Good formatting makes documents easier to read.

Follow the format in the sample minutes and report.

Separate paragraphs with one line. There is no need to indent at the beginning of a paragraph.

Make sure the spacing and columns are consistent.

Make sure the indentations are aligned.

The space between the number and text should equal 2 spaces, not more.

Use the Tab key to indent. Do not use the space bar.

Add the writer’s name and a line above the name for the signature.

Include page numbers at the bottom.

<td><p>Re: Report on the Bidding Team meeting, August 15, 2016</p>
<p>I attended a meeting of the inter-departmental bidding team on August 16 in PP Dept. Room C.</p>
<p>The chairperson, William Carr (PP Dept.), convened the meeting at 14:30. The regular team members, Steve Bates (Mkt. Dept.; Topic leader), Everett Chou (PP Dept.), Beverly Linden (Engineering Dept.), and Michelle Tanaka (Logistics Dept.), attended. Ms Tanaka arrived at 14:40 coming from a meeting at Dresnel. Tom Mills (Finance Dept.) was not able to attend as he was sick.</p>
<p>The purpose of the meeting was to consider the feasibility of bidding on a large construction project for a new client, Cates &amp; Doyle Associates.</p>
<p>The meeting opened with Steve Bates, the topic leader, presenting C&amp;D’s specifications and schedule for the project. Mr. Bates distributed copies of the project specs to the team members. His presentation was followed by a review of McConnell’s schedule and the feasibility of bidding on the project.</p>
<p>The main concern of the members was the large size of the project and the tight schedule. The deadline for bids is October 31 and the project must be completed within 42 months.</p>
<p>Through the discussion all items on the agenda were covered and several decisions were made:</p>
<p>1. The C&amp;D project is feasible;</p>
<p>2. Steve Bates and two engineers will make a site inspection from August 24 to 26;</p>
<p>3. Mr. Carr will select the engineers by August 18;</p>
<p>4. PP Dept. budget will finance the site inspection.</p>
<p>Discussion about the Dresnel project was tabled for a separate meeting. Our next meeting will be after the site inspection on Thursday, September 1, at 10:00. The meeting adjourned at 15:25.</p>
<p>Comments: This is a very large undertaking for McConnell and it is potentially very profitable in terms of income as well as enhancing our reputation. However, the size of the project and the tight schedule are risks that we will have to manage very carefully so that this project does not exceed our capabilities and compromise our other ongoing projects. In order to successfully undertake this project McConnell will probably have to increase its resources in terms of capital and human resources.</p>
<p>_____________________________________</p>
<p>Everett Chou / August 16, 2016</p></td>
