**About *In Company***

**The Target:** Understand and practice how to create successful meetings.

**The Training: 5 Points**

**Theory** Understand the structure and process of successful meetings.

**Language** Learn and practice the language of meetings.

**Models** Study and practice the theory and language of meetings with model meeting transcripts.

**Simulations** Practice the theory, structure, and language in business meeting simulations.

**Feedback** Listen to the feedback about your performance in the simulations.

**About Simulations**

The purpose of the simulations is to practice the structure, tasks and language of meetings.

Time window: each simulation has a time window that includes the meeting simulation and follow up feedback.

Everyone in the class will have an opportunity to practice all the skills for successful meetings: chairing, presenting, asking questions, and taking notes.

Each meeting simulation will have the same time window for the simulation and feedback.

Preparing for & conducting simulations

For the first simulations in the course the chairpeople write a complete chairing script suitable for the purpose of the meeting. The chairing script includes only what the chairperson says.

The script should be <u>written by hand in a notebook</u>. Writing by hand is better than typing on a computer. Writing notes in the text is not acceptable.

In the first round of simulations the chairpeople should read their chairing scripts.

After the first simulation the chairpeople write a chairing script and practice it, but they cannot use the script in the simulation. They must chair the meeting simulations from memory.

**1. The Business Meeting**

<img src="assets/media/image4.jpeg" style="width:2.43403in;height:1.61944in" alt="staff meeting" />\[\[SHAPE:staff meeting\]\]

**About Business Meetings**

A business meeting is a creative process in which information, questions, and ideas are exchanged, and understanding and agreements are created.

**Two Goals of Communication: Understanding & Agreement**

The first goal of all communication is to create understanding.

The ultimate goal of communication is to achieve agreement.

Agreement cannot exist without understanding.

**Social Protocol of Communication**

Although a business meeting focuses on business, it is a meeting of people.

People are social by nature so successful meetings always include the social exchanges that help create and maintain good relationships.

Observing the social protocol of communication is an important part of meetings.

**<u>The Social Protocol of Communication</u>**

The rules of social communication differ from culture to culture

but the goal is always the same: to create understanding and harmony.

**The Rules of Social Interaction consist of:**

What to do and say.

When to do it and say it.

**Examples of social protocol:**

Courteous language: “Please” and “Thank you”.

Exchanging greetings: Greetings are always returned.

Standing and shaking hands (or bowing) to exchange greetings.

Listening to others while they speak and responding appropriately.  
**The Reasons for Meetings**

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

There are two reasons to hold a meeting:

To share information that will benefit the people in the meeting.

Discuss specific issues or problem and make decisions.

**Tasks & Structure: Creating a successful meeting**

Meetings need to be efficient & effective: the staff’s time is valuable: it should be well spent and productive. Knowing the tasks and structure of meetings is the first step to making them productive.

**2. Chairing a Meeting**

<img src="assets/media/image5.png" style="width:1.60069in;height:1.27361in" />

**An Effective Chairperson**

The successful meeting depends on the chairperson to take the lead and coordinate the process.

Without a good chairperson the meeting will not be focused and it will not create good results. The participants will feel they have wasted their time.

**The Chairperson’s Tasks**

The effective chairperson is like an effective manager, keeping the meeting focused on the issues, guiding the discussion toward an appropriate conclusion, and keeping to the schedule.

When we describe the chairperson’s tasks we are actually describing the structure of the meeting.

The effective chairperson guides the meeting through the six stages in a way that is appropriate to the situation.

The chairperson must:

- Begin by getting everyone’s attention.

- Start the meeting with greetings.

- Confirm the purpose and goals of the meeting.

- Confirm the issues and review the agenda and allotted time.

- Introduce each item on the agenda.

- Coordinate the discussion of each item on the agenda.

- Encourage participation in questions and discussion.

- Manage time wisely.

- Call for breaks and restart the meeting.

- Bring the meeting to a close:

- Review the results of the meeting and the date of the next meeting.

- Thank the participants.

- Close the meeting.

**The Agenda**

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

The agenda is a list of items to be discussed during the meeting. It is one of the most important documents in a meeting.

**A meeting must have an agenda**

The agenda is like a road map and helps to keep the meeting focused on the goals and on schedule.

The items on the agenda are organized to make efficient use of time.

The chairperson reviews the agenda before beginning discussion so all the participants can focus on the issues.

During the meeting each item on the agenda is introduced by the chairperson, discussed by the participants and decided upon before moving on to the next item.

**Setting the agenda**

The agenda is usually set before the meeting. In the meeting, the agenda must be reviewed before the discussion begins in order to focus the discussion.

In other cases the agenda can be set in the meeting but before the discussion begins.

To set the agenda the chairperson must know the purpose of the meeting and get input from others.

In a regular staff meeting the agenda is usually the same every time.

**Who sets the agenda?**

The type of meeting determines how the agenda is set.

For an information meeting or training session, the agenda is set by the chairperson or trainer.

In a team meeting the participants together decide the agenda.

In a meeting with a client (client relations) the agenda is not so rigid – it is set and followed according the client’s needs.

In negotiation, the counterparties must equally contribute to and agree upon the agenda.

The agenda is critical in negotiation. The party that sets the agenda controls the focus and process of the negotiation.

**Changes and additions to the agenda**

Changes and additions to the agenda can be made at the beginning of the meeting:

Changes are not usually made during the discussion.

New items that come up during the discussion are often are held (or “tabled”) for the next meeting.

**<u>Vocabulary Note</u>**

**“Agenda”**

The agenda is a list with several items for discussion.

Never say “agenda” when you mean “item on the agenda”.

For example:

*- We have <u>three items on our agenda</u>.*

*- The <u>next item on our agenda</u> is a report from Mr. Honma.*

**Q&A and Discussion**

The chairperson’s second important task is to manage the discussion and questions from the participants.

Discussion and Q&A are essential parts of a meeting.

The chairperson creates the discussion by asking for questions from the participants and encouraging participation.

After the participants have asked questions the chairperson can also ask questions.

However, the chairperson should always be prepared to ask questions in order to encourage others to do so.

The chairperson must guide the discussion toward a clear conclusion.

**Making Decisions**

A decision is composed of the answers to 3 essential Wh-questions:

**<u>Who</u> is doing <u>What</u>, <u>by When</u>?**

If one of the answers is missing there is no decision.

When decisions are made they must be written down.

In a staff meeting, the note taker records the decisions and prepares the meeting minutes or report.

With a counterparty, decisions are recorded in an M.O.U. – a memo of understanding - and provide the basis for the contract.
