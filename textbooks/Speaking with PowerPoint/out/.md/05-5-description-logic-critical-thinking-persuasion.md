- **Effective presentations**

> Effective presentations include description, logic, critical thinking and persuasion. The model speech in Unit 9 is an example of all four of these elements.

- **Four elements for effective presentations**

> The four elements of effective presentations (description, logic, critical thinking, and persuasion) use the *Wh* *Questions*. The *Wh Questions* are not just points of grammar. *Wh Questions* are the tools we use to understand and organize information.

1.  **Description**

> Description uses the questions *who, where, what, when*, and *how* to create a picture with words. When a description is successful, the audience should be able to see an image in their minds.

- Effective description requires understanding the audience and speaking to that audience.

- The speaker must give all the necessary details to the audience. The speaker cannot expect the audience to guess the meaning or fill in the gaps in information. Expressions such as “and so on” and “etc.” are meaningless.

- Effective description is well organized; it requires logic.

2.  **Logic**

> Logic is the systematic organization of facts and analysis in order to present a clear conclusion and message.

- The three-part structure of a speech is a logical structure:

  1.  Introduction: Tell your audience what you are going to say.

  2.  Body: Tell your audience what you want to say.

  3.  Conclusion: Tell your audience what you want them to understand and do.

- An example of logic from the model speech in Unit 9, Paragraph 9. The model speech is about a new car called the Ventura.

<!-- -->

- Statement: the Ventura is environmentally responsible.

- Evidence/facts: With its efficient hybrid engine and ultra-low emissions,

- Analysis: it far exceeds the government’s targets.

- Evidence/fact: In addition, 95% of the car is made of recyclable material.

- Conclusion: So, totally, the Ventura is an environmentally responsible car.

3.  **Critical thinking**

> Basically, critical thinking is a logical process of asking and answering the question, *Why*? We can identify six steps in the process of critical thinking:

1.  Gather facts and understand a situation.

2.  Separate the main ideas from the details.

3.  Separate fact from opinion.

4.  Bring the facts together in a description of the situation.

5.  Analyze and evaluate the facts. Look for connections between them.

6.  Draw conclusions from the facts, analysis, and evaluation.

<!-- -->

4.  **Persuasion**

> Persuasion is the ability to get an audience to understand and accept an idea. Persuasion requires description, logic, and critical thinking.

- Three Elements of Persuasion:

1.  The speaker must seem believable and logical about the subject.

2.  The speaker must help the audience make an emotional connection to the subject.

3.  The speaker must give the audience enough information about the subject so the audience can understand it and accept the conclusion.

- Five Stages of Persuasion

  1.  Get the audience’s attention so they want to listen.

  2.  Describe the situation so the audience feels action is required.

  3.  Make a proposal that will satisfy the need for effective action.

  4.  Create an image of the results of the proposal so the audience will feel they will get some benefit from the action.

  5.  Make a specific request to the audience to act so the proposal is successful.
