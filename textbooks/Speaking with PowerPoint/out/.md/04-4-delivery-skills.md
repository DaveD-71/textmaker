# 4 Delivery Skills

- **Good delivery skills are essential for a good presentation.**

Good delivery skills build your confidence and win the attention of your audience.

- Stand up and practice the skills.

- Your teacher will model each of these skills for you one-by-one.

- Practice the skills until they are easy to do.

**Voice**

**Clothing**

**Gestures**

**Posture**

**Speak to your audience. Deliver your message.**

1.  **Voice**

Your voice is your most important delivery skill.

- A clear strong voice delivers your message to your audience. If they cannot hear you they will not receive your message.

- Speak smoothly and without hesitation.

- Don't rush or speak too quickly.

- Emphasize important words and phrases.

- Eliminate the unnecessary pauses and Ummmms.

2.  **Eye contact**

Eye contact connects the speaker and the audience.

- Look at your audience, not at your notes.

- Look at the whole audience. Move your head so you can make eye contact with everyone.

- Memorize parts of your speech so you can look at your audience. Memorize your greetings, introduction, transition to each main point, and your closing thoughts.

3.  **Posture**

Good posture makes you appear more confident.

- Position yourself so you can see your audience and your audience can see you.

- Stand up straight with your feet slightly apart and your weight evenly on both feet.

- Don't lean on the table or lectern.

4.  **Gestures**

Gestures are an important part of your delivery. During your presentation your hands should be holding your notes, gesturing or pointing at your PowerPoint.

- Gestures add emphasis and rhythm to your presentation.

- Good gestures are small, firm, and clear. Big gestures are not appropriate.

- When you gesture, look at your audience: If you are not looking at your audience, do not gesture.

- When you count (1st, 2nd, 3rd), you can gesture with one finger, two fingers, three fingers.

- But, if you say, "My <u>next</u> point is…" or "My <u>last</u> point is…" - don’t count with your fingers.

- Don't scratch your face or play with your hair.

- Don’t cover your face with your hand if you adjust your glasses.

5.  **Clothing – appropriate and neat**

Be sure your clothing is neat and appropriate for the situation.

- Before your meeting check your appearance - your tie, your jacket collar, and your hair. Make sure everything is neat.

- Inappropriate or messy clothing will distract your audience’s attention away from your message.

6.  **Speaking notes**

If you use a speaking script or notes make sure they are clear and easy to read.

- Hold your notes in your hand: Don't put them on a table or lectern – You will look down at them and not look at your audience!

- Hold your notes in one place just above your waist where they are easy to see.

- Don’t move your notes: it’s very distracting!

- Hold your notes in the middle of the page so they won’t fold over.

7.  **Standing still or moving**

- In very formal presentations and with small groups it is best to stand in one place.

- With a more informal presentation with a large audience it is good to move so you can speak directly to different sections of the audience and create better communication.
