- **Flag Expressions**

<!-- -->

- *Flag Expressions* are special expressions that introduce each step in the structure of your speech.

- Flag Expressions are like a tour guide’s flag: Everyone can follow the guide’s flag and not get lost. If there is no flag the guide is too hard to follow.

- Memorize and use the Flag Expressions. They will help you organize your information and ideas, and express yourself clearly.

- **Notes About Flag Expressions**

1.  **Counting Vs Transitions**

> In the introduction and conclusion you can count your main points with *First*, *second*, *third*; or *First*, *then*, *next*, *finally*.
>
> However, in the body these words are not correct for introducing the main points.
>
> In the body, introduce each point with transition expressions such as -

- *Let me begin with my first point.*

- *Now/Next, I’ll move on to my second point.*

- *Finally/Now, I will tell you my third point.*

2.  **Use *at first* correctly**

> Do not use *at first* to count or to introduce your first point.

- *At first* is a different grammatical structure from counting: first, second, third.

- *At first* introduces a condition that is going to change. For example:

> *When I went to Canada, <u>at first</u> it was very difficult for me to speak English, <u>however</u>, after awhile it became much easier for me.*

3.  **Use *Let’s* correctly**

> *Let’s* is suitable only for <u>informal</u> proposals such as -

- *<u>Let’s</u> get started.*

- *<u>Let’s take</u> a short break.*

> *Let’s* <u>should not</u> be used for <u>formal</u> proposals and conclusions. \[\[LINEBREAK\]\]For example-

- *<u>Let’s</u> improve our products and advertising.* <u>Not appropriate.</u>

- *<u>I think we need</u> to improve our products and advertising.* Appropriate.
