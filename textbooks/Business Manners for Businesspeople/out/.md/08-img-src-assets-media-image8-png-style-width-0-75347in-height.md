# <img src="assets/media/image8.png" style="width:0.75347in;height:0.50764in" />8 Making an Appointment

> *Confirming the purpose of a meeting / Setting the time & place*

- Business Situation: On the phone

1.  Calling a client

> Takashi Ikeda calls Amanda Jones at Atlas Construction for the first time to arrange a meeting.
>
> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Jones Good morning. Atlas Construction. Amanda Jones speaking. May I help you?
>
> Ikeda Good morning, Ms Jones. My name is Takashi Ikeda. I’m from Nippon Taiyo’s sales department.
>
> Jones Yes, Mr. Ikeda. What can I do for you?
>
> Ikeda I’m Nippon Taiyo’s new sales representative for Atlas Construction. I would like to meet you to introduce myself. Could we meet sometime this week?
>
> Jones Let me look at my schedule. One moment please… I can meet you this Thursday at two o’clock. Is that convenient for you?
>
> Ikeda Yes, this Thursday at two would be fine. Thank you very much, Ms Jones.
>
> Jones Thank you Mr. Ikeda. I’ll see you then.
>
> Ikeda Thank you again, Ms Jones. Good-bye.
>
> Jones Good-bye.

2.  Calling a vendor

> Miwa Tani in Nippon Taiyo’s purchasing department calls a vendor to arrange a meeting.
>
> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Wong Good morning. Wade Materials. Anita Wong speaking. May I help you?
>
> Tani Good morning Ms Wong. This is Miwa Tani at Nippon Taiyo.
>
> Wong Yes, good morning Ms Tani. What can I do for you?
>
> Tani I’d like to arrange a meeting to discuss our current order for plastic sheeting. I’d like to meet tomorrow if possible.
>
> Wong Tomorrow? I’m very sorry but I’ll be away on business tomorrow. Could we make it Thursday?
>
> Tani I’m sorry no. Thursday’s not good for me. How about Friday afternoon sometime between 2 and 4?
>
> Wong Would three o’clock be alright?
>
> Tani Yes, three this Friday is alright.
>
> Wong Thank you Ms Tani. I’ll be at your office at three this Friday.
>
> Tani Thank you Ms Wong. See you then. Good-bye.
>
> Wong Thank you for calling Ms Tani. Good-bye.

- **Available Vs. Convenient**

> Available and convenient refer to the same thing: It is possible to do something at a particular time or place.
>
> However, the words are used in different ways. For example:

- **Saying good-bye**

> In the situation in dialog 1, notice that Mr. Ikeda says good-bye first.
>
> In telephone etiquette, it is the person who makes the phone call who should close the conversation and say good-bye first.
