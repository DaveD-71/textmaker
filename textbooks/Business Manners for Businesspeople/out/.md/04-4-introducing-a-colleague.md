# 4 Introducing a Colleague

> *Procedure & language*

- Business Situation: At a client

> Takashi Ikeda takes his colleague, Kenji Ogawa, to a meeting with his client, Amanda Jones. Mr. Ikeda introduces Mr. Ogawa to Ms Jones.

- Standard Procedure for Introducing a Colleague

> Ikeda Good afternoon, Ms Jones. It’s nice to see you again.
>
> Jones Good afternoon, Mr. Ikeda. It’s nice to see you again, too.
>
> Ikeda Ms Jones, I’d like to introduce my colleague, Kenji Ogawa. He works with me in the international sales department. Mr. Ogawa, this is Amanda Jones.
>
> Jones It’s a pleasure to meet you, Mr. Ogawa (Holds out her hand to shake).
>
> Ogawa (Shaking hands) It’s a pleasure to meet you, Ms Jones. Here is my card.
>
> Jones Thank you. And here’s mine.
>
> Ogawa Thank you.
>
> Jones Shall we go to the meeting room?

- Business Protocol

1.  Introducing others

> The situation above illustrates the standard western protocol for introductions. This is the pattern to follow and the language to use.
>
> In the situation above, since Mr. Ikeda knows both Mr. Ogawa and Ms Jones, he <u>must</u> do the introductions. It would be extremely rude if they had to introduce themselves.

2.  Showing respect in introductions

> During introductions, look at and speak to the person to whom you want to show respect. For example: To introduce your colleague to a client, show respect to the client by looking at and speaking to him or her.

- The junior/senior rule for introductions

> In business, the rule for making introductions is to introduce the junior person to the senior person:

- Speak to the senior person and introduce the junior person.

- Clients are senior to your colleagues; managers are senior to staff.

- In the situation above, Ms Jones is senior because she is the client.

3.  Courtesy notes

<!-- -->

1.  Notice that when Mr. Ogawa greets Ms Jones he does not repeat his own name. Repeating his name would be rude to Mr. Ikeda because he would be ignoring Mr. Ikeda’s introduction. It would also be embarassing for Ms Jones.

2.  Notice also that Mr. Ogawa says Ms Jones’ name. This is a courtesy to Ms Jones and it helps Mr. Ogawa to remember her name.

- Expressions for Introductions

1.  **Common expressions**

- I’d like to introduce my colleague, Kenji Ogawa.

- Let me introduce my colleague. This is Kenji Ogawa.

- Let me introduce Kenji Ogawa. He works with me in the sales department.

2.  **Introducing a colleague to someone senior (client or manager)**

- Ms Jones, <u>allow me to introduce</u> my colleague, Kenji Ogawa.

- Ms Jones, <u>may I introduce</u> my colleague, Kenji Ogawa?

3.  **Introducing a client to your manager or someone senior**

- Mr. Smith, <u>allow me to introduce you to</u> my manager, Mr. Yoshi Fuijimura. Mr. Fuijimura is the general manager of the sales department.

- Mr. Smith, <u>let me introduce you to</u> my manager, Mr. Yoshi Fuijimura. Mr. Fuijimura is the general manager of the sales department.

**Business Card Note:**

> When juniors meet seniors, juniors offer business cards but seniors might not always offer a business card:

- if the introduction was just a courtesy, the senior person would probably not offer his card.

- However, if the introduction was going to lead to ongoing business then the senior person would probably offer his card.

<!-- -->

- Expressions to Avoid

1.  ***His name is*…**

*This is my colleague. His name is Kenji Ogawa* is correct English, however, it is not suitable for introductions.

It is more polite to say, *I’d like to introduce my colleague, Kenji Ogawa.*

2.  ***I’d like you to meet…***

*I’d like you to meet…* is not suitable for business because it confuses the positions of seniority. It can be used in non-business social occasions amongst friends.

*Bob, I’d like you to meet my friend Hiroshi.*
