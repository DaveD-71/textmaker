# 6 Basic Telephone Skills

Answering the phone & transferring calls

> <u>Situation: In the office, answering the phone</u>
>
> Tomoya Ishii is working at his desk. The phone rings and he answers it.

1.  Answering the phone and transferring the call

> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ishii Good afternoon, Industrial Safety Research Agency. May I help you?
>
> Caller Yes. My name is Andreas Garcia from PV Plastics. May I speak to Mr. Sakurai, please?
>
> Ishii I’m sorry. Could you repeat your name please?
>
> Caller Yes. It’s Andreas Garcia.
>
> Ishii Thank you Mr. Garcia. One moment please. I’ll transfer your call
>
> Caller Thank you.

2.  Asking for identity and transferring a call

> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ishii Good morning, Industrial Safety Research Agency. May I help you?
>
> Caller Yes. May I speak to Mr. Otsuka, please?
>
> Ishii May I ask who’s calling, please?
>
> Caller Yes. My name is Rick Baxter.
>
> Ishii And may I have the name of your company, please, Mr. Baxter?
>
> Caller Yes, I’m with Fairway Textiles.
>
> Ishii Thank you Mr. Baxter. I’ll transfer your call. One moment please.
>
> Caller Thank you.

> <u>Introductions and Identification</u>
>
> Identifying and introducing yourself on the telephone

1.  **Identifying yourself: *This is…***

> For identifying yourself on the telephone to people who already know your name.
>
> It is not used to introduce yourself in face to face situations: it is used to introduce others: *This is my colleague, Ayako Mori.*

2.  **Introducing yourself: *My name is …***

> Introduce yourself for the first time on the telephone by saying, *My name is…* (This is also used face to face introductions.)

3.  **Note:** *I’m ..* is not used for introductions or identification on the telephone.

4.  **In-company identification**

> When making a phone call, be prepared to give your name and your company name.
>
> Even within the company, when calling other branches, always give your name and department or location.

- Hello. <u>This is Tanaka</u> in marketing. (Standard)

- Hi. <u>It’s Hiroshi</u> in the Tokyo office. (Very informal for colleagues who know you well.)

5.  **Caller ID**

> When answering the phone, if the caller does not identify themselves, always ask for their name and company.

- May I ask who is calling, please?

- May I have your name please?

> <u>Expressions for the Telephone</u>

1.  **Greetings**

- May I help you?

- How may I help you?

2.  **Closing**

- Thank you for calling. Good-bye.

3.  **Gathering information: *May I … / Could I …***

- May I ask who’s calling, please?

- May I have your name, please?

- Could I have your number, please?

- May I have the name of your company, please?

**Note**: *What is your name?* and *What is your number?* are correct English but too direct and not polite enough for business. Don’t say them.
