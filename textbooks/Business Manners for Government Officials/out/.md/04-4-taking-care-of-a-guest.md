# 4 Taking Care of a Guest

Simple Courtesies

> <u>Situation: In the office</u>
>
> At the ISRA office in Tokyo, Kayoko Yamamoto, director of the consumer products department, often has guests from other countries. She usually asks Ms Mori to meet the guest and take him/her to the meeting room. Ms Mori greets guests in the reception area.

1.  Meeting a guest

> Mori Excuse me, Mr. Bartlett?
>
> Guest Yes, that’s right.
>
> Mori Good afternoon. My name is Ayako Mori. Ms Yamamoto asked me to meet you.
>
> Guest Thank you.
>
> Mori I’ll take you to the meeting room. It’s this way.

2.  At the meeting room

> Mori This is the room. Please have a seat here. (Gesturing)
>
> Guest Thank you.
>
> Mori Ms Yamamoto will be with you in a moment. (Mr. Mori leaves the room.)

3.  Apologizing for a delayed meeting

> Mori Excuse me, Ms Chaundry.
>
> Guest Yes?
>
> Mori I’m very sorry but Ms Yamamoto is delayed. She is still in a meeting. She can be with you in about 10 minutes.
>
> Guest That’s quite all right. I understand.
>
> Mori Thank you very much for your patience.

4.  Room Change

> Mori Excuse me, Mr. Mason.
>
> Guest Yes?
>
> Mori Thank you for waiting. I’m very sorry but the meeting room has been moved to the 6th floor. Please follow me, I’ll take you there.
>
> Guest Thank you.
>
> <u>Business Protocol</u>

- Business cards

> When taking care of a guest it is not usually necessary to exchange business cards.

- Small talk

> The guest has an appointment to meet Ms Yamamoto, not Ms Mori. There is no need to engage in small talk in this situation.
>
> <u>Expressions for Introductions</u>

- I‘ll take you to the meetng room.

- Please follow me.

- Please have a seat.

- The washroom is at the end of the hall on the left.

- I’m very sorry but …

- I’m very sorry to have kept you waiting.

- Thank you for waiting.
