# 3 Meeting a Counterparty

Basic protocol for introductions, greetings, & courtesies

> <u>Situation: Meeting a counterparty</u>
>
> Tomoya Ishii is on a research trip in Europe. He has an appointment to meet Alicia Phan, at the European Safety Council (ESC) in Brussels. Mr. Ishii and Ms Phan have exchanged e-mail but this is the first time for them to meet face-to-face.
>
> Mr. Ishii arrives at ESC’s office and goes to the reception desk.

1.  At the reception desk

> Mr. Ishii identifies himself at the reception desk with his name, his agency’s name and appointment information.
>
> Recep’t. Good morning. How may I help you?
>
> Ishii Good morning. My name is Tomoya Ishii. I’m with the Japan Industrial Safety Research Agency. I have an appointment with Alicia Phan at 10:30.
>
> Recep’t. One moment please. I’ll call Ms Phan (Calls Ms Phan). Mr. Ishii?
>
> Ishii Yes?
>
> Recep’t. Ms Phan will come down to meet you.
>
> Ishii Thank you.
>
> Recep’t. Here is a visitor’s badge. Please wear it in the building and return it when you leave.
>
> Ishii Thank you. I’ll do that.
>
> Recep’t. Ms Phan will meet you over there in the waiting area (Gesturing).
>
> Ishii Thank you very much.

2.  Meeting for the <u>first</u> time

> Ms Phan is expecting to meet Mr. Ishii; however, they have not met before so she begins by confirming his identity.
>
> Phan Good morning, Mr. Ishii?
>
> Ishii Yes, that’s right. I’m Tomoya Ishii from ISRA.
>
> Phan (Holds out her hand to shake) I’m Alicia Phan. It’s a pleasure to meet you, Mr. Ishii.
>
> Ishii Good morning, Ms Phan. It’s a pleasure to meet you, too. Here’s my card.
>
> Phan Thank you. And here’s mine.
>
> Ishii Thank you very much.
>
> Phan Please come this way. The meeting room is over here.
>
> Ishii Thank you.

3.  Meeting <u>again</u>

> The next time Mr. Ishii meets Ms Phan they will use different greetings.
>
> Phan Good morning, Mr. Ishii (Holds out her hand to shake).
>
> Ishii Good morning, Ms Phan. It’s nice to see you again.
>
> Phan It’s nice to see you again, too. How have you been?
>
> Ishii I’ve been very well, thank you. And you?
>
> Phan Very well, too, thank you. The meeting room is this way.
>
> <u>Notes</u>

1.  **At the reception desk**

> You can use the same expressions when you use a reception phone.

2.  **First meetings**

> Ms Phan and Mr. Ishii introduce themselves with *“I’m…”* not *“My name is…”*
>
> *“I’m …”* is used to identify yourself when the other person is expecting to meet you, as in the case of Ms Phan and Mr. Ishii. They had an appointment so they were expecting to see each other.

3.  **Meeting again**

> Ms Phan and Mr. Ishii greet each other with “It’s nice to <u>see</u> you again.”
>
> “It’s nice to <u>meet</u> you again.” is not a correct greeting. Don’t say this.

4.  ***How are you?***

> The expression, *How are you?,* is a standard part of courteous greetings. It is not a question about health. <u>Never</u> respond, *I’m a little sick.* Or *I’m tired.*
>
> Correct responses are:

- *Fine thank you, and you?*

- *Very well, thank you. And you?*

- *Fine thanks, and you?* (Informal.)

<!-- -->

- *How have you been?* means, *since the last time we met*. The appropriate response is *I<u>’ve</u> been very well, thank you. And you?*

5.  **Business Card Etiquette**

> Exchanging business cards is common business practice throughout the world, but the protocol differs from culture to culture:

- Present your card with the local language side face up.

- In Asia, business cards are a key part of the introduction: they are presented and received with two hands and are treated with great respect.

- In the west, business cards are treated more casually. The key part of the introduction is the person to person contact, not the business card: the card is simply an additional useful element. Cards can be exchanged at the beginning or the end of a meeting. They are usually presented and received with one hand.

Maintain the Japanese style of presenting and receiving business cards. It’s a sign of respect for the other person that is easily understood across cultures.
