# 1 Meeting for the First Time

> *Greetings & Introductions*

- Business Situation: At a trade fair

> Takashi Ikeda works for a Japanese company that handles construction materials. He is attending an international trade fair for the construction industry. Mr. Ikeda approaches someone he would like to meet.

1.  Introducing yourself

> Ikeda Hello. My name is Takashi Ikeda (Holds out his hand to shake).
>
> Smith (Shakes hands) It’s nice to meet you, Mr. Ikeda. My name is John Smith.
>
> Ikeda It’s nice to meet you, too, Mr. Smith. I work for Nippon Taiyo Trading. This is my card (Offers his business card). Who are you with?
>
> Smith Thank you. I’m with Aims International. This is my card (Offers his business card).
>
> Ikeda Thank you.

2.  Exchanging information

> Ikeda What kind of company is Aims International?
>
> Smith Aims is a shipping company. We operate fleets of trucks, ships, and cargo planes.
>
> Ikeda And what do you do at Aims?
>
> Smith I work in the logistics department. And what does Nippon Taiyo do?
>
> Ikeda Nippon Taiyo is a trading company. We handle all types of construction materials.
>
> Smith And what do you do at Nippon Taiyo?
>
> Ikeda I’m in the international sales department. I handle wall paneling.
>
> Smith I see. That’s very interesting.
>
> … the conversation continues…

3.  Saying Good-bye

> Ikeda Well, it was very nice to meet you, Mr. Smith, but I’m sorry, I must be going now. I have to meet a colleague in a few minutes.
>
> Smith I understand. It was very nice meeting you too, Mr. Ikeda (Holds out his hand to shake).
>
> Ikeda Thank you Mr. Smith. Good-bye.
>
> Smith Good-bye, Mr. Ikeda.

- Language Check Points

1.  **My name is … Vs. I’m …**

> *My name is …* is the correct formal expression for business introductions.
>
> *I’m …* is appropriate for informal social introductions and when the people are expecting to meet each other. (Please see Unit 2)

2.  **Nice to meet you**

> *Nice to meet you* is a common expression for introductions. However, it is not correct to begin introductions with, *Hello. Nice to meet you. My name is Hiroshi Tanaka.*
>
> *Nice to meet you* is said <u>only</u> <u>after</u> hearing the other person’s name – not before.

3.  **Belong**

> *Belong* is not used for companies or departments. It is used for teams, clubs, political parties and religions. For example:

- The Hand Shake

<!-- -->

- **There are four simple steps to a good handshake:**

> 1\. Make eye contact and extend your open hand.
>
> 2\. Let your hands meet at the base of the thumbs.
>
> 3\. Close your hand firmly and shake three times, – 1, 2, 3 – and smile!
>
> 4\. Open your hand and bring it back to your side.

- **The two handed shake? - Don’t do it!**

> The two-handed shake is for politicians: it is not generally used by business people. Avoid the two handed shake!
>
> Shake hands with your right hand only. Leave your left hand by your side. Don’t add your left hand to the shake by putting it on the other person’s hand or arm.

- **Culture Note: Different styles of handshake**

> **Firmness**: Your handshake should be firm but not too strong. Avoid giving a weak handshake: it’s considered unprofessional.
>
> North Americans tend to have firm handshakes. Europeans and Asians prefer a gentler grip.
>
> **Women**: In North America, both men and women generally offer to shake hands but in Britain, Europe, and Asia, men usually wait for the woman to offer her hand.
