# 5 Basic Telephone Skills

> *Answering the phone & transferring calls*

- Business Situation: In the office, answering phone

> Takashi Ikeda is working at his desk. The phone rings and he answers it.

1.  Answering the phone

> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ikeda Good morning, Nippon Taiyo international sales. May I help you?
>
> Caller Yes. May I speak to Mr. Ikeda, please?
>
> Ikeda Yes, speaking. How may I help you?
>
> Caller Hello, Mr. Ikeda. This is Amanda Jones from Atlas Construction.
>
> Ikeda Oh hello, Ms Jones. What can I do for you?
>
> Caller I’m calling about …

2.  Answering the phone and transferring the call

> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ikeda Good afternoon, Nippon Taiyo international sales. May I help you?
>
> Caller Yes. My name is Diego Menzes from PV Plastics. May I speak to Mr. Sugimoto, please?
>
> Ikeda I’m sorry. Could you repeat your name please?
>
> Caller Yes. It’s Diego Menzes.
>
> Ikeda Thank you Mr. Menzes. One moment please. I’ll transfer your call.
>
> Caller Thank you.

3.  Asking for identity and transferring a call

> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ikeda Good morning, Nippon Taiyo international sales. May I help you?
>
> Caller Yes. May I speak to Mr. Oe, please?
>
> Ikeda May I ask who’s calling, please?
>
> Caller Yes. My name is Ray Carter.
>
> Ikeda And may I have the name of your company, please, Mr. Carter?
>
> Caller Yes, I’m with Rockland Design.
>
> Ikeda Thank you Mr. Carter. I’ll transfer your call. One moment please.
>
> Caller Thank you.

4.  Reception Phone

> In some offices, guests have to contact their counterparties by a reception phone. This is the case at Nippon Taiyo.
>
> Phone Ring-ring.
>
> Ikeda Good afternoon, international sales. Ikeda speaking. How may I help you?
>
> Caller Good afternoon, Mr Ikeda. This is Ranolf Singh. I am here for our two o’clock meeting.
>
> Ikeda Thank you Mr. Singh. Please wait a moment. I’ll be right with you.
>
> Caller Thank you.

- Expressions for the Telephone

1.  **Greeting for receiving a call**

- May I help you?

- How may I help you?

2.  **Caller <u>introducing</u> him/herself on the phone**

> *Hello. My name is Brenda Smith* for introducing yourself for the first time on the telephone. (This is also used face to face introductions.)

3.  **Caller <u>identifying</u> him/herself on the phone**

> *Hello, this is Brenda Smith* for identifying yourself on the telephone to people who already know your name.
>
> *This is …* is used to introduce others face to face: *<u>This is</u> my colleague, Brenda Green.* It is not used to introduce yourself face to face.
>
> **Note:** *<u>I’m</u> Allan Li* is <u>not</u> used for introductions or identification on the telephone.

4.  **In-company identification**

> When calling within your company, always identify yourself with your name and department or location.

- Hello. <u>This is Tanaka</u> in marketing. (Formal. Standard)

- Hi. <u>It’s Hiroshi</u> in the Tokyo office. (Informal for colleagues who know you well.)

5.  **Asking for information from a caller**

> If the caller does not identify themselves, always ask for their name and company.

- May I ask who’s calling, please?

- May I have your name, please?

- Could I have your number, please?

- May I have the name of your company, please?

**Note**: *<u>What</u> is your name?* and *<u>What</u> is your number?* are not polite. Don’t use them.
