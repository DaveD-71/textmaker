> **What is Critical Thinking?**
>
> Critical thinking is the ability to think skillfully about an issue from a variety of aspects, observe similarities and differences, evaluate the pros and cons (costs and benefits), and draw a well-reasoned conclusion.

- **Analyzing and Arguing a Case**

> To argue a case, you need to analyze the issue carefully and present your ideas clearly.

- **The Wh Questions**

> One simple way to analyze and organize information is with the Wh questions.
>
> **Who** Who are the players?

- The people and organizations involved.

> **Where** Where do the events take place?
>
> **What** What happened? What are the results?

- What are the costs? What are the benefits?

> **When** When do the events take place?

- Think about the past, present, and future.

> **Why** Why did the events happen?

- If you ask Why? Why? Why? You can analyze a problem back to its source.

> **How** How many? How far? How long?

- Details of quantity, distance, and time.

  - **Background, Situation, Results**

> When you present the case, organize your information, analysis and results.
>
> On a timeline, we can think of background, current situation, and results as being similar to past, present, and future.
>
> **Background** The basic facts or information.
>
> **Situation** The particular events that take place in the background conditions; or the costs and benefits.
>
> **Results** The conclusion or proposal. What is the result of the situation?

- **Analyze & Propose**

  - **The Task:** Explain a problem then propose a solution.

> Identify an important problem you are familiar with and propose a solution. \[\[LINEBREAK\]\]For example, the problem could be in business, in your company, the environment, education, social welfare, or some other area.

- **The Process**

> **1. Brainstorm to identify a problem.** You should be familiar with the problem so you do not have to do any research.
>
> **2. Analyze the problem:** Think about it and brainstorm to create a proposal to overcome the problem.
>
> **3. Prepare your presentation.** It must include 3 elements:
>
> **Background** Describe and explain a situation so your audience will understand what it is.
>
> **Situation** Explain why the situation is a problem: What are the costs in time, effort, money, or well being? Your audience should feel these costs are unacceptable and that a solution needs to be found.
>
> **Results** Make a clear proposal to solve the problem. Show the benefits of the solution and how it will improve the situation.
>
> The presentation is followed by questions, answers, and discussion.

- **Argue / Defend**

  - **The Task:** Present arguments for and against, and then, take a position.

> Argue/Defend is a form of individual debate. It is similar to Analyze/Propose; however, the speaker must present both sides of the issue and then decide which side is more suitable.

- **The Process (Similar to Analyze & Propose)**

> Your presentation must include three elements:

**Background** The speaker considers a proposal for a change. The speaker explains the proposal and the background.

**Situation** The speaker considers and presents both sides: the reasons for accepting the proposal and for rejecting the proposal; the costs and the benefits.

**Result** Finally, the speaker must decide to accept or reject the proposal and explain why.

> The presentation is followed by questions, answers, and discussion.
