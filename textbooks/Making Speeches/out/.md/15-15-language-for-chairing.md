- **Language and Protocol Notes for Chairing**

  - **In a meeting, the chairperson greets the participants and introduces the speaker**

<!-- -->

- *Good afternoon.*

- *It's my pleasure to introduce our first (next) speaker. His name is Frank Johnson.*

- *Mr. Johnson works in the marketing department of ABC Electronics.*

- *Today, he’ll be talking about market trends in home electronics.*

- *Please give Mr. Johnson a warm welcome.*

  - **The speaker needs to acknowledge the introduction**

- *Thank you, Ms Watson (chairperson).*

- *Thank you for the introduction, Ms Watson.*

  - **After the presentation, the chairperson opens the Q&A session**

- *Now we have time for <u>some</u> / <u>a few</u> questions.*

- *Are there any questions for Mr Johnson?*

  - **The chair might need to call on the participants**

- *Yes, there is a question from Mr. Smith.*

- *Ms. Jones, do you have a question?*

- *Are there any more questions?*

  - **The chair needs to close the Q&A session**

- *We have time for one or two more questions.*

- *I'm sorry, that's all the time we have for questions.*

- *Thank you all.*

  - **The chair needs to close the session by thanking the speaker and participants**

- *I would like to thank Mr. Johnson for a very interesting talk this afternoon.*

- *On behalf of everyone here, I would like to thank Mr. Johnson for a very interesting presentation.*

- **Notes on the Protocol of Meetings**

  1.  **Greetings:** In a staff meeting with reports and discussion the participants might return the greeting however, in a meeting with speeches the audience <u>does not</u> return the greeting.

  2.  **Applause:** The chairperson is responsible for starting the applause.

  3.  **Introductions**: When the speaker is introduced by a chairperson the speaker does not begin his speech by repeating his own name.

  4.  **Eye Contact:** During the Q&A session the chairperson must keep eye contact with all the participants and the speaker in order to coordinate the questions, comments, and discussion.

  5.  **Raising Hands:** In a small meeting it is not necessary for participants to raise their hands high and wait for the chair’s approval before asking a question or making a comment.

  - A small hand gesture and eye contact are all that is necessary.

  - The participant should begin speaking at once.

  - In large meetings raising hands and getting permission are necessary to keep the meeting running smoothly.

- **Transition Expressions for Class Presentations**

  - **The chairperson or the speaker should ask the instructor for feedback.**

    - Mr. Smith, could we have your feedback please.

    - Thank you very much for your feedback. It is very useful.

  - **Asking the next person to chair**

    - And now I would like to ask Ms Ito to take over as the chair.
