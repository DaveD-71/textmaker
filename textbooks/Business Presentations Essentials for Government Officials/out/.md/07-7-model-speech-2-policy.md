# 7: Model Speech 2: Policy

> One of the purposes of giving a speech can be to explain policy and express an opinion and thereby influence how others think about an issue. Below is a speech explaining the Japanese government’s ‘Cool Biz’ initiative.

<td><p>Good afternoon everyone, and thank you for the opportunity to speak to you. I’m here today to talk to you about Cool Biz, an initiative of the Japanese government that has changed modern Japanese society. First I’ll explain why and how Cool Biz was introduced, and then I’ll move onto its impact on Japanese society. I’ll finish by briefly discussing new initiatives that are based on the success of Cool Biz. If you have any questions, I’ll be happy to answer them at the end of my presentation.</p>
<p>So let’s begin with my first point – why and how Cool Biz came into effect. Many people ascribe Cool Biz as being the brainchild of the then-popular Prime Minister, Junichiro Koizumi. However, it was actually Ms Yuriko Koike’s idea. In 2005, Ms Koike was the Minister of the Environment. In order to reduce electricity consumption, and thereby reduce carbon pollution, she proposed the Cool Biz initiative for government offices. The basic concept is that air conditioners should be set to 28 degrees Celsius. This then reduces the amount of energy that air conditioners would otherwise use to sustain cooler temperatures. To compensate for the increased office temperature, employees were encouraged to wear cooler clothes. The initiative quickly crossed from the public sector to the private sector. Initially, the Cool Biz period was from June to September, but in 2011 it was extended to May to October to help Japan deal with its energy needs resulting from the 2011 Tohoku earthquake.</p>
<p>Now, I’d like to talk about my second point – the effects of Cool Biz. In 2005 the Ministry of Energy conducted a survey that showed 95.8% of survey participants were aware of the Cool Biz campaign, and that almost a third of them had already changed their air conditioners to 28 degrees Celsius. The Ministry then projected that the Cool Biz initiative resulted in a reduction of carbon dioxide emissions of 1.14 million tons in 2006. In 2009, another survey indicated that 57% had increased their settings on their air conditioners. However, the impact was not just environmental – it also had a significant impact on the economy. An analysis by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry in 2005 showed an increase in the demand for clothing to the value of 100 billion yen.</p>
<p>Now I’d like to move onto my last point – new initiatives which are connected to Cool Biz. In 2011, after the Tohoku earthquake, the government introduced Super Cool Biz which encouraged the dimming or turning off of lights and the opening of windows and turning off air conditioners completely. In return, an even looser dress code of polo shirts, Hawaiian shirts and casual shoes was encouraged. Now, the Ministry of Environment’s website promotes ‘Cool Share’ whereby people are encouraged to reduce electricity further by being together in a cool air conditioned place, and turning off all other air conditioners in the home, instead of being apart in separately cooled rooms. Also, the department store chain Ito Yokado is promoting a campaign called ‘Cool Choice’ where people make buying choices that result in reduced electricity consumption. One example given is buying trousers and rolling them up 5 cm from the bottom to allow for cool ankles!</p>
<p>In conclusion, Cool Biz has become a standard part of Japanese summer culture. People look forward to not having to wear a tie and jacket in the summer heat. New initiatives continue to pop-up and encourage consumers to buy seasonally appropriate clothing, showing just how much of an economic impact the initiative has had. I think Cool Biz is great for Japan, both economically and environmentally, and the government should continue to actively promote it. Thank you very much for listening. Now, I’ll be happy to answer any questions you may have.</p></td>
