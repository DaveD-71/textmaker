- **Roundtable Discussions and Working Groups**

> At international conferences, roundtable discussions and working groups are small groups of people brought together to consider specific issues, and sometimes, make concrete proposals.
>
> The participants in roundtable discussions and working groups tend to be peers - they are at the same level of experience and authority- so they can freely discuss the issues and talk to each other as equals.
>
> The number of participants can be quite small - four to six people - or quite large with twelve to fifteen people. However, if the group is too large, real discussion becomes more difficult.
>
> The ideal size for effective discussion and decision-making is nine people, preferably with diverse backgrounds, to stimulate critical and creative thinking.
>
> The chairperson is responsible for managing the meetings so everyone is able to participate. Two key functions of the chair are;

1.  Be systematic by following the meeting structure step by step.

2.  Provide the right information about the procedure at the right time. If the members are informed about what is going to happen they will be able to participate more easily.

- **Meeting Protocol: Eye Contact**

> Eye contact is an essential part of the social protocol of communication. Through eye contact we acknowledge other people and confirm that we are paying attention.

- The topic leader needs to look at the participants.

- The participants need to look at the topic leader.

- The chair needs to look at everybody to coordinate the meeting.

- **Expressions for Meetings**

> **Chairperson**

- Good afternoon, everyone. I’d like to start our meeting now.

- Let’s begin by reviewing our agenda.

- There will be time after each report for questions and discussion.

- Our first report is from Mr. Ono.

- Mr. Ono, could you give us your report, please?

- Thank you for your interesting report, Mr. Ono.

- Now, we have time for some questions. (See Vocabulary Note 1 on the next page.)

- We have time for one more question.

- I’m sorry, but that’s all the time we have for discussion. We need to move on to our next speaker/item. (See Vocabulary Note 2 on the next page.)

- If there are no more comments or questions, we’ll move on to our next speaker/item. (See Vocabulary Note 2.)

- And now, our next speaker/ item on the agenda is …

- It’s time for a short break. We’ll break for 10 minutes and start again at 10:30.

- It’s time to close the meeting.

- Let’s arrange our next meeting. It will be on Monday, September 3<sup>rd</sup> at 10:30 and it will be here, again.

- We’ve had some interesting presentations and discussion. I’d like to thank you all for your participation this afternoon.

- Our meeting is closed. Thank you, everyone.

> **Speaker**

- Thank you, Mr. Yamada.

- This afternoon, I’ll be talking about …

- Thank you for your question.

- That’s an interesting question.

- I’m glad you asked that.

> **Participants**

- Thank you. I’d like to ask about …

- I have a question about …

- My question is about …

- (To interrupt) May I ask a question? / Could I ask a question about this?

> **Vocabulary Notes**
>
> **1. About asking questions**

1.  After the chair calls for questions don’t say, *“May I ask a question?”* *“May I ask a question?”* is used for interrupting a speaker.

2.  In the Q&A session you can say, *“I’d like to ask about…”* or *“I have a question about…”*

3.  Never say, “May I <u>have</u> a question?” It is not correct.

> **2. About closing the question period**

There are two reasons and different expressions for closing the question period:

1.  When there is no more time for questions and discussion the chair says:

> *“I’m sorry, but that’s all the time we have for discussion. We need to move on to our next speaker/item.”*

2.  If the participants have stopped asking questions or making comments the chair says:

> *“If there are no more comments or questions, we’ll move on to our next speaker/item.”*
