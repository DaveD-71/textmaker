# REFERENCE IMAGE DESCRIPTION

**Situations Workbook — Scene-Based Illustration Style**

---

## 1. Overall Visual Impression

The images present a **warm, professional, institutional textbook style** that balances:

* **Authority** (government / official setting)
* **Approachability** (human interaction, friendly tone)
* **Pedagogical clarity** (clear task separation, visual flow)

They do **not** feel:

* cartoonish
* childish
* corporate SaaS / HR explainer
* photo-realistic

Instead, they sit firmly in the space of a  **modern educational illustration for adult learners** .

---

## 2. Illustration Style & Rendering

### Line & Form

* No hard black outlines.
* Edges are soft, painterly, and slightly textured.
* Forms are clearly defined but not sharply cut.

### Color & Texture

* Watercolor / gouache-like texture throughout.
* Subtle paper-grain feel in backgrounds.
* Gradients are soft and diffuse, not glossy.

### Lighting

* Even, neutral lighting.
* No dramatic shadows.
* No strong light source direction.
* Characters are evenly lit so they read clearly at small sizes.

### Abstraction Level

* Semi-realistic proportions.
* Faces are recognisable but  **not highly detailed** .
* No exaggerated expressions.
* Characters read as  **roles** , not personalities.

This is important:

> You can tell *who is staff* and  *who is a visitor* , but you cannot infer backstory or character traits.

---

## 3. People Depiction (Critical Reference)

### Roles

* Typically  **2 people per scene** .
* Clear role contrast:
  * Japanese embassy staff (often female in the examples)
  * Visitor / local person (often male in the examples)

### Appearance

* Business or business-casual clothing.
* Neutral colors (navy, grey, blue, beige).
* No logos, no text on clothing.
* Hairstyles are neat and conservative.

### Facial Expression & Body Language

* Calm, neutral, polite.
* Slight smiles at most.
* Gestures are functional:
  * pointing
  * indicating direction
  * open hand gestures
* No exaggerated pointing, no dramatic motion.

### Cultural Signalling

* Japanese staff are subtly identifiable through:
  * setting
  * role
  * context
    —not stereotypes.

This is very well judged and should be preserved.

---

## 4. Scene Composition

### Camera Angle

* Eye-level or very slightly elevated.
* Medium shot:
  * upper body to waist visible
  * environment visible but not dominant

### Framing

* Characters are centred or slightly offset.
* Enough background to establish location.
* **No clutter** .

### Negative Space

* Clear open areas around scenes.
* Backgrounds fade softly, allowing text boxes to sit comfortably nearby.
* Nothing visually “pushes” against text elements.

This is one of the strongest aspects of the images.

---

## 5. Environments & Furniture

### Locations Depicted

* Embassy / consulate interior
* Reception desk area
* Corridor / internal signage area
* Lobby space

### Furniture

* Reception counter
* Walls, doors, corridor openings
* Occasional plants for warmth

Furniture is:

* minimal
* generic
* clearly institutional
* never decorative for its own sake

### Signage

* Simple, generic icons (e.g. restroom symbols).
* No readable small text.
* Signs support comprehension but do not dominate.

---

## 6. Use of National / Institutional Cues

* Japanese flag appears subtly in the header area.
* Government buildings are suggested, not detailed.
* The setting feels “official” without being specific to a real building.

This avoids political or geographic over-specificity while maintaining authenticity.

---

## 7. Visual Hierarchy & Layout Integration

Although we are *not* reusing the full page layout, the images themselves are designed to:

* Sit comfortably inside boxes or frames.
* Be readable when scaled down.
* Not demand attention away from text.

Importantly:

* Images do **not** contain instructional arrows or teaching symbols internally.
* Directional arrows, icons, and task flow graphics are part of the  **layout layer** , not the illustration layer.

This separation is something we should preserve.

---

## 8. Emotional Tone

The overall emotional tone is:

* Calm
* Helpful
* Professional
* Welcoming
* Low stress

This is particularly appropriate for:

* A2–B1 learners
* Government / institutional training
* Situations involving visitors, instructions, or coordination

Nothing feels urgent, confrontational, or chaotic.

---

## 9. What Should Be Preserved as a Reference

For future image creation, the following should be treated as  **non-negotiable reference traits** :

* Semi-flat, painterly institutional illustration style
* Two-person interaction scenes
* Role-based human depiction
* Minimal furniture, clear function
* Soft textures and warm but restrained color palette
* Neutral, polite body language
* Clear negative space around figures

---

## 10. What Should *Not* Be Automatically Reused

Some elements in these images are  **page-design artefacts** , not scene requirements:

* Arrows connecting Roleplay 1 → Roleplay 2
* Decorative dashed paths
* Section headers and badges
* Writing task boards with icons

Those belong to layout design, not to scene assets.
