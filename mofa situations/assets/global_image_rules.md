# Global Image Rules (Frozen)

This document defines the **non‑negotiable, system‑wide rules** governing all images created for the *Situations* project. These rules apply to **all scenes**, regardless of CEFR level or task type, and are used for **verification and approval**.

Once frozen, these rules should **not be edited lightly**. Scene‑specific decisions live in the *Scene Asset Register*; this document functions as the **visual style contract**.

---

## 1. Diversity & Representation (Non‑Negotiable)

### Japanese Staff
Japanese staff depicted across scenes must show **clear variation** in:
- gender
- age
- body shape
- hairstyle
- clothing cut and fit (within institutional norms)

Reusing the same‑looking Japanese person across scenes is **not acceptable** in final assets.

### Local / Non‑Japanese People
Across the full set of scenes, local people must collectively represent:
- a wide range of skin tones (Black, brown, white, East Asian, etc.)
- varied genders and ages
- culturally diverse but professional clothing

Local people often represent **visitors, vendors, drivers, candidates**, and may show more clothing variety than embassy staff.

> **Identity rule:** A different name in the Scene Asset Register always represents a different individual (face, body, presence).

---

## 2. Visual Freshness & Scene‑to‑Scene Variety

To avoid repetition and learner fatigue:
- Perspective and camera angle must rotate regularly
- Avoid repeated layouts such as:
  - Japanese staff always on the left
  - local person always on the right
  - repeated back‑facing or over‑the‑shoulder views

### Environments
- Different embassies, offices, and facilities may legitimately differ in:
  - wall colours
  - materials
  - desk types
  - architectural feel
- Do **not** default to beige walls and wood‑brown desks across scenes

---

## 3. Colour & Warmth Balance Rule

Neutral or grey‑based institutional palettes are **permitted**, but must not result in a cold or flat visual experience.

### Requirement
Scenes with predominantly neutral or grey palettes must include **at least one positive, humanising visual element**, such as:
- national flags
- framed wall art or photographs
- plants or flowers
- soft furnishings
- visible daylight or windows

These elements should:
- lift the tone
- support approachability
- never distract from the task

---

## 4. Rendering Style (Locked)

All images must use a **semi‑realistic painterly illustration style** suitable for institutional textbooks.

**Required characteristics**:
- Realistic proportions and anatomy
- Softened edges and gentle blending
- Even, illustrative lighting (no dramatic shadows)
- Subtle watercolor or digitally painted texture
- Slight abstraction of details (no readable text, no micro‑detail)

**Explicit exclusions**:
- ❌ Photorealistic or stock‑photo style imagery
- ❌ Manga, anime, or cartoon stylisation
- ❌ High‑contrast cinematic lighting

The image should resemble a *painted editorial illustration*, not a photograph.

---

## 5. Props & Technology (2026‑Safe)

- ❌ No dated technology (wired desk phones, old handsets, obsolete computers)
- ✅ Only modern, plausible devices appropriate for 2026

Props must:
- directly support the communicative task
- be minimal and purposeful
- never contradict the roleplay scenario

---

## 5. Gesture, Body Language & Cultural Accuracy

Gestures must align with **task intent**:
- Giving directions → gesture toward **space or corridor**, not people
- Explaining rules → open, neutral gestures
- Listening → attentive posture, no note‑taking unless contextually required

Avoid gestures that may be interpreted as:
- rude
- dismissive
- inattentive

Cultural accuracy is essential, especially for Japanese professional contexts.

---

## 6. Composition & Layout Readiness

All images must be layout‑ready for **A4 print integration**:
- Preserve clean negative space (typically right or top‑right)
- No arrows, captions, icons, or written signage embedded in the image
- Images should integrate cleanly with text overlays handled by layout, not illustration

---

## 7. Preview vs Final Rule

- Existing images may function as **style or composition previews**
- **Final images must fully comply** with:
  - the Scene Asset Register row
  - named identities
  - all Global Image Rules in this document

Images that violate these rules should be revised before being marked final.
