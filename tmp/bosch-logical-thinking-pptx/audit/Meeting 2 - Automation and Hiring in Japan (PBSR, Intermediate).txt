Balancing Automation and Workforce Retention 
Introduction: Understanding the Issue 
Japan is facing one of the tightest labor markets in its modern history. Unemployment is 
around 2.6%, and there are about 1.25 jobs available for every job seeker. Many 
manufacturing companies—including Bosch and its suppliers—are struggling to find enough 
qualified workers to meet production needs. At the same time, automation and AI 
technologies are becoming more affordable and more widely used across the country. 
Robots are already common in the automotive and electronics industries, but the pace of 
digital transformation varies greatly between companies and plants. 
For Bosch, this situation creates a difficult balance. On one hand, increasing automation 
could solve many labor and productivity problems. On the other hand, relying too heavily on 
machines might increase costs, reduce employee motivation, or harm the company’s 
reputation for human-centered innovation. Some divisions believe that hiring and developing 
more staff will maintain quality and morale, while others argue that faster automation is the 
only way to stay competitive in Japan’s shrinking workforce environment. 
You need to decide a future plan for your organisation regarding automation and staffing. 
 
Meeting procedure 
Stage 1: 
1. Put students into 3 groups: Pro-automation, pro-staffing, and a hybrid approach.  
2. Give the pro-automation group Information Sheet A: Automation in Japan. Give the 
pro-staff group Information Sheet B: Hiring and Labor Shortages in Japan: Give the 
hybrid group both information sheets.  
3. Get them to create their own PBSR argument supporting their preferred approach. If 
they cannot create an argument, show them the two ideas below to help elicit their 
own idea, or let them choose one of the arguments. 
Stage 2:  
1. In a large group meeting, each group presents their PBSR argument.  
2. A winning approach is chosen with reasons given. 
3. In a debrief session, feedback is focused on the strengths and weaknesses of the 
arguments presented.

Information Sheet A: Automation in Japan 
Robots and factory automation  
• 
Japan is one of the most automated countries in the world. There are about 435,000 
industrial robots working in Japanese factories. This number grew by 5% in one year. 
• 
 In 2023, about 46,000 new robots were installed. This is the second highest number 
in the world after China. Most of these robots are used in electronics, car 
manufacturing, and machinery. 
• 
There are about 419 robots for every 10,000 workers in Japan’s manufacturing 
industry. This puts Japan in 5th place globally, after South Korea, Singapore, China, 
and Germany. 
• 
In the car industry, the number is even higher — about 1,500 robots per 10,000 
workers, which is the 4th highest in the world. 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Smart Factories 
• 
A 2024 survey showed that about 1 in 4 companies in Japan already use AI. Another 1 
in 3 plan to use it soon. 
• 
The main reasons for using AI are labor shortages and the need to reduce costs. 
• 
However, many companies still hesitate because of high costs, lack of skills, and 
employee worries about AI. 
• 
The use of generative AI (for example, ChatGPT-type tools) is growing quickly — from 
33% of workers in 2024 to 42% in 2025. 
What this means for Bosch 
• 
Japan already uses a lot of automation, especially in electronics and automotive 
industries. 
• 
There is still room to add more automation, but companies need to train employees 
and help them adjust to new systems.

Information Sheet B: Hiring and Labor Shortages in Japan  
Key statistics 
• 
Japan’s unemployment rate is only 2.6%, which means almost everyone who wants a 
job has one. 
• 
For every job seeker, there are about 1.25 open positions, so companies compete 
hard to find people. 
Worker shortages and business impact 
• 
About two-thirds of Japanese companies say they are seriously affected by not having 
enough workers. 
• 
In 2024, 342 companies went bankrupt because they couldn’t find enough staff. This 
was the highest number ever recorded. 
• 
The total number of company bankruptcies is expected to go over 10,000 in 2024, the 
highest level in more than ten years. 
How companies are coping 
• 
Most companies allow employees to work until age 65, but only about 30% keep 
employees longer than that. 
• 
The Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) visa program is expanding. It lets foreign workers 
fill jobs in industries like construction, manufacturing, and care services. 
• 
The OECD says Japan’s job market is very tight, and foreign workers will play a growing 
role in the future. 
What this means for Bosch 
• 
It is very difficult to hire in Japan now because there are more jobs than workers. 
• 
Bosch may need a balanced approach: increase automation, retrain current staff, 
keep older workers longer, and hire skilled foreign employees when possible. 
• 
The company should also watch its suppliers, since smaller firms might fail if they 
can’t find enough workers.

Balancing Automation and Workforce Retention 
Pro-Automation 1: Cost and Efficiency Focus 
Problem 
Rising labor costs and staff shortages are slowing production and 
increasing expenses. 
Background 
Japan’s job market is extremely tight, with only 2.6% 
unemployment and 1.25 jobs per applicant. Recruiting takes 
longer, and some suppliers cannot fill critical roles. 
Solution 
Introduce more robots and AI systems in repetitive tasks such as 
inspection, packaging, and logistics. This reduces dependency 
on human labor for routine work. 
Result 
Production speed and consistency improve. The company lowers 
long-term labor costs and becomes less vulnerable to labor 
shortages, though short-term training costs increase. 
 
Pro-Automation 2: Quality and Innovation Focus 
Problem 
Product quality and delivery schedules are inconsistent because 
of human error and low workforce availability. 
Background 
Automation technologies such as smart sensors and AI-based 
quality control are becoming more affordable and reliable. 
Competitors are already using them. 
Solution 
Expand automation to focus on quality control and data analysis, 
allowing faster detection of defects and smarter decision-
making. 
Result 
Fewer quality problems, faster decision-making, and improved 
competitiveness in high-precision markets like automotive parts 
and electronics.

Balancing Automation and Workforce Retention 
Pro-Staffing 1: Human Expertise Focus 
Problem 
Automation can handle routine work but not creative or 
judgment-based decisions that rely on human experience. 
Background 
Bosch’s reputation for reliability and innovation depends on 
skilled engineers and technicians who can adapt quickly to 
change. 
Solution 
Invest in recruiting and developing highly skilled workers. Offer 
better career paths, training, and flexible work systems to attract 
younger workers and retain older ones. 
Result 
A stronger and more loyal workforce improves innovation, 
problem-solving, and company culture. Employee satisfaction 
and retention increase. 
 
Pro-Staffing 2: Customer Relationship Focus 
Problem 
Automation reduces human interaction, which can hurt 
customer relationships and service quality. 
Background 
Japanese customers often value personal trust and direct 
communication more than low cost or speed. 
Solution 
Increase hiring in customer-facing departments and invest in 
relationship-based sales and service teams. Provide 
communication training to strengthen client trust. 
Result 
Bosch maintains a strong human connection with clients, 
protecting its brand image and ensuring long-term partnerships 
despite rising automation elsewhere.

Balancing Automation and Workforce Retention 
Hybrid 1: Smart Automation with Human Oversight 
Problem 
Labor shortages limit productivity, but full automation could 
cause morale problems and high costs. 
Background 
Japan is a world leader in robotics, yet many companies lack 
skilled workers to manage automated systems. 
Solution 
Introduce automation for repetitive processes, but ensure that 
trained employees manage, maintain, and optimize the systems. 
Pair each robot with a human supervisor. 
Result 
Production becomes faster, and employees develop new skills 
managing technology. The balance between people and 
machines increases stability and efficiency. 
 
Hybrid 2: Regional and Task-Based Strategy 
Problem 
Different factories face different challenges—some lack workers, 
while others already have strong teams. 
Background 
A one-size-fits-all automation policy is risky and may waste 
money in areas where staffing is stable. 
Solution 
Apply automation selectively in plants with severe labor 
shortages or high-cost operations. In other locations, strengthen 
training and flexible work systems to retain staff. 
Result 
Each site gets a customized solution. Overall costs stay under 
control, productivity rises, and both automation and staffing 
investments are used where they deliver the most benefit.
