The EV Supply Chain Dilemma 
Bosch Logical Thinking & Discussion Course 
Discussion 
Before starting the simulation, review and discuss these key terms and ideas. Understanding 
them will help you take part in the meeting more effectively.
Competitiveness – How a company 
compares to others in its industry. A 
competitive company offers high-
quality products at a good price and 
can adapt quickly to market changes. 
ESG (Environmental, Social, and 
Governance) – A framework 
companies use to measure their 
impact on the planet, how they treat 
people, and how responsibly they are 
managed. ESG is becoming a major 
factor in investment and business 
decisions. 
Ethical sourcing – Buying materials and 
products from suppliers that respect 
people, communities, and the 
environment. It means checking that 
suppliers do not use unfair labor 
practices or harm the environment. 
Supply chain transparency – The ability to 
see and understand every step in the 
supply chain—from raw materials to 
final product—so the company can 
confirm that partners follow laws and 
ethical standards. 
Due diligence – The careful investigation a 
company must do before working with 
another company. It means checking 
legal, environmental, and social risks 
before signing a contract. 
CSDDD (Corporate Sustainability Due 
Diligence Directive) – A new rule from 
the European Union that will require 
large companies to check their entire 
supply chain for environmental and 
human rights risks. It aims to make 
global business more responsible and 
transparent.
Discuss with your classmates: 
1. Why do you think ESG and ethical sourcing have become important in global 
business? 
2. What might happen if a company ignores due diligence or hides supply chain 
problems? 
3. How could strong supply chain transparency help Bosch maintain its 
competitiveness and reputation?

The Situation 
Bosch Japan is preparing to launch a new line of high-efficiency electric vehicle (EV) 
components for both domestic and international markets. The project is part of Bosch’s 
global initiative to expand its role in sustainable mobility and reduce its carbon footprint. 
However, the production of one key component — a lithium-ion battery module — faces a 
serious supply chain challenge. 
A major European supplier has recently increased prices by 30% due to higher energy and 
labor costs. Meanwhile, a new supplier based in Southeast Asia has offered to supply the 
same component at a much lower price. On paper, this alternative supplier meets technical 
specifications and delivery schedules. However, early research suggests that the supplier’s 
environmental and labor practices may not meet Bosch’s ESG (Environmental, Social, and 
Governance) standards. Independent auditors have raised questions about factory 
emissions, worker safety, and transparency in sourcing materials. 
Senior management has called an emergency meeting to decide whether Bosch should:  
1. Continue with the current European supplier, despite the high cost; 
2. Switch to the Southeast Asian supplier, gaining a strong cost advantage but 
potential ethical and brand risks; or 
3. Adopt a temporary hybrid solution, dividing production between the two suppliers 
while gathering more data. 
Your task in this meeting is to: 
1. Discuss the problems and brainstorm options to solve them, 
2. Evaluate the risks and benefits of each option, and  
3. Decide the best course of action. 
The decision will affect production schedules, pricing strategy, brand reputation, and 
Bosch’s long-term ESG commitments. 
 
Note 
All facts, figures, and sources in this simulation are fictionalized for educational 
purposes. They are designed to resemble realistic business data and to help students 
practice assessing credibility, reliability, and logical reasoning.

Role A: Managing Director (Chair)
You are chairing the meeting and must 
guide the discussion toward a decision 
today. Your main goal is to ensure Bosch 
maintains profitability while protecting its 
brand image. You are under pressure from 
headquarters in Germany to reduce 
production costs by at least 10% by Q4, 
but you are also responsible for upholding 
Bosch’s sustainability commitments.  
Global media attention on corporate ESG 
compliance has been increasing — in 
2023, 78% of Japanese consumers said 
they would stop buying from a company 
linked to unethical suppliers (Nikkei 
survey).
Evidence you can use: Bosch’s 2024 
Annual Report highlights a 9% profit 
margin target for its EV division. Any 
supply change that lowers production 
costs by more than 5% could significantly 
support this goal. 
Personal view: You prefer to make 
decisions based on data and risk analysis. 
You are concerned about long-term 
reputation and compliance, but you must 
also meet financial targe
 
Role B: Purchasing Manager
Your role is to manage supplier contracts 
and cost efficiency. You have verified that 
the Southeast Asian supplier can meet all 
technical and logistical requirements and 
deliver on time. The supplier’s price per 
battery module is ¥27,000, compared to 
¥40,000 from the European partner — a 
cost reduction of nearly 33%. The cost 
savings could reach ¥1.2 billion per year 
across all production lines. 
You argue that Bosch cannot stay 
competitive in the EV market if it 
continues to pay European prices, 
especially with strong competition from 
China and Korea.  
Evidence you can use: Bloomberg data 
shows lithium-ion battery prices have 
fallen 14% globally in 2024, yet Bosch’s 
current supplier has raised costs. 
Additional info: You believe ESG risks can 
be managed through regular audits and 
clear supplier contracts with penalties for 
violations. You have prepared a draft 12-
month monitoring plan. 
 
 


===== PAGE 4 =====
4 
Role C: Sustainability Officer 
You are responsible for ensuring all 
operations comply with Bosch’s global 
ESG policy. You are alarmed by reports 
from an NGO indicating that the 
Southeast Asian supplier’s factory emits 
30% more CO₂ per unit than industry 
standards and that its subcontractors have 
been cited for poor worker safety.  
Bosch’s global sustainability report is due 
in three months, and any negative findings 
could have serious reputational 
consequences. 
Evidence you can use: Bosch has pledged 
to achieve carbon neutrality (Scope 1 & 2) 
by 2030 and a 15% reduction in Scope 3 
emissions by 2027. Partnering with a non-
compliant supplier could put these targets 
at risk. 
Additional info: You believe customers 
and investors increasingly prioritize ethical 
production — a 2023 Deloitte survey 
showed that 65% of global investors 
consider ESG performance a key factor in 
long-term company value.
 
Role D: Legal Counsel
Your responsibility is to evaluate the legal 
and compliance risks. If Bosch knowingly 
contracts a supplier that violates labor or 
environmental regulations, it could face 
fines, lawsuits, and international trade 
restrictions.  
You also know that the EU Corporate 
Sustainability Due Diligence Directive 
(CSDDD) will come into effect in 2026, 
requiring full supply chain transparency for 
all companies operating in the EU. 
Evidence you can use: Non-compliance 
penalties under similar EU laws can reach 
up to 5% of global annual turnover. The 
Southeast Asian supplier’s emissions 
reports lack third-party verification, which 
would be required under these 
regulations. 
Additional info: If Bosch signs a contract 
now and problems arise later, the 
company may be held liable for failing to 
perform adequate due diligence, even if 
violations occur abroad.
 
 


===== PAGE 5 =====
5 
Role E: Customer Sales Manager
You manage relationships with Bosch’s 
main automotive OEM customers in Japan. 
If Bosch changes the battery supplier, you 
must coordinate with customers, since this 
could affect their production and quality 
control. You often balance internal cost 
goals with customer expectations. 
Main concerns: Maintaining customer 
trust, preventing production delays, and 
giving clear explanations for any supplier 
change. 
Evidence you can use: Japanese OEM 
customers increasingly value ethical 
sourcing—72% of automotive buyers say 
ESG factors influence their decisions 
(Dentsu Research, 2024). But they also 
demand stability and consistent quality. 
Additional info: Customers will likely need 
formal documentation and testing 
approval before accepting a new supplier. 
Personal view: The supplier must cut costs 
while maintaining Bosch’s reliability and 
customer confidence. You prefer a 
cautious approach that protects long-term 
relationships. 
 
Role F: Head of R&D (Technical Expert)
You are responsible for product quality 
and innovation. Both suppliers meet 
Bosch’s basic engineering standards, but 
the European supplier has a 12-year 
partnership record with Bosch and a 99.2% 
on-time delivery rate.  
Their failure rate per batch is below 0.3%, 
compared to the Southeast Asian 
supplier’s trial rate of 2.1%. 
Evidence you can use: Delays or quality 
issues could add ¥300 million in warranty 
and recall costs if products fail in the 
market. 
Additional info: You also know that the 
European supplier is testing a new 
recycling process that could reduce 
production emissions by 10% by 2026 — 
aligning closely with Bosch’s carbon goals.
