

===== PAGE 1 =====
Meeting 1 — Bosch Investment Opportunities (ORE) 
Procedure: 
Students are tasked with choosing a project Bosch should invest in. 
Stage 1: 
Students work in pairs. Give each pair two project options from the list. Tell them 
they must choose one project and prepare an ORE argument supporting their 
decision. 
 
Stage 2: 
• Before the meeting a begins, the decision-making method to be used in this 
meeting is explained (consensus vs authority). 
• Each group presents their chosen project and argues for it using the ORE 
argument approach. 
• The winning project is chosen, and a rationale for choosing the winner is given. 
• Time is spent at the end on a meeting debrief session. 
 
 


===== PAGE 2 =====
1) Heat pumps for homes and buildings 
Why it matters:  
Heat pumps replace gas or oil boilers and can heat and cool homes efficiently 
using electricity. They help reduce carbon emissions and energy bills while 
supporting global climate goals. Many countries now offer government incentives 
to encourage installation.  
Evidence ideas:  
• According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), global heat pump sales 
grew by 11% in 2022, with particularly strong growth in Europe.  
• The U.S. Department of Energy reported that in 2022, heat pump sales 
exceeded gas furnaces for the first time.  
• Germany’s government aims for 6 million heat pumps by 2030, supported 
by financial subsidies.  
2) EV charging solutions (hardware + software) 
Why it matters:  
As electric vehicles become more common, there is an urgent need for charging 
stations at home, at work, and in public places. Investing in reliable charging 
infrastructure positions Bosch to support the EV revolution and reduce 
transportation emissions.  
Evidence ideas:  
• Global EV sales grew 35% in 2023 to reach 14 million units, according to 
the IEA Global EV Outlook 2024.  
• Europe alone now has more than 750,000 public charging points (European 
Alternative Fuels Observatory).  
• Governments across the EU, Japan, and the U.S. have committed billions 
of dollars to charging infrastructure.  
 
 


===== PAGE 3 =====
3) Battery recycling and second‑life storage 
Why it matters:  
When EV batteries lose capacity, they can still be reused for stationary energy 
storage. Recycling also recovers valuable metals like lithium, nickel, and cobalt, 
reducing waste and dependence on mining.  
Evidence ideas:  
• Recycling firms such as Li‑Cycle and Redwood Materials report recovery 
rates of up to 95% of battery materials.  
• The EU Battery Regulation (2023) requires at least 50% lithium recovery by 
2027 and rising thereafter.  
• Nissan’s “4R Energy” project reuses used EV batteries to power streetlights 
and solar systems in Japan.  
4) Hydrogen components (fuel cells and H₂ engines) 
Why it matters:  
Hydrogen power provides a clean alternative to fossil fuels, especially for 
industries and heavy vehicles where full electrification is difficult. Hydrogen fuel 
cells emit only water vapor and could help reduce global CO₂ emissions.  
Evidence ideas:  
• The German National Hydrogen Strategy allocates €9 billion for hydrogen 
technology development.  
• Toyota and Hyundai already sell fuel‑cell cars, and Bosch announced 
investment in hydrogen fuel‑cell production in Stuttgart and Chongqing.  
• The IEA projects hydrogen demand could triple by 2030 under current 
plans.

5) Smart‑home energy management 
Why it matters:  
Smart systems connect household devices like solar panels, thermostats, and 
batteries to automatically save energy and money. They help homeowners reduce 
bills and support the transition to renewable power.  
Evidence ideas:  
• The global smart‑home market exceeded $100 billion in 2023 (Statista).  
• Google Nest reports that smart thermostats save customers 10–12% on 
heating and 15% on cooling costs.  
• Utility companies in the U.S. and EU offer rebates to users who install 
energy‑saving smart devices.  
6) E‑bike and micro‑mobility drive systems 
Why it matters:  
Cities want to reduce car use and traffic congestion. E‑bikes and scooters provide 
clean, efficient travel for short distances. Bosch’s proven technology in e‑bike 
motors and batteries makes it a natural leader in this market.  
Evidence ideas:  
• The European Cyclists’ Federation reports that over 5.5 million e‑bikes 
were sold in Europe in 2022.  
• In Germany, e‑bikes now account for 48% of all new bicycle sales (ZIV 
report 2023).  
• Cities like Paris and Amsterdam are investing heavily in bike‑friendly 
infrastructure.  
 
 


===== PAGE 5 =====
7) “AI retrofit kits” for factories (sensors + predictive maintenance) 
Why it matters:  
Many older machines are reliable but lack smart monitoring. By adding sensors 
and AI software, companies can predict maintenance needs before breakdowns 
occur—saving costs and improving productivity.  
Evidence ideas:  
• According to Deloitte, predictive maintenance can reduce downtime by 20–
50% and increase equipment life by 20–40%.  
• Bosch Rexroth’s own pilot projects reported up to 25% lower maintenance 
costs after installing AI‑based monitoring systems.  
• The World Economic Forum notes predictive maintenance could save 
industries $630 billion annually worldwide.  
8) On‑demand 3D printing for spare parts 
Why it matters:  
3D printing lets manufacturers produce spare parts locally, reducing delivery 
times and storage costs. It supports sustainability by cutting waste and emissions 
from transport.  
Evidence ideas:  
• Siemens Mobility reduced lead times for replacement train parts from six 
weeks to 13 hours using 3D printing.  
• The global additive manufacturing market is valued at $20 billion (2024) 
and projected to double by 2030.  
• Aerospace firms like Airbus and Boeing use printed components to reduce 
material waste by up to 50%.  
 
 


===== PAGE 6 =====
9) Cybersecurity for connected products and vehicles 
Why it matters:  
More Bosch products are internet‑connected—from cars to appliances—creating 
risk of hacking or data theft. Strong cybersecurity builds trust and ensures 
compliance with new international regulations.  
Evidence ideas:  
• The EU Cyber Resilience Act (2024) and UNECE R155 require automotive 
cybersecurity management systems.  
• Cybersecurity Ventures predicts global cybercrime damage will reach 
$10.5 trillion annually by 2025.  
• Bosch already operates internal programs ensuring secure software 
updates for connected products.  
10) Warehouse and last‑mile robotics modules 
Why it matters:  
E‑commerce growth increases pressure on logistics. Robots that lift, move, and 
sort packages help warehouses work faster, safer, and more efficiently, 
addressing labor shortages.  
Evidence ideas:  
• McKinsey estimates that warehouse automation can improve productivity 
by 25–50%.  
• Amazon operates over 750,000 robots in fulfillment centers worldwide, 
cutting delivery time and cost.  
• The global warehouse robotics market is expected to reach $20 billion by 
2030 (MarketsandMarkets).
