## Agent 3 Review: Business Presentation Specialist

Review role: business realism, model parity, case fit, data examples, tool-neutral workflows, slide/document roles, delivery guidance, Q&A, contingency practices, finance/trading guardrails, government administrative guardrails, and final-presentation usefulness.

Teaching-point fit rule applied: each teaching point must logically connect to the model's presentation topic. Data, charts, visuals, and document roles should not be added unless the model topic naturally requires them.

### Critical issues

1. Model names are not yet stable enough for unit cross-references.

The process model file uses specific model names: `Reducing trade confirmation delays` and `Reducing returned application forms` (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:7`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:160`). The launch model file uses generic labels: `Business-client launch` and `Government-agency launch` (`product-service-program-launch-models.md:7`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:141`). The project-results model file uses sector labels rather than presentation titles (`project-results-briefing-models.md:14`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:125`).

Recommended fix: give all six models stable learner-facing names that match the concrete briefs in `plan3-case-model-brief.md`. Suggested names:

- `Reducing trade confirmation delays`
- `Reducing returned application forms`
- `Launching a client reporting dashboard`
- `Launching an application support desk`
- `Reporting results from the exception resolution pilot`
- `Reporting results from the intake checklist trial`

2. Workplace model timing may be confused with the Standard final-presentation target.

The process models use `6-minute presentation plus 4-minute Q&A` (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:51`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:204`), the launch models use `7 minutes plus 5 minutes for Q&A` (`product-service-program-launch-models.md:43`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:177`), and the results models use `8 minutes plus 5 minutes for Q&A` (`project-results-briefing-models.md:53`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:164`). The Standard final delivery target is 5-7 minutes plus required Q&A.

These timings are realistic for workplace reference models, but Unit 12 learners may treat the appendix models as exact final-task timings.

Recommended fix: add a short note at the start of the appendices: "These are workplace reference models. Your course final may use a shorter timing such as 5-7 minutes plus Q&A, depending on teacher instructions." Also revise Unit 12 references so learners use the model structure, not necessarily the model timing.

### Important improvements

1. Delivery and contingency guidance is uneven across model sets.

The project-results models include explicit in-person, online, and async/recorded delivery adaptation (`project-results-briefing-models.md:62-67`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:173-177`). The process and launch models include setting and materials, but not concrete delivery adaptation or contingency guidance (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:46-68`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:39-57`).

Recommended fix: add two or three short delivery adaptation bullets to the process and launch model sets. Do not overbuild them. For process improvement, include room/online workflow-visual handling. For launch, include hybrid dashboard/service-flow visibility and backup PDF/document handling.

2. Confidentiality, accessibility, and guardrail checks should be parallel in every model.

The finance/trading process model has a strong confidentiality check (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:64-68`). The government process model has a strong accessibility and symbol check (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:218-223`). The launch finance model has confidentiality guidance (`product-service-program-launch-models.md:57`), and the launch government model has accessibility guidance (`product-service-program-launch-models.md:191`). The results finance model has confidentiality guidance (`project-results-briefing-models.md:68`), and the results government model has accessibility guidance (`project-results-briefing-models.md:179`).

This division makes sense by context, but Phase 6 QA expects both privacy/security and audience/visual accessibility to be checked across deliverables.

Recommended fix: add one compact "Privacy, security, and accessibility check" subsection to each model. The emphasis can differ by sector, but no model should omit the category entirely.

3. Unit 9 usefulness is limited to the project-results models.

The model-to-unit trace matrix correctly maps Unit 9 to project results, because online/async adaptation naturally fits chart-based results briefings. However, process and launch presentations also often happen online or hybrid, and the appendices currently give fewer cues for that use.

Recommended fix: keep Unit 9's main appendix reference to the project-results models, but add optional one-line online/hybrid notes to process and launch models so teachers can use them in mixed delivery contexts without distorting the model purpose.

4. Q&A is strong, but sector pressure points could be made more reusable.

All models include useful Q&A pairs (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:111-147`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:267-303`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:119-128`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:252-261`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:103-112`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:214-223`). The project-results comparison table is especially useful because it names sensitive information risk and Q&A pressure side by side (`project-results-briefing-models.md:240-247`).

Recommended fix: add similar comparison rows for Q&A pressure and sensitive information risk to the process and launch teacher comparison notes. This will help Unit 10 draw sector-safe examples without adding high-risk details.

## Agent 2 Review: Language Editor

Review role: B1-B2 level control, ESL pedagogy, Japanese-learner support, presentation-English language usefulness, pronunciation/intelligibility opportunities, learner-owned English policy, Standard voice, and workbook usability.

### Critical issues

No blocking language issue. The appendix drafts are usable first drafts for Standard, but they should receive a consistency and scaffolding pass before being used as learner-facing model appendices.

### Important improvements

1. Heading style is inconsistent across appendix files.

The style sheet prefers sentence-style headings. The process and project-results files mostly use sentence-style headings (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:1`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:1`), while the launch file title uses title case (`product-service-program-launch-models.md:1`). Model headings are also inconsistent: specific titles in process, generic labels in launch, and sector labels in project results.

Recommended fix: use sentence-style appendix headings and stable model names. The learner should see a presentation topic, not an internal category label.

2. Some specialist terms need brief first-use glosses.

Terms such as `exception`, `handoff`, `control process`, `sanitized`, `pre-read`, `leave-behind`, `proprietary system screens`, `async`, and `volume-adjusted results` are useful workplace English but may be heavy for B1 learners (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:11`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:36`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:57`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:100-104`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:60`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:62-67`).

Recommended fix: add a short key-word note in each appendix or define terms parenthetically at first use. Keep definitions concrete, for example: "sanitized examples means examples with names, account numbers, and identifying details removed or changed."

3. The appendix models should make pronunciation/intelligibility support easier to reuse.

The sample openings are useful (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:94-99`, `product-service-program-launch-models.md:59-65`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:70-72`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:181-183`), but they do not yet show where learners should pause, stress key words, or reduce long sentences before speaking.

Recommended fix: add one short "Delivery language note" after each sample opening or language excerpt. Include one pause/stress point, not a full pronunciation lesson. This supports Units 8, 11, and 12 without overloading the appendix.

4. Model answers in Q&A are strong but sometimes long for B1 practice.

The paragraph-style Q&A answers in the process model are clear but sometimes longer than a B1 learner can reuse in speaking (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:117`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:135`, `process-improvement-briefing-models.md:291`). The launch and project-results tables are easier to scan but still include multi-sentence answers.

Recommended fix: where answers are long, add a short "answer frame" before or after the full answer. Example: "Short frame: No, this is not a new report. It is a shared handoff record."

5. Japanese-learner support is underdeveloped in the appendices.

The appendices currently model useful direct but polite English, such as "I recommend..." and "By the end..." (`process-improvement-briefing-models.md:96-98`, `project-results-briefing-models.md:72`). However, they do not explicitly call out common learner issues such as vague topic openings, indirect recommendations, overlong evidence explanation, or reading a full script.

Recommended fix: add brief teacher notes, not intrusive learner notes, on:

- direct purpose statements: avoid "I will explain about..."
- direct but polite recommendation language: "I recommend..."
- cautious evidence language: "suggests," "appears to," "does not prove"
- avoiding full-script reading from the model text
