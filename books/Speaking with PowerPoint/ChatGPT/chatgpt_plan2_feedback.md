`plan2.md` is substantially better than the first plan. It now resembles a genuine curriculum and production plan rather than a repository-processing workflow. Most of my earlier concerns have been addressed.

However, it is not quite ready for execution. One major scope conflict and several curriculum omissions remain.

## What Plan 2 fixes successfully

It now defines:

* the intended product: the tiered `Presentation Skills` series;
* a canonical 12-lesson Standard course;
* B1–B2 adult business learners in Japan;
* outcomes for Essentials, Standard and Long;
* a coherent curriculum sequence;
* recurring modern business cases;
* answer keys, trainer notes and assessment materials;
* an instructional-first asset policy;
* phased production and separate QA criteria;
* concrete deliverables.

The revised curriculum map is particularly strong. Lessons 1–6 create a logical progression from audience and purpose through message, structure, storyboarding, visual design and data. Lessons 8–12 then move effectively from delivery to interaction, rehearsal and final performance.

## The unresolved scope conflict

Plan 2 says:

> “The old two-day sprint framing is retired for this series rebuild.”

That is a project decision, not something the plan can assume. The original requirement was to review, revise and rewrite the textbook within two days. The consolidated task list deliberately distinguished between:

* the two-day modernization of the present book; and
* a future 10–12-unit course rebuild.

Plan 2 has selected the second option and expanded it further into three tiered manuscripts. That may be the right direction, especially if the existing repository already contains the tiered series, but it is not compatible with the original deadline.

The plan therefore needs one explicit statement of authorization, such as:

> The project scope has changed from a two-day revision of the 39-page textbook to a longer rebuild of the existing three-tier Presentation Skills series.

If that decision has not actually been made, Plan 2 is planning the wrong project.

It should also replace “production-ready” with “proposed production plan” until the specification decisions in Phase 1 have been confirmed. At present, the Summary declares several decisions as settled while Phase 1 says they still need confirmation.

## Important curriculum content missing from the map

### 1. AI-assisted presentation work

This is the clearest omission. The consolidated list explicitly requires:

* responsible use of AI for brainstorming, outlining, language support, slide drafting, images and critique;
* fact-checking;
* confidentiality and company-policy awareness;
* copyright and licensing caution;
* human responsibility;
* an activity evaluating an AI-generated slide, outline or visual.

Plan 2 discusses using the OpenAI Images API to produce the textbook, but it does not teach learners how to use AI. That is a serious distinction.

AI should either receive part of Lesson 7 or be integrated visibly into Lessons 2, 5 and 7, with its own acceptance criteria in QA.

### 2. Presentation English is too dispersed

The map names language focuses, but it lacks a clearly planned language syllabus covering:

* opening and establishing relevance;
* previewing the presentation;
* moving between sections;
* referring backwards and forwards;
* emphasizing the key message;
* summarizing;
* making recommendations;
* closing with a decision, action or next step;
* pronunciation and intelligibility;
* Japanese-learner issues such as katakana-influenced pronunciation and register.

These should not necessarily become another lesson, but every lesson should contain a more precise language inventory. “B1–B2 language support is visible” is too vague to function as an acceptance criterion.

### 3. Opening and closing have become too implicit

Lesson 3 includes structures and agenda language, but the consolidated requirements preserve the basic opening–development–close frame. Learners also need to know how openings and closings change according to the business outcome.

The map should explicitly include:

* opening with purpose and audience relevance;
* previewing an appropriate structure;
* summarizing the key message;
* stating the requested decision or action;
* moving naturally into discussion or Q&A.

### 4. In-person delivery is underdefined

Lesson 8 covers speaking with visuals, while Lesson 9 covers online and hybrid delivery. Neither explicitly covers the modernized in-person guidance required by the consolidated list:

* voice and intelligibility;
* posture and movement;
* eye contact in different room configurations;
* use of notes;
* safe pointer and cursor use;
* natural rather than rigid body-language advice;
* cultural context.

Lesson 8 should be broadened to “Delivering In Person with Visuals,” or its outcome and activities should explicitly include these areas.

### 5. Document roles and contingency planning are missing

Lesson 7 deals with format choice, but it should explicitly distinguish:

* live presentation slides;
* presenter notes;
* pre-reads;
* worksheets used during a session;
* leave-behinds;
* appendices and backup slides;
* detailed reports or documents.

It should also include the practical preparation requirements from the consolidated list:

* approved cloud location;
* offline copy;
* PDF fallback;
* export and compatibility testing;
* font, media and link checks;
* venue/platform testing;
* company security requirements.

These are more useful than discussing a long list of individual presentation products.

### 6. Accessibility is only a QA requirement

Accessibility appears in the asset and production checks, but it is not clearly taught to the learner. The consolidated requirement calls for an accessibility checklist within the visual-design teaching and reference to accessibility in the final rubric.

Lesson 5 should explicitly cover:

* contrast;
* avoiding color-only meaning;
* readable text;
* slide titles;
* logical reading order;
* alt text;
* captions;
* accessible templates.

The final rubric should assess accessibility or, at minimum, visual usability.

### 7. Asynchronous presentation is absent

This was a lower-priority recommendation, so it does not require a full lesson. Nevertheless, Lesson 9 could include a brief extension on recorded presentations:

* scripting versus speaking naturally;
* pacing;
* captions;
* visual clarity without live explanation;
* recording and review.

## The three-model strategy needs tighter control

Using three model strands is better than forcing every teaching point through one case. However, the plan also says learners will progressively build one final presentation. The relationship between these two ideas is not yet defined.

For each lesson, the curriculum map should specify:

| Lesson | Teaching model used | Learner’s cumulative output |
| ------ | ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 1      | Process Improvement | Audience-outcome brief      |
| 2      | Process Improvement | Key message and evidence    |
| 3      | Launch comparison   | Selected structure          |
| 4      | Process Improvement | Storyboard                  |
| …      | …                   | …                           |

Otherwise, three recurring cases could easily become three unrelated sets of examples, while the cumulative learner journey remains theoretical.

“Three model strands are used intentionally, not randomly” should therefore be replaced by a concrete model-to-lesson allocation.

## The tier plans are not equally developed

The Standard tier is reasonably well specified. Essentials and Long are still descriptions rather than production plans.

For Essentials, “combine Lessons 5–7” is especially problematic: visual design, data visuals and format choice are three large areas. Simply combining them does not establish what is retained or removed.

For Long, four extension topics are listed, but it is unclear whether they become:

* additional lessons;
* extensions within existing lessons;
* optional modules;
* a separate final team task.

Before drafting, Plan 2 needs a tier-mapping table showing every Standard lesson and what happens to it in Essentials and Long.

## Assessment needs more definition

The rubric is named but its relationship to the course could be clearer. The plan should establish:

* the rubric categories;
* the performance scale;
* whether language is assessed separately from presentation effectiveness;
* how B1 and B2 performance are distinguished;
* whether the same rubric is used across all tiers;
* whether Q&A is compulsory;
* how peer feedback differs from formal assessment;
* what evidence is collected during the course.

A single B1–B2 course can work, but the assessment needs level-sensitive descriptors. Otherwise, “B1–B2” describes the audience without controlling the difficulty or expected performance.

## Production-plan refinements

A few smaller changes would make execution safer:

* Add formal approval gates after the specification, curriculum map, model cases and asset register. The plan refers to approval, but it does not say who approves what or what prevents premature drafting.
* Define the style sheet’s contents: English variety, terminology, capitalization, punctuation, instruction style, model-answer conventions and Japanese-note policy.
* Add factual/source QA for all case data, not only software claims.
* Add confidentiality, privacy and copyright checks to both learner-facing AI guidance and the production process.
* Replace “as far as tooling allows” in PDF accessibility QA with a stated minimum test and a documented list of limitations.
* Add a traceability matrix connecting every consolidated task-list item to a lesson, deliverable, QA check or explicit deferral. This is the simplest way to ensure nothing disappears during the rebuild.
* Remove or demote the list of named presentation products from the Summary. The curriculum itself is appropriately principle-led; the product list makes the plan look more tool-focused than it really is.

## Overall judgment

Plan 2 has moved from a weak technical workflow to a strong proposed course architecture. I would consider it approximately 85–90% ready.

Before execution, I would require five changes:

1. Resolve and document the conflict between the two-day rewrite and the three-tier rebuild.
2. Add learner-facing AI guidance and an AI-critique activity.
3. Expand the curriculum mapping for presentation English, openings/closings, in-person delivery, accessibility and document roles.
4. Create explicit Essentials–Standard–Long and model-to-lesson mappings.
5. Add a traceability table against the consolidated revision task list.

Once those are done, the plan should be robust enough to guide the rebuild without silently losing important requirements.
