# 12 Delivery Skills for PowerPoint 

> <img src="assets/media/image27.jpeg" style="width:2.09861in;height:1.48125in" alt="PR Presentation Strategy" />\[\[SHAPE:PR Presentation Strategy\]\]

- **Slides Vs. Handouts**

<!-- -->

- Presenting PowerPoint as projected slides is very effective for a large audience.

- Giving PowerPoint handouts can be very effective for a small audience of 1 to 5 people.

- There are different delivery skills for projection and handouts.

<!-- -->

- **Speaking with PowerPoint Slides**

1.  **Speaking Position**

- Stand at the front near the screen so you can see your audience and your audience can see you.

- You can move from one side of the screen to the other if you need to.

2.  **Speaking notes**

- Do not read the projected PowerPoint.

- Do not read your presentation from the computer screen. You will be looking down, not at your audience. They will think you are not prepared and don’t know what you are talking about.

- Speak to your audience: Speak from memory or speak from notes in your hand.

3.  **Pointing and speaking**

- Point and look at the screen then turn and speak to your audience: Do not look at the screen and speak to the screen.

- Point to the screen with your inside hand, the one closest to the screen. If you point with your outside hand, you will turn your back to your audience.

- Point to the screen with your hand or a pointer stick. Avoid using infrared pointers: they are dangerous to eyes and the red dot on the screen always moves so it is hard to look at.

4.  **Changing the slides**

- If you control the slide changes from a computer, make sure it is close and convenient.

- If you use a remote control, hold it in one hand.

- Test all the equipment before your presentation begins.

5.  **Presenting PowerPoint**

- Have your title slide on the screen before you begin.

- Show your charts and graphs only when you need them.

- Don't show them before you speak about them.

- Don't leave them up too long.

- Introduce the slide when it comes on the screen by giving the title, explaining any symbols, and pointing at and explaining the key points.

- The audience will not be able to understand your chart until you introduce it.

- During the Q&A leave your conclusion slide on the screen.

<!-- -->

- **Speaking with PowerPoint Handouts**

<!-- -->

- Sit where you can see your whole audience and they can see you.

- Sit up straight at the front of your chair.

- Don’t distribute your handouts until you are going to use them.

- If you give the handout too soon, your audience will look at the handout and ignore you.

- Introduce your presentation briefly then, when you are ready to use it, distribute the handout to the audience.

- Hold your handout so everyone can see it. Point to the item you are talking about.

- Speak to your audience: Do not read your handout to your audience.

- Tell your audience what page to look at and when to turn the page: keep your audience looking at the correct page and focused on your message.

- Introduce each page: What is it? What is the message of the page?

- When you finish each page, confirm the message before going to the next page.

<!-- -->

- **Language for PowerPoint Handouts**

Introduce and distribute your handouts

- I’ve prepared some material to illustrate my presentation.

- You can take notes on this if you like.

**Go to the content page**

> Introduce the structure of your presentation.

- Please turn to page 2.

- This is an outline / overview of my presentation.
