#  10 Rules of Design for PowerPoint

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

- **Design Rules for PowerPoint**

  There are two key words for PowerPoint: Simple and Clear.

<!-- -->

- PowerPoint that is simple and clear will help deliver you message.

- Poorly designed PowerPoint will confuse and bore your audience. This will not help you deliver your message.

<!-- -->

- **Structure of PowerPoint Presentations**

  PowerPoint presentations should include the following four parts:

  1.  **Title Slide**

      Begin your PowerPoint presentation with a simple title slide with:

<!-- -->

- Title of your presentation.

- Date of the presentation

- Name of the organization or company you are presenting to.

- Your company’s name.

- Your name (optional).

  1.  **Index Slide**

      The index is a list of your main points.

- You will need an index if your PowerPoint has 5 or more pages of information.

- Use the page titles in the index.

- If your PowerPoint has 4 or fewer pages of information, a content page is probably not necessary.

  1.  **Content Slides**

      This is the main part of your PowerPoint. You will need one or two slides for each main idea.

- Follow the design rules below.

  1.  **Conclusion Slide**

      The last page is the Conclusion slide.

- The conclusion highlights the message of your presentation.

- See Unit 10 for Speech Model 2 with PowerPoint.

- **Check your PowerPoint!**

> Before presenting your PowerPoint, check it carefully. Look at it as your audience will look at it.

- Projected PowerPoint: project your PowerPoint on a wall screen. Don’t just look at it on the computer screen. Computer color and projected color do not look the same.

- PowerPoint handout: print your PowerPoint to check the color and layout.

- Check these points:

<!-- -->

- Are the colors attractive, clear, and easy to look at?

- Does the text look balanced on the page?

- Can you see all the words clearly?

- Is it easy to see the main points?

- Will your audience understand them easily?

- Check the spelling and punctuation.

- Check the margins, indentation, and spacing.

<td><p><u><strong>2. Sans-serif Fonts</strong> - <strong>Aa Bb Cc</strong></u></p>
<ul>
<li><p><em>Sans</em> is French for <em>without</em>. Sans-serif fonts do not have the little feet at the end of the stroke.</p></li>
<li><p>Sans-serif fonts are used for posters, titles, and short phrases - they are not as easy to read on paper.</p></li>
<li><p>Sans-serif fonts are easier to read on computers and Websites and are commonly used for e-mail and texting.</p></li>
<li><p>Sans-serif fonts are informal and not suitable for business letters.</p></li>
</ul></td>
