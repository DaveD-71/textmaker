## Unit Role in the Course

Unit 1 starts the cumulative presentation portfolio. It teaches learners to plan from audience outcome rather than from the speaker's topic or available content.

The unit must stay role-agnostic between business-client and government-agency contexts. Use short neutral examples in the main body. Refer to the Process Improvement Briefing Models when learners need a business-client or government-agency variant.

## Learner Outcomes

By the end of this unit, learners can:

1. Identify the audience, occasion, and business purpose of a presentation.
2. State what the audience should know, decide, or do by the end.
3. Distinguish a presentation from a document, update, briefing, or informal explanation.

## Main Business Skill

Planning from audience outcome rather than speaker content.

Core planning question:

After this presentation, what should the audience know, decide, or do?

## Presentation English Focus

Required language:
- "The purpose of this presentation is to..."
- "By the end, I want the audience to..."
- "This matters because..."
- "The audience needs to decide whether..."
- "The audience already knows..."
- "The audience may be concerned about..."
- "This is a briefing/update/proposal because..."

Avoid:
- topic-only goals such as "I will talk about..."
- vague goals such as "I want to explain..."
- over-formal speech framing
- PowerPoint-specific framing

## Case/Model Use

Primary model set: Process Improvement Briefing Models

Use in Unit 1:
- short scenario brief only
- audience-purpose box
- no long role-specific model text in the main body

Business-client reference:
- Seika Global Trading Operations: reducing import document handoff delays

Government-agency reference:
- Midori Ward Administrative Services Office: reducing returned application forms

Main-text wording should say something like:

"For two versions of this planning problem, see the Process Improvement Briefing Models: one from a general trading-company operations team and one from an administrative services office."

## Required Learner Deliverable

Presentation brief.

Minimum fields:
- working topic
- audience
- occasion/context
- presentation type
- audience's current situation
- desired audience outcome
- why the presentation matters now
- one likely audience concern

## Core Tasks

1. Situation sorting
   Learners read three short workplace situations and decide whether each calls for a presentation, document, update, or informal explanation.

2. Outcome rewrite
   Learners rewrite topic-only goals into audience-outcome goals.

3. Audience-purpose brief
   Learners complete a one-paragraph presentation brief for their own presentation or a provided scenario.

4. Pair check
   Learners explain their audience outcome to a partner in 30-45 seconds and receive one clarification question.

## B1-B2 Scaffolding

Use:
- short scenarios
- controlled choice menus
- sentence frames
- one completed model
- one partially completed practice item
- one independent learner item

Do not require learners to invent complex business context from scratch before they understand the planning frame.

## Japanese-Learner Support

Keep support brief and practical.

Possible note:

Learners may first think through the situation in Japanese or another language if that helps, but the final presentation brief should be written in natural English. Do not translate a Japanese sentence word for word if the English becomes too long or indirect.

## AI Policy for Unit 1

No AI task is required in Unit 1.

If AI is mentioned at all, keep it to a teacher note:
- AI can produce generic audience descriptions.
- Learners must still decide the real audience, purpose, and outcome themselves.
- Do not use AI to replace the learner's own planning judgment.

## Assessment Link

## Teacher Notes Needed

Include notes on:
- why topic is not the same as purpose
- how to push vague goals toward audience outcome
- how to accept either business-client or government-agency learner contexts
- how to keep examples role-agnostic in the main unit

## Drafting Boundaries

Do not:
- write a generic motivational public-speaking introduction
- frame this as a PowerPoint unit
- make the main examples only banking/leasing or general trading-company or only government
- include a long model presentation
- introduce AI as a learner workflow
- overcomplicate the first unit with full case data

Do:
- make the course feel business-relevant from the first page
- give learners a usable planning tool
- keep the language practical and B1-B2 accessible
- make the first deliverable concrete
- set up later Units without overexplaining them
