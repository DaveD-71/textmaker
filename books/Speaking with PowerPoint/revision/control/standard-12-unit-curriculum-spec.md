# Standard 12-Unit Curriculum Spec

Prepared: 2026-08-12

Purpose: approval artifact for Plan 3 Production Workflow step 3. This spec locks the Standard course spine before rewriting units. Standard is the source of truth for Essentials and Long.

## Course Identity

Working series title: *Presentation Skills*

Course target: adult B1-B2 business English learners who need to present with visuals in international, in-person, online, hybrid, or short asynchronous workplace settings.

Course frame: business presentation skills, not PowerPoint training and not general public speaking. PowerPoint may appear as one common workplace tool, but the course teaches durable skills: audience, message, structure, visual thinking, evidence, delivery, interaction, and tool choice.

AI policy: AI is mentioned only as critical literacy, checking, critique, ethics, confidentiality, copyright, and limitation awareness. The course must not promote AI as a replacement for English language development, learner-owned message work, rehearsal, or final spoken performance.

Standard final delivery target: 5-7 minutes plus required Q&A, unless a client/teacher sets a different local requirement.

## Standard Course Outcomes

By the end of Standard, learners can:

1. Define the audience, business purpose, and intended outcome of a presentation.
2. Build a clear core message and relevant opening.
3. Organize content using flexible business structures.
4. Use natural business English to preview, transition, emphasize, summarize, recommend, and close.
5. Design readable, accessible visual support for a spoken message.
6. Explain simple business data and evidence clearly.
7. Choose appropriate tools, document roles, and backup formats for the presentation context.
8. Deliver in person, online, hybrid, or short recorded formats with intelligible voice and professional presence.
9. Handle Q&A with clarification, direct answers, deferral, and follow-up.
10. Deliver a final presentation with visuals and reflect on performance.

## Recurring Business Cases

Use three recurring cases across Units. These cases replace the old Ventura-only spine unless a specific old example is deliberately reused as historical contrast.

| Case | Purpose | Typical Audience | Main Teaching Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process improvement briefing | Recommend a workflow change | Manager or cross-functional team | Audience outcome, problem-solution structure, recommendation close |
| Product/service launch | Explain value and launch plan | Sales, clients, or internal stakeholders | Relevance, visual hierarchy, concise benefits, Q&A |
| Project results briefing | Report results and next steps | Leadership, project sponsors, or team | Data storytelling, evidence, status update, action close |

Each model must be traceable to language points, visual-design points, delivery behaviors, and assessment criteria.

Concrete model variants must follow `plan3-case-model-brief.md`: business-client examples should focus on banking/leasing or general trading-company operations, reporting, client-service, workflow, or control contexts; government-agency examples should focus on administrative service, process, reporting, coordination, or service-delivery contexts. Main unit explanations should remain role-agnostic.

## Unit Specs

### Unit 1: Audience, Purpose, and Business Context

Learner outcomes:
- Identify the audience, occasion, and business purpose of a presentation.
- State what the audience should know, decide, or do by the end.
- Distinguish a presentation from a document, update, briefing, or informal explanation.

Main business skill: planning from audience outcome rather than speaker content.

Presentation English focus: purpose statements, audience relevance, "By the end..." outcomes, basic context-setting language.

Model/case: process improvement briefing, initial scenario only.

Learner deliverable: presentation brief.

Core tasks:
- Analyze three short business situations and choose the real presentation purpose.
- Rewrite topic-only goals into audience-outcome goals.
- Draft a one-paragraph presentation brief.

Assessment link: message clarity and audience fit.

Essentials note: keep as a compact planning unit.

Long note: expand with stakeholder adaptation and competing audience needs.

### Unit 2: Message, Objective, and Relevance

Learner outcomes:
- Write a core message as a claim, not only a topic.
- Connect the message to audience relevance.
- Draft a short opening that gives context, purpose, and value.

Main business skill: turning a topic into a decision-ready message.

Presentation English focus: relevance statements, opening moves, purpose-versus-message language, cautious AI critique if used.

Model/case: process improvement briefing.

Learner deliverable: core message and opening.

Core tasks:
- Compare weak topics with strong messages.
- Test messages using "So what?" and "What should they do with this?"
- Improve a flawed opening.
- Optional AI-literacy task: critique a generic or risky AI-written opening and rewrite it in learner-owned English.
- Add a bilingual-planning note: learners may plan ideas efficiently, but the assessed message and opening must be drafted and rehearsed in natural learner-owned English.

Assessment link: message clarity, audience fit, opening effectiveness.

Essentials note: keep the message/opening sequence.

Long note: add executive relevance and stakeholder resistance.

### Unit 3: Structure and Flow

Learner outcomes:
- Choose a structure that fits the presentation purpose.
- Organize supporting points and evidence in a logical sequence.
- Use a planning map without forcing every presentation into three equal points.

Main business skill: selecting flexible structures for business communication.

Presentation English focus: previewing structure, moving between points, backward and forward references.

Model/case: process improvement briefing and project results briefing.

Learner deliverable: full presentation outline.

Core tasks:
- Match business purposes to structures: problem-solution, situation-analysis-recommendation, options-criteria-recommendation, project update, data story.
- Convert a weak list into a structured outline.
- Build a planning map with audience, outcome, message, evidence, and action.

Assessment link: organization and logic.

Essentials note: keep fewer structure options.

Long note: add executive-summary and decision-deck structures.

### Unit 4: Business English for Signposting

Learner outcomes:
- Use natural phrases to preview, transition, refer back, refer forward, emphasize, summarize, and close.
- Choose formal, neutral, or conversational business register.
- Avoid common false rules and translation traps.

Main business skill: helping listeners follow the argument in real time.

Presentation English focus: signposting, transitions, emphasis, summary, recommendation, action close, role/responsibility vocabulary.

Model/case: all three cases in short excerpts.

Learner deliverable: preview, transitions, and references for the learner's outline.

Core tasks:
- Replace stiff or outdated phrases with natural alternatives.
- Add signposting to a bare outline.
- Practice role/responsibility language: responsible for, manage, lead, take care of, in charge of.
- Japan-specific note: directness, articles/plurals in slide text, and avoiding overly scripted English.

Assessment link: spoken English, organization, professionalism.

Essentials note: keep a core phrase bank.

Long note: add register shifts for senior leaders, clients, and technical audiences.

### Unit 5: Clear Visual Communication

Learner outcomes:
- Design visual support around one main message.
- Improve readability through hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and concise text.
- Apply basic accessibility principles to slides or visual materials.

Main business skill: making visuals support understanding rather than decorate or duplicate speech.

Presentation English focus: introducing visuals, directing attention, describing layout, explaining what to notice.

Model/case: product/service launch.

Learner deliverable: accessible slide or visual draft.

Core tasks:
- Diagnose weak visual examples, including old 7x7-style slides.
- Rewrite slide titles as takeaway titles.
- Rebuild Sample A/B or equivalent readability comparison as editable text.
- Apply accessibility checklist: readable text, contrast, color not as the only signal, alt-text awareness, cognitive load.
- Optional AI-literacy task: critique a flawed AI-generated slide for generic language, factual risk, and readability.
- Explain the revised visual in 45-60 seconds using target language for introducing visuals and directing attention.
- Check slide text for article/plural problems, unclear noun phrases, and over-compressed English.

Assessment link: visual effectiveness and accessibility.

Essentials note: keep the core readability/accessibility checklist.

Long note: add advanced visual hierarchy and document-style visuals.

### Unit 6: Data, Charts, and Evidence

Learner outcomes:
- Choose an appropriate chart or evidence format for a simple business point.
- State the takeaway of a chart before explaining details.
- Explain trends, comparisons, and limits clearly.

Main business skill: turning evidence into a clear spoken business point.

Presentation English focus: trend language, comparison, approximation, source language, cautious claims.

Model/case: project results briefing.

Learner deliverable: one chart explanation.

Core tasks:
- Match data questions to chart types.
- Improve a cluttered or misleading chart.
- Practice explaining a chart in 60-90 seconds.
- Label fictional data clearly and cite real data where used.
- For banking/leasing or general trading-company examples, keep the task to workflow, reporting, evidence, service quality, or control escalation; do not create securities trading recommendations, securities-market predictions, client/account, shipment, or transaction examples, or legal or regulatory advice.
- Check chart titles and labels for article/plural problems and unclear compressed English.

Assessment link: evidence, visual effectiveness, spoken explanation.

Essentials note: keep one simple chart task.

Long note: expand into data storytelling, uncertainty, and decision-deck evidence.

### Unit 7: Tool-Neutral Slide and Document Workflow

Learner outcomes:
- Choose an appropriate visual/document format for the audience and setting.
- Decide the role of slides, notes, pre-reads, worksheets, leave-behinds, and backup materials.
- Prepare export, compatibility, and security checks.

Main business skill: choosing the right medium and workflow rather than defaulting to one software product.

Presentation English focus: explaining handouts, referring to documents, managing notes, explaining backup or follow-up materials.

Model/case: product/service launch and process improvement briefing.

Learner deliverable: visual pack plus notes.

Core tasks:
- Use a tool-choice checklist: audience, collaboration, data source, visual complexity, accessibility, export, confidentiality.
- Choose between slides, PDF, document walkthrough, dashboard, worksheet, leave-behind, and appendix.
- Prepare a backup/export checklist: approved cloud, offline copy, PDF fallback, fonts/media/links, venue/platform testing, company security.
- Discuss purposeful builds, transitions, audio, and video versus decorative effects.
- Justify one format choice in 45-60 seconds using target language for handouts, pre-reads, backup files, confidentiality, and follow-up materials.
- Use constrained choice menus and short scenarios in Standard. Reserve full multi-format comparison and complex workflow governance for Long.
- For banking/leasing or general trading-company examples, use fictional or sanitized workflow/reporting data only; do not use real customer names, account or transaction data, securities trading recommendations, securities-market predictions, or legal or regulatory advice.

Assessment link: professionalism, visual effectiveness, preparation quality.

Essentials note: compress to slides, notes, PDF fallback, and basic handout choice.

Long note: add collaboration, templates, governance, and complex document ecosystems.

### Unit 8: Delivery: Voice, Presence, Movement, and Notes

Learner outcomes:
- Use voice, pace, pauses, thought groups, and stress to improve intelligibility.
- Use notes without reading a script word for word.
- Present with natural posture, eye contact, gesture, movement, and pointer discipline.

Main business skill: delivering a planned message in a way listeners can follow.

Presentation English focus: chunking, stress, prominence, recovery phrases, prepared openings/transitions/closes.

Model/case: learner's own developing presentation.

Learner deliverable: rehearsed 3-minute segment.

Core tasks:
- Mark thought groups and emphasis in a short script.
- Convert a full script into usable speaker notes.
- Practice a 3-minute segment with peer feedback.
- Practice pointer/cursor choices: safe laser use, cursor highlight, annotation, zoom, and avoiding pointing at people.
- Include a brief Japan-specific intelligibility note where useful: katakana-influenced pronunciation risk, word stress, thought groups, and avoiding a flat read-aloud script.

Assessment link: spoken English, delivery, professionalism.

Essentials note: keep voice, notes, and basic rehearsal.

Long note: add room dynamics, executive presence, and difficult-room conditions.

### Unit 9: Online, Hybrid, and Async Delivery

Learner outcomes:
- Adapt a presentation for online or hybrid delivery.
- Use screen sharing, presenter notes, chat, captions, and remote Q&A appropriately.
- Prepare a short recorded or asynchronous version when needed.

Main business skill: adjusting delivery to platform, attention, and interaction constraints.

Presentation English focus: online housekeeping, checking understanding, managing chat/Q&A, recorded-presentation signposting.

Model/case: project results briefing.

Learner deliverable: adapted online or recorded version.

Core tasks:
- Convert an in-person plan into an online/hybrid checklist.
- Practice opening and transition language for online delivery.
- Plan camera, microphone, lighting, screen share, notes, chat, captions, timing, and contingency.
- Record or storyboard a short async version with captions or transcript awareness.
- Use constrained Standard tasks: learners adapt one chosen presentation mode, not every possible mode. Reserve full async production and multi-platform comparison for Long.

Assessment link: delivery, interaction, professionalism, accessibility.

Essentials note: mention online basics if space is limited.

Long note: expand into recorded presentation, async review, and hybrid facilitation.

### Unit 10: Q&A, Challenge Handling, and Interaction

Learner outcomes:
- Listen for the purpose behind a question.
- Clarify, answer directly, structure longer answers, defer appropriately, and follow up.
- Respond professionally to skepticism or disagreement.

Main business skill: keeping control of interaction without avoiding difficult questions.

Presentation English focus: clarification, direct answers, bridging, deferral, disagreement, checking satisfaction, follow-up.

Model/case: all three cases through Q&A scenarios.

Learner deliverable: Q&A response bank.

Core tasks:
- Classify common question types.
- Improve weak Q&A responses.
- Practice skeptical-question role plays.
- Build a personal Q&A response bank for the final presentation.
- Practice polite direct disagreement and safe deferral phrases, including cases where the presenter lacks a number, needs policy confirmation, or must follow up after checking with another team.
- For banking/leasing or general trading-company examples, avoid investment advice, securities-market predictions, client/account, shipment, or transaction details, and legal or regulatory interpretation.

Assessment link: Q&A, spoken English, professionalism.

Essentials note: keep core Q&A phrases and short role play.

Long note: add stakeholder challenge, hostile questions, and facilitation moves.

### Unit 11: Final Rehearsal and Peer Feedback

Learner outcomes:
- Rehearse the full presentation using visuals and notes.
- Give and receive focused peer feedback.
- Revise message, visuals, language, delivery, and Q&A plan.

Main business skill: using rehearsal as a repair process, not only repetition.

Presentation English focus: peer-feedback language, self-correction, revision language, timing language.

Model/case: learner's own final presentation.

Learner deliverable: revised full presentation.

Core tasks:
- Revisit the Unit 1 presentation history inventory and personal goal.
- Rehearse with timing and peer checklist.
- Revise slides/visuals, opening, transitions, data explanation, and Q&A bank.
- Separate peer feedback from formal assessment.

Assessment link: all rubric categories except final live performance.

Essentials note: one focused rehearsal cycle.

Long note: add second rehearsal cycle and instructor conference.

### Unit 12: Final Presentation and Reflection

Learner outcomes:
- Deliver a business presentation with visuals.
- Handle Q&A using prepared strategies and learner-owned English.
- Reflect on strengths, weaknesses, and next development steps.

Main business skill: delivering a complete business presentation and evaluating performance.

Presentation English focus: final opening, transitions, recommendation/action close, Q&A, reflection language.

Model/case: learner's own final presentation.

Learner deliverable: delivered presentation plus self-review.

Core tasks:
- Deliver a 5-7 minute final presentation with required Q&A, unless a client/teacher sets a different local requirement.
- Submit visual pack or equivalent presentation materials.
- Complete self-review using the final rubric.
- Identify one next-step language goal and one next-step presentation-skill goal.

Assessment link: final rubric: message clarity, audience fit, structure, evidence, visual effectiveness, spoken English, delivery, Q&A, timing, professionalism, accessibility.

Essentials note: shorter final presentation and simpler rubric.

Long note: second assessed cycle or expanded final presentation with stakeholder scenario.

## Assessment Model

Standard final assessment must include:

| Category | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Message clarity | Core message, opening, final delivery |
| Audience fit | Presentation brief, relevance statements, examples |
| Structure | Outline, signposting, final flow |
| Evidence | Data/chart explanation, source or fictional-data label |
| Visual effectiveness | Visual pack, accessibility checklist |
| Spoken English | Intelligibility, signposting, register, Q&A language |
| Delivery | Voice, pace, notes, posture, eye contact, movement or online presence |
| Q&A | Required Q&A performance and response quality |
| Professionalism | Timing, preparation, document roles, backup awareness |
| Reflection | Self-review and next-step goals |

Peer feedback is formative. Formal assessment is based on the final presentation, required Q&A, submitted visual pack/materials, and self-review.

## Essentials and Long Adaptation Rules

Essentials:
- Keep the minimum credible 2026 course: audience, message, structure, signposting, clear visuals, basic data explanation, delivery, Q&A, final presentation.
- Compress or omit advanced async delivery, complex document roles, deeper data critique, and extended rehearsal.
- Use shorter readings, fewer options, and simpler tasks.

Long:
- Expand Standard without changing the core pedagogy.
- Add advanced stakeholder adaptation, executive-summary structures, decision decks, richer data storytelling, async presentation, facilitation, difficult Q&A, and a second assessed cycle.
- Keep terminology, cases, and rubric categories aligned with Standard.
