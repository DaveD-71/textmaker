# Process Improvement Business-Client Deck: Slide Text Plan

Source Model: `process-improvement-briefing-models.md`, Model 1.

Presentation Context: Seika Global Trading Operations, Yokohama Import Documentation Desk. The presenter asks for approval for a four-week pilot using a shared exception log and a fixed 2:15 p.m. handoff checkpoint before the 3:00 p.m. shipping-document preparation cutoff.

Design Style: Restrained operations-control briefing. Use a clean PowerPoint-native business template, white or very light background, dark blue/charcoal text, and one muted accent. Keep all shapes editable. Do not use generated text images.

## Slide 1: Approve a Four-Week Workflow Pilot

On-Slide Composition:

Today, we need to agree on three points:

1. Approve a shared exception log pilot.
2. Confirm the 2:15 p.m. checkpoint owner.
3. Agree to review late document handoffs and average rework time after four weeks.

Emphasis:

- The audience must make a clear approval decision.
- The pilot is limited and controlled.

Visual Direction:

- Use a title-and-content layout with the lead sentence at the top.
- Use a numbered list because the slide presents three required decisions.
- Avoid standalone fragment labels; use the lead sentence to carry the slide purpose.

## Slide 2: Handoff Information Is Spread Across Three Places

On-Slide Composition:

A Supplier A packing-list issue can move through three separate channels:

1. Email
2. Spreadsheet
3. Handoff message

Result:

- Ownership is unclear before the 3:00 p.m. cutoff.

Emphasis:

- The problem is scattered ownership, not careless staff.
- The cutoff makes timing important.

Visual Direction:

- Use a three-step or three-column current-state layout.
- Place `Ownership unclear before 3:00 p.m. cutoff` as the outcome after the three channels.
- Keep the Supplier A detail small; the slide is about the structure of the problem.

## Slide 3: Most Late Handoffs Involve Unclear Notes

On-Slide Composition:

Last month, the team saw:

- 38 late document handoffs
- 61% linked to unclear handoff notes
- 22 minutes average rework time per exception

Note:

- Fictional data for practice

Emphasis:

- The repeated problem is large enough to justify a pilot.
- This evidence supports a limited workflow change, not a full system change.

Visual Direction:

- Use a three-number evidence panel.
- Make `61%` the largest number.
- Keep the fictional-data note small but readable.

## Slide 4: One Shared Log Makes Ownership Visible Earlier

On-Slide Composition:

The pilot changes the handoff point:

- Shared exception log
  - Issue
  - Owner
  - Next action
- Fixed 2:15 p.m. checkpoint
- Control process unchanged

Emphasis:

- The recommendation changes where and when ownership is confirmed.
- The control process does not change.

Visual Direction:

- Use a before/after workflow layout.
- Current side: Scattered notes.
- Pilot side: Shared log plus checkpoint.
- Do not make the log look like a dense spreadsheet.

## Slide 5: The Pilot Is Short, Owned, and Measurable

On-Slide Composition:

Pilot schedule:

1. Week 0
   - Control review of log fields
2. Weeks 1-4
   - Daily 2:15 p.m. checkpoint
3. End Review
   - Late document handoffs
   - Average rework time
   - Ownership feedback

Target:

- 25% fewer late document handoffs

Emphasis:

- The pilot has an owner and review measures.
- The target is a test point, not a promise.

Visual Direction:

- Use a timeline layout.
- Use numbered chronology because the slide follows time.
- Add the target as a small callout, not the dominant message.

## Slide 6: Approve the Pilot and Confirm Ownership

On-Slide Composition:

If we agree today, we can start next Monday:

1. Approve the four-week pilot.
2. Confirm the documentation lead as checkpoint owner.
3. Review late handoffs and rework time at the end of the pilot.

Emphasis:

- The close returns to the exact decision.
- The next action is immediate and practical.

Visual Direction:

- Use a decision-summary layout.
- Use a numbered list because the slide closes with three action items.
- Do not introduce new evidence on this slide.
