# Model Deck Message Audit (2026-10-02)

Purpose: before building the six appendix model decks as a set of *different slide styles and approaches*, check that the scripts and slide text (a) reflect the textbook's learning points, (b) match each deck's intended style, (c) put the key message words on the slides, and (d) are not all the same presentation.

Scope read: Units 3, 5, 6 in full; all three appendix script files; all six `assets/model-slide-text/*.md` plans; `standard-12-unit-curriculum-spec.md` for Units 1, 2, 4, 7, 9, 10, 12 (outcomes only, not the full unit drafts).

Status: **audit and proposal only. No script, slide-text plan, or unit has been edited.** Canva trial decks were generated from the old plans and are to be deleted by the user.

## 1. Learning points the models should carry

| Unit | Learning point the models are tagged to show |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Audience outcome; core message as a claim; value/relevance opening |
| 3 | Pick the structure that fits the purpose. Five are taught: problem-cause-solution-action; situation-options-criteria-recommendation; objective-status-issue-next step; question-evidence-meaning-implication; need-value-plan-next step. "No rule that every presentation needs exactly three points." |
| 4 | Signposting: preview, transition, refer back/forward, emphasis, summary, action close |
| 5 | One visual, one message; **takeaway titles** (sentence-style capitalization); hierarchy; concise text; accessibility; explain the visual, do not read it |
| 6 | Takeaway before detail; simplest chart; careful claims; label fictional data; do not read every number |
| 7, 9, 10, 12 | Document roles and backup; online/recorded adaptation; Q&A strategies; complete final presentation |

## 2. Findings: scripts against learning points

1. **Unit 3 teaches five structures; the six scripts model effectively one.** Every script is: opening that states the decision, then problem, evidence, recommendation, benefit, plan, then close that restates the same decision. The results scripts vary the middle (objective, results, learning) but still open and close on an approval request.
2. **Missing structure: situation-options-criteria-recommendation.** No appendix model demonstrates it. Unit 3 only has a 4-row mini example, and the QA checklist already carries this as a tracked gap.
3. **Missing structure: objective-status-issue-next step (progress update).** Taught in Unit 3, not modeled.
4. **Decision-ask repetition is built in.** The ask is stated in the opening, again in the close, and a third time in the Q&A "What do you need from us?" answer. Useful for teaching a firm close, but it makes every deck feel identical.
5. **Unit 9 (online/recorded adaptation)** is only listed as a skill link on the results models; no model shows a different version for online delivery.
6. **Script/slide inconsistencies to fix:**
   - Process-government script says the checklist covers "the income certificate attachment, the contact field, the ID field, and one confirmation item". The slide plan and the results-government script both list **signature field**.
   - Launch-business script states a 30 percent reduction target in repeated status-check messages. The slide plan's rollout slide omits it (the other three pilot decks put their target on the schedule slide).
   - Results-government script says attachment-related returns fell "about 24%". 77 to 58 is 24.7%, so "about 25%" is the accurate figure.
   - Results-business script and Q&A use "about 29%" for 38 to 27 (28.9%, correct); the slide plan correctly omits it.
7. **Unit 5 conflict:** Unit 5 teaches sentence-style capitalization for visual titles. All slide-text plans use Title Case.

## 3. Findings: slide key words against message

- Several slide titles are labels or restated lists, not takeaway messages, against Unit 5. Examples: "Confirm Users, Access Plan, and Feedback Process"; "Approve Schedule, Staffing, and Review Measures"; "Approve the Pilot and Confirm Ownership".
- Nine of 36 titles start with "Approve"; several more use Confirm/Expand/Launch/Assign. The title states the *ask*, not the *reason*.
- The strongest message phrases in the scripts are under-weighted on slides:
  - Process-business: "without changing the control process" appears only as a small line on slide 4.
  - Launch-government: "before submission" is the whole idea; it appears in two titles but the "desk does not decide" limits are body text.
  - Results-business: "positive, but not full rollout" is a lead sentence, good, but not repeated as a title.
- High-frequency words across the slide text (on-slide composition only): review 20, pilot 19, week 12, staff 12, month 12, owner 11, repeat 10, fewer 10. Titles reuse frames: "The Pilot Creates Three Practical/Service Benefits" (twice); "...Both Fell" (twice).
- Lead-in sentences in the plans ("Today, we need to agree on three points:") carry most of the message but are the first thing a generator drops.

## 4. Findings: structural sameness

Slide-level pattern across all six decks:

| Slide | Pattern | Decks following it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approval/decision request with numbered list | 6 of 6 |
| 2 | Problem or objective | 6 of 6 |
| 3 | Evidence panel (large numbers or paired bars) | 5 of 6 |
| 4 | Change/solution explained | 6 of 6 |
| 5 | Timeline, schedule or recommendation list | 6 of 6 |
| 6 | Restated decision as numbered list | 6 of 6 |

Numbered lists are the main device in all six. Four of the six carry a "target %" (25, 20, 30, 15). The result is one presentation recoloured six times. Different Canva styles cannot fix this: the *message architecture* has to differ first.

## 5. Proposed approach per deck

Principle: each deck uses a different Unit 3 structure, a different opening move and a different close, then a visual style that suits that approach. Data and fictional organizations are kept; only structure, emphasis and phrasing change.

| # | Deck | Unit 3 structure | Opening move | Close | Style |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Process, Business (Seika) | Problem - cause - solution - action | A concrete incident (Supplier A packing list at 2:45 p.m.), then the pattern | Decision request | Soft cards |
| 2 | Process, Government (Midori Ward) | **Situation - options - criteria - recommendation** (fills the gap) | A question: which of three ways to cut returned forms? | Recommendation justified against criteria | High-contrast checklist and criteria matrix |
| 3 | Launch, Business (Koyo) | Need - value - plan - next step | **Show the dashboard first**, then explain | Dated next steps (Today/Friday/Monday), not an approval | Dark dashboard |
| 4 | Launch, Government (Aoba City) | Need - value - plan - next step, told as a **resident journey** | A resident's moment of difficulty | Reassurance on what does not change, then approval | Service flow |
| 5 | Results, Business (Seika results) | **Question - evidence - meaning - implication** | **Answer first**: "positive, but not full rollout" | Conditional recommendation (two controls) | Data-first editorial |
| 6 | Results, Government (Midori results) | **Objective - status - issue - next step** | Scorecard snapshot | Watch-list: waiting time, next review date | Assertion and evidence |

Deck 2 is the only one that needs new content: two or three alternative options and three criteria for the Midori Ward case. The chosen option stays the front-counter checklist and FAQ, so the numbers do not change.
