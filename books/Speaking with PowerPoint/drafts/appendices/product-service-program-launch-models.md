# Product, Service, or Program Launch Models

These models show how to explain the value, plan, and next steps for a new product, service, program, or internal support process. Use them when you need a launch presentation that helps the audience understand what is changing, why it matters, and what action is needed next.

Both examples use fictional organizations and fictional practice data. Do not copy the names, figures, or situations into a real workplace presentation.

## Model 1: Business-client launch

### Scenario brief

Koyo Markets Client Services is preparing a pilot launch of a client reporting dashboard. The dashboard will give relationship managers one weekly view of client inquiry status, reporting deadlines, and unresolved reporting requests.

The dashboard is not a trading tool and does not recommend any trade, product, or market action. It is an internal service-support tool for reporting workflow and client-service coordination.

### Audience and purpose

Audience:
- relationship managers
- client-service staff
- reporting operations
- team leadership

Purpose: secure agreement for a pilot launch with two client teams.

Audience decision or action: nominate pilot users, confirm the launch date, and agree how feedback will be collected.

### Core message

The dashboard will help relationship managers see reporting issues earlier, reduce repeated status-check messages, and give each team a clearer view of ownership before client deadlines.

### Outline

1. Why now: status checks increase near reporting deadlines.
2. What changes: one dashboard view shows inquiry status, deadlines, and open requests.
3. How it helps: earlier visibility, fewer repeated messages, and clearer ownership.
4. Rollout: two-team pilot, weekly feedback, and a revised dashboard after four weeks.
5. Next step: nominate pilot users and confirm the launch date.

### Delivery context profile

Format: internal launch briefing.

Timing: 7 minutes plus 5 minutes for Q&A.

Setting: hybrid team meeting with some relationship managers joining online.

Required materials:
- dashboard mockup with fictional client names only
- rollout timeline
- one-page pilot user guide as a leave-behind

Backup materials:
- data-field list
- feedback form
- issue-escalation contact list

Confidentiality check: use sanitized examples only. Do not show real client names, account numbers, trade details, ticket IDs, or proprietary system screens.

### Sample opening

Good morning. Today I would like to introduce the pilot version of our client reporting dashboard.

The reason for this pilot is simple. In the last reporting cycle, many status-check messages came near the deadline, and several teams were asking for information that another team already had. This created extra messages and made ownership less clear.

The dashboard will not replace our official reports. It will give relationship managers an earlier weekly view of inquiry status, reporting deadlines, and unresolved requests. By the end of this briefing, I would like us to confirm the two pilot teams, agree the launch date, and decide how we will collect feedback during the first four weeks.

### Visual and document artifact

Use a dashboard mockup and a rollout timeline. The mockup should show visual hierarchy: the most important status information appears first, supporting details appear below it, and action items are clearly separated from general information.

Fictional practice data for the launch rationale:

| Item from last reporting cycle | Fictional figure for practice |
|---|---:|
| Status-check messages | 74 |
| Messages asking for information held by another team | 46% |
| Estimated staff time spent answering repeated status checks | 9 hours per week |
| Pilot target | 30% fewer repeated status-check messages |

Dashboard mockup content:

| Area | Purpose | Example label |
|---|---|---|
| Top status bar | Show the main current position | `Reporting cycle: Week 2 / 4` |
| Client team list | Show which teams need attention | `Client Team A`, `Client Team B` |
| Request status | Show open, waiting, and completed requests | `Open: 6`, `Waiting: 3`, `Complete: 18` |
| Deadline panel | Show upcoming reporting dates | `Next deadline: Friday 15:00` |
| Owner column | Show who should act next | `Owner: Reporting Ops` |

Rollout timeline:

| Week | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | Confirm pilot users and access | pilot user list |
| Week 1 | Launch dashboard and collect first issues | issue log |
| Week 2 | Review repeated questions | dashboard adjustment list |
| Week 3 | Check owner and deadline fields | revised field labels |
| Week 4 | Review pilot and recommend next step | launch decision note |

Document-role choices:
- Pre-read: one-page summary of the dashboard purpose and pilot schedule.
- Live visual: dashboard mockup and rollout timeline.
- Leave-behind: pilot user guide with support contact and feedback form.
- Backup material: field list and confidentiality rules, used only if someone asks.

### Language-analysis excerpt

> The dashboard will not replace our official reports. It will give relationship managers an earlier weekly view of inquiry status, reporting deadlines, and unresolved requests.

This excerpt does two useful things. First, it removes a possible misunderstanding: the dashboard is not replacing official reports. Second, it states the value in audience terms: relationship managers will see key information earlier.

Useful phrases:
- `This will not replace...`
- `It will give you a clearer view of...`
- `The main benefit is...`
- `The next step is to confirm...`
- `During the pilot, we will check...`

### Q&A pairs

| Function | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| Answer directly | Will this replace the existing reports? | No. It will not replace the official reports. It is a weekly status view that helps teams see open items earlier. |
| Clarify material role | Is the dashboard for clients or only for internal teams? | It is for internal teams only during this pilot. If we later share any client-facing view, we will need a separate approval process. |
| Acknowledge limitation | What if different teams use different reporting cycles? | That is one limitation of the first version. For the pilot, we will include only two teams with similar reporting cycles, then check what changes are needed before wider use. |
| Bridge to launch rationale | Why do we need a dashboard instead of more email updates? | The issue is not only the number of updates. The issue is that ownership and status are hard to see across teams. The dashboard gives one shared view before the deadline. |
| Defer safely | Can client names appear in the dashboard later? | I cannot confirm that today. For the pilot, we will use sanitized names only. I will check the data and confidentiality requirements before any wider launch. |
| Confirm follow-up | Who updates the dashboard after launch? | Reporting operations will update the status fields each week. I will send the owner list after this meeting and include it in the pilot guide. |

### Unit skills demonstrated

| Unit | Skill demonstrated in this model |
|---|---|
| Unit 2 | value-focused opening, audience relevance, action request |
| Unit 4 | launch-plan transitions, contrast between what changes and what does not change, next-step language |
| Unit 5 | dashboard visual hierarchy, readable labels, explanation of what to notice |
| Unit 7 | pre-read, live visual, leave-behind, backup material, confidentiality check |
| Unit 10 | adoption-limit Q&A, direct answers, safe deferral, follow-up |
| Unit 12 | final-presentation purpose: explain a workplace change and request a clear next action |

## Model 2: Government-agency launch

### Scenario brief

Aoba City Administrative Support Center is preparing a three-month pilot of an application support desk. The support desk will help residents complete online forms before submission, especially when they have questions about attachments or required fields.

The presentation is about administrative service delivery. It does not argue for a political position, legislation, budget campaign, or policy change.

### Audience and purpose

Audience:
- administrative managers
- counter-service staff
- call-center staff
- service-planning team

Purpose: gain approval for a three-month pilot of the support desk.

Audience decision or action: approve the pilot schedule, confirm staffing, and agree what service data will be reviewed after three months.

### Core message

A small application support desk will reduce incomplete submissions and make the online form process easier for residents by giving practical help before they submit a form.

### Outline

1. Why now: incomplete online forms and repeat inquiries are increasing.
2. What changes: the support desk helps users before submission.
3. How it helps: fewer errors, fewer repeat visits, and clearer guidance.
4. Rollout: limited hours, trained staff, weekly issue tracking, and a three-month review.
5. Next step: approve the pilot schedule and staffing plan.

### Delivery context profile

Format: internal program-launch briefing.

Timing: 7 minutes plus 5 minutes for Q&A.

Setting: in-person meeting with managers and staff representatives.

Required materials:
- service-flow diagram
- pilot timeline
- short staff guidance sheet

Backup materials:
- sample inquiry categories
- draft staffing schedule
- accessibility and plain-language check notes

Accessibility check: the service affects public-facing communication, so instructions should be plain, readable, and usable for residents with different levels of confidence using online forms.

### Sample opening

Good afternoon. Today I will explain the proposed pilot for an application support desk.

The reason for this proposal is that more residents are starting online forms but still need help before they can submit them correctly. When a form is incomplete, staff need to answer another inquiry, and the resident may need to visit or call again.

The support desk gives practical help before submission. It does not change the application rules. It helps users understand the form, prepare the required attachments, and avoid common entry errors. By the end of this briefing, I would like us to approve the pilot schedule, confirm the staffing plan, and agree what we will measure after three months.

### Visual and document artifact

Use a service-flow diagram and a pilot timeline. The service-flow diagram should make the new support point easy to see. The timeline should show when staff training, pilot launch, weekly issue review, and the three-month review happen.

Fictional practice data for the launch rationale:

| Item from last month | Fictional figure for practice |
|---|---:|
| Online form inquiries | 312 |
| Inquiries about attachments or field entry | 47% |
| Inquiries that led to a repeat visit or follow-up call | 28% |
| Pilot target | 15% fewer repeat inquiries |

Service-flow diagram content:

| Step | Current process | Pilot process |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | User opens online form | User opens online form |
| 2 | User completes form alone | User can ask support desk before submission |
| 3 | User submits form | User submits form after checking attachments and fields |
| 4 | Staff review form | Staff review form |
| 5 | Incomplete forms are returned | Fewer preventable incomplete forms are expected |

Pilot timeline:

| Month | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Before launch | Train assigned staff and prepare guidance sheet | staff guidance sheet |
| Month 1 | Open support desk during limited hours | inquiry log |
| Month 2 | Review common questions and adjust guidance | revised guidance sheet |
| Month 3 | Review repeat inquiries and staff workload | pilot review note |

Document-role choices:
- Pre-read: one-page pilot proposal for managers.
- Live visual: service-flow diagram and pilot timeline.
- Leave-behind: staff guidance sheet and inquiry-log categories.
- Backup material: draft staffing schedule and plain-language notes.

### Language-analysis excerpt

> The support desk gives practical help before submission. It does not change the application rules. It helps users understand the form, prepare the required attachments, and avoid common entry errors.

This excerpt explains the service value and sets a clear limit. The audience learns what the support desk will do, but also what it will not do.

Useful phrases:
- `The purpose of the pilot is...`
- `This does not change...`
- `It helps users...`
- `The first step is...`
- `After three months, we will review...`

### Q&A pairs

| Function | Question | Model answer |
|---|---|---|
| Answer directly | Will this reduce counter capacity? | It may use some staff time, so the pilot starts with limited hours. We will check staff workload each week before making any wider recommendation. |
| Clarify service scope | Will staff complete the forms for residents? | No. Staff will explain the form and check common problem points, but the resident remains responsible for the submitted information. |
| Acknowledge limitation | What about residents who do not use online forms? | The support desk is only for the online form process. Counter and phone support will continue, and we will track whether the desk changes repeat inquiries from those channels. |
| Bridge to launch rationale | Why not improve the form first instead of adding a desk? | Form improvement is still important, but it will take longer. The support desk gives immediate help and also shows which parts of the form create the most questions. |
| Defer safely | Can we use the same support desk for other application types? | Possibly, but I do not want to assume that today. After the three-month review, we can decide which application types are suitable for expansion. |
| Confirm follow-up | What will we measure after the pilot? | We will review repeat inquiries, common question categories, staff workload, and user comments. I will include these measures in the pilot review note. |

## Comparing the two launch models

The two models teach the same launch-presentation skill, but the workplace details are different.

| Shared teaching point | Business-client model | Government-agency model |
|---|---|---|
| Value-focused opening | reduces repeated status checks before reporting deadlines | reduces incomplete submissions and repeat inquiries |
| Audience relevance | relationship managers need earlier visibility and clearer ownership | staff and managers need a practical support process |
| Visual hierarchy | dashboard mockup puts status, deadlines, and owners first | service-flow diagram makes the support point visible |
| Rollout timeline | four-week two-team pilot | three-month limited-hours pilot |
| Document roles | user guide, field list, feedback form | staff guidance sheet, inquiry categories, staffing schedule |
| Adoption-limit Q&A | dashboard scope, data confidentiality, update ownership | service scope, staff workload, user access |
| Next-step language | nominate users and confirm launch date | approve schedule and staffing plan |
