# 14 Handling Questions

> *Language & strategies*

<img src="assets/media/image20.png" style="width:2.6in;height:1.46806in" />

- **Preparation**

> As a speaker, the best technique for handling questions is to be prepared.

- When preparing your presentation don’t include every detail or example. Keep some information to use in the Q&A session.

- Think about your audience and the questions they might ask. Think about the easy questions and the difficult questions.

<!-- -->

- **During Q&A**

<!-- -->

- Listen to the whole question. Don’t cut the speaker off before they finish asking the question even if you understand it.

- Take notes as you listen to the question.

- Look at the speaker as they ask the question. It shows you are paying attention and it is easier to respond.

- Pause slightly before answering to gather your thoughts. But don’t pause too long: don’t keep the audience waiting.

- Learn and practice gap filler expressions. They will give you time to think and start your answer. For example:

<!-- -->

- *That’s an interesting question.*

- *Thank you for the question.*

- *I’m glad you asked that question.*

<!-- -->

- Keep your answers short and specific.

- Begin your response with the key words from the question. It makes an easy introduction, gives you time to think, and connects your answer to the question.

- Answer with key words from your presentation.

- If you repeat information from your presentation in your answer, say so.

<!-- -->

- *As I said in my presentation …*

<!-- -->

- Conclude your answer with –

<!-- -->

- *Does that answer your question?*

- *Is that the information you were looking for?*

<!-- -->

- **Dealing with Difficult Questions**

<!-- -->

- Only respond to questions you understand. If you think you don’t understand, always ask for clarification.

<!-- -->

- *I’m sorry, but could you rephrase your question please?*

- *I’m sorry, but could you repeat your question slowly please?*

- *I’m sorry, but I didn’t catch what you said. Could you … please?*

<!-- -->

- Respond honestly. If you don’t have the answer to a question or can’t respond, say so right away.

> You have to make a quick decision: can you answer the question or not? If the answer is “no”, then don’t try: it’s a waste of time. Just say -

- *I’m sorry, but I don’t have that information. But I will get it for you and send it to you after the conference.*

<!-- -->

- Don’t include irrelevant information in your answer. It annoys people and wastes time.

- If the question is outside the scope of your presentation, give an answer if you are able to: this is the best response.

> However, if you are not able to give a good answer, don’t try. Just say -

- *I’m sorry, but that question is outside the scope of my presentation and I don’t feel I can give a satisfactory response at this time.*

<!-- -->

- If the questioner seems hostile towards your presentation and you have difficulty responding you can say –

<!-- -->

- *I’m sorry; it’s difficult for me to answer your question. Could we meet after the session to discuss it?*

<!-- -->

- If the questioner persists in demanding an answer simply reconfirm your response:

<!-- -->

- *As I said, it’s difficult for me to answer your question. I can meet you or I can send you a response after the conference. But now we should take a question from someone else.*
