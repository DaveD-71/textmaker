- **Small Group Meetings**

> Small group meetings follow the same 6-step procedure described in Unit 2: Meeting Structure, however, the tone and language of small group meetings tend to be more informal:

1.  Plenary sessions are tend to be very formal because they are usually large meetings and participants include people at senior as well as more junior levels.

2.  The participants in small group meetings are peers - they are at the same level of experience and authority so they can freely discuss the issues and talk to each other as equals.

3.  Small group meetings have a smaller number of participants which encourages informality and open discussion.

    - **Roundtable Discussions**

> Roundtable discussions are for an exchange of ideas, discussion, and questions and answers.

- The discussion usually begins with a short presentation from one or more of the participants followed by general discussion and Q&A.

- There are no decisions made in roundtable discussions.

  - **Meeting Practice Transcript**

> Read the meeting transcript below. Take note of the language and how the chairperson follows the meeting structure.

- **Language**: Much of the language of meetings consists of fixed phrases – expressions that everyone uses. Identify new expressions and practice them so that you can use them easily without the text.

- **Practice:** Take turns chairing meeting simulations with the transcript. Use the language carefully. All the students will chair, report, and ask questions.
