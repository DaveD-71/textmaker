# 9 Handling Difficult Situations

> *Strategies & language*

- **The Key Player**

> In any meeting, the key player is the chairperson.

- The chairperson is responsible for conducting the meeting as planned and for the purpose it was intended.

- The chairperson is like the captain of a ship. The captain is responsible for the total operation of the ship: handling all situations during a voyage, charting the course, and bringing the ship safely into port.

- In the same way, the chairperson of a meeting is responsible for handing all situations during the meeting, both planned and unplanned, and making the meeting successful.

- When the participants see that the chairperson is in control of the meeting they will tend to cooperate with the chairperson.

- If the chairperson seems poorly prepared or is having a difficult time, the participants tend to be less cooperative and difficult situations could arise.

  - **Before the meeting**

> The successful meeting begins with preparation.

- Be totally prepared: know everything about the meeting including who the attendees and participants are.

- Make sure everything is ready.

- Being well-prepared is the basis for making a successful meeting.

  - **At the beginning of the meeting**

> Make a good start.

- Take control: establish yourself as the boss of the meeting.

- Maintain a confident and respectful tone. The attendees should know that you are conducting the meeting for everyone.

- Make sure everyone knows what the ground rules are and why they exist: people tend to cooperate when they understand the reason for rules.

- Very clearly review the agenda, the speaking protocol, and time limits for speaking, asking questions, and answering questions.

- Explain that if necessary you will remind speakers of time limits and ask them to finish up.

  - **During the meeting**

> Demonstrate that you are in control of the meeting by your body language, your behaviour, and your language.

- **Your Body Language: Position, posture, eye contact, & voice**

<!-- -->

- Be in a position where you can see everyone and everyone can see you at all times.

- If you are standing – stand up straight.

- If you are sitting – sit up straight and lean slightly into the table.

- Always be looking at either the speaker or the audience. Never look down or away or seem to be inattentive. Don’t get distracted with your notes or note taking.

- During the discussion make eye contact with people who want to ask questions so they know you have seen them and that you will call on them.

- Always speak in a strong, clear, pleasant voice. Never too quickly.

- Maintain a pleasant look on your face – smile!

<!-- -->

- **Your Behaviour**

<!-- -->

- Be patient, confident, and courteous.

- Apply the ground rules to everyone strictly and fairly from the beginning – no exceptions.

- When you enforce the ground rules, be respectful and polite. Remind the participants that you are enforcing the rules for everyone’s benefit.

- During Q&A control the flow of questions by calling on people who want to ask questions.

<!-- -->

- **Your Language**

<!-- -->

- Use courteous language with everyone at all times.

- Practice, memorize, and practice again the fixed expressions for all the situations until they are easy to use.

- Be ready to interrupt speakers if necessary with appropriate expressions.

  - **Language for handling difficult situations**

<!-- -->

- **The chair opens the meeting**

> … In our session this afternoon, we will have three reports. After each report there will be an opportunity for questions and a full discussion.
>
> I know that everyone here wants to participate in the discussion so we will have to manage our time well. I’d like to review the ground rules for our meeting.
>
> We have three hours scheduled for the meeting so each speaker’s time is limited to no more than 15 minutes. I will have to interrupt if people go overtime.
>
> I would like to ask you to keep your questions short and ask no more than two questions at a time.
>
> I would also like to ask the speakers to keep their responses as brief as possible. In this way everyone will have a chance to participate.
>
> To make sure everyone has a chance to ask questions please raise your hand and I will call on you in turn.
>
> Are there any questions about the procedure? … No? Then, thank you all for your cooperation.

- **The chair interrupt a speaker (Mr. Smith)**

<!-- -->

- Excuse me, Mr. Smith but could you <u>bring your remarks / response to a close</u>, please? We need to go to the next question. Thank you very much.

<!-- -->

- **The chair interrupt a difficult participant (Mr. Blake)**

<!-- -->

- Excuse me, Mr. Blake but <u>just two questions at a time</u>, please. Other people would like to ask questions. Thank you very much.

- Excuse me, Mr. Blake but would you please <u>let Mr. Smith respond to your question</u>? Thank you very much.

- Excuse me, Mr. Blake but <u>Mr. Smith has said he cannot answer your question at this time</u>. We need to move to the next question. Thank you very much.

- Excuse me, Mr. Blake. <u>Your point / question is very interesting but it is outside the purpose of this meeting</u>. We need to move to the next question. Thank you very much.
