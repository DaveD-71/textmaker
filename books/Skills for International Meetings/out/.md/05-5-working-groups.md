**Working Groups, Proposals and Decisions**

> Working groups deal with proposal and decisions on specific issues. Usually the decision of the working group is not final: it is usually sent to a more senior body for consideration and final approval.

- **Elements of a Decision**

> There are three essential elements to a decision: *Who* is doing *what* by *when*?

- If one of the elements is missing there is no effective decision and a follow up meeting will have to be held to clarify and complete the decision.

  - **Coming to a Decision**

> There are three basic ways to come to a decision in a meeting. Good decisions can be made only after exchanging and discussing differing ideas.

1.  **Consensus:** All participants agree on one idea.

- Simply agreeing with the first speaker in order to avoid conflict or save time is not a consensus. There must be a full discussion.

- Deciding by consensus maximizes the support for the decision and assures the decision will be carried out.

  1.  **Majority:** Most (not all) participants agree on one idea.

- To establish a majority position a vote must be carried out, usually by a show of hands for and against. The chairperson must announce the number of votes for and against.

- A majority vote is one way of overcoming a deadlock when consensus cannot be achieved.

  1.  **Authority:** One person in authority makes the decision.

- The person in authority will listen to the discussion of differing ideas and then make a decision.

- The authority can stop the discussion at any time even before all the ideas have been presented.

- **Guiding a discussion toward a decision**

The Chair can guide the discussion in various ways:

- Begin the discussion by clearly stating the issue to be discussed:

<!-- -->

- Keep the discussion focused on the issue.

- Set aside off-issue comments.

<!-- -->

- Make sure all the background information is presented before discussion begins. This is a critical first step.

- Encourage discussion to find common points and points of disagreement. Make sure all the participants have an opportunity to speak.

- Summarize the points from the discussion:

<!-- -->

- Identify the points agreed upon.

- Identify the points of disagreement.

<!-- -->

- When agreement seems to appear, restate it to the participants for their attention and confirmation:

<!-- -->

- In your restatement, include all the details and ask for confirmation from all the participants.

- The note taker will record your restatement.

<!-- -->

- If there is no clear agreement, clearly state the two positions:

<!-- -->

- Emphasize the points of agreement and clearly state the differences.

- Ask the participants to focus on these options.

- Allow for new proposals that will overcome the deadlock.

<!-- -->

- If no consensus appears, suggest…

<!-- -->

- Making a decision by majority vote.

- Leaving the issue undecided and scheduling a new meeting.
