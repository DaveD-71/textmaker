1.  **Note-taking**

<!-- -->

1.  Note taking and summarizing are important tasks in meetings.

2.  Notes are the record of the meeting. Summarizing identifies the main points.

3.  If the notes are not good the discussion and decisions will be lost and the meeting will have to be done over again.

    1.  **Note-taking in Meetings: Digital or Analog?**

    - **Digital**

> Taking notes on a laptop seems convenient because the notes can later be quickly converted into digital formats – e-mail, documents, or PowerPoint. However;

- Typing creates a bothersome click-click sound for other people at the meeting.

- Computers are a barrier to interpersonal communication. The note-taker seems focused on the computer, not the other people in the meeting.

- Effective note taking on a computer requires special note taking software and excellent typing skills. Not everyone has the software or the typing skills.

<!-- -->

- **Analog**

> Taking notes with a pen and paper is simple and easy.

- No special equipment or technical skills are required.

- It’s quiet and not distracting. The note-taker can be fully engaged in the meeting.

- Digital notes have a fixed format but analog notes are flexible – changes can be made easily and different styles of notes can be used on the same page.

- Loading notes into the computer after the meeting is an excellent way to review them.

<!-- -->

- **Conclusion**

<!-- -->

- Official note takers who are not chairing or participating in the discussion can take notes digitally - on a laptop - or analog - with a pen and paper.

- Participants in a meeting need to give their full attention to the issues and to the other participants. Their note taking should not interfere with those responsibilities. Using a pen and paper is the easiest and least distracting way to take notes.

2.  **Six Techniques for Taking Notes**

<!-- -->

1.  **Be an active note-taker**

> Avoid distractions. Concentrate from beginning to end.

2.  **Listen in English. Write in English.**

> Listen, think, and write <u>in English.</u> Don’t try to translate: Translation is difficult, time consuming, and the results are not good.

3.  **Be systematic**

> Take notes with a note-taking system that includes key words, themes, and a summary. See the sample below.

4.  **Aim for quantity and quality**

> Write as much as possible. You can organize it later.

- Write down all the key words and ideas you can: names, dates, numbers, technical terms, trends, changes, proposals, and decisions.

- Write quickly. Use abbreviations and symbols such as & / = \\ €

- Don’t stop to figure out if it’s 15 or 50. Guess – write down both: 15/50?

- Don’t erase: Cross out – ~~15~~/50?

5.  **Wh Questions: Who, where, what, when, why, and how**

> Use the Wh questions to gather and organize the information and your summary.

6.  **Review and consolidate your notes**

> After the meeting, review and revise your notes so they are clear and easy to understand when you refer back to them later.

1.  **A Note Taking System: Cornell Notes**

> Cornell-style notes are widely used by students and professionals for taking notes at meetings and lectures.

- They were originally developed for college students in the 1950s by Professor Walter Pauk at Cornell University.

- The Cornell system is based on a simple page layout and incorporates notes, key words, and a summary.

2.  **Summarizing Strategy**

> A summary is a brief report of the main points of an article, report, or presentation.
>
> A summary is always much shorter than the original and cannot include all the details. However, the summary must include the theme, the main points, the important details and a conclusion.
