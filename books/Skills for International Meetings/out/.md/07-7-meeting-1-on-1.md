- **Gathering Information at Conferences**

> One of the important aspects of international conferences is that they give an opportunity to meet and speak with experts in the field. It is common practice at conferences to approach people to engage in a specific discussion.

- **Social Protocol of Communication**

> The people you want to meet at conferences are probably well known experts in their field. That means they are usually very busy at conferences meeting people, making presentations, chairing and possibly participating in a variety of meetings.
>
> When approaching these people it is important to respect their time. The successful 1-on-1 meeting begins with preparation:

1.  Before the conference, do some research on the person you want to meet: What do they do? Where do they work? What articles have they published?

2.  Find a picture of the person: What do they look like?

3.  Prepare your questions: What important questions do you want to ask?

4.  Write your questions down. They should be short and specific.

5.  Practice saying your questions out loud so they are easy to say.

6.  Study the conference information and agenda: find out what the person is doing at the conference. How busy are they? Are they presenting or chairing or participating in a small group discussion that you can attend?

7.  When and where is a good place to introduce yourself?

8.  At the conference, before you introduce yourself, find a convenient place to meet: a quiet place where you can sit down and talk.

9.  When you introduce yourself, be prepared for the person to suggest having the meeting right then.

- **Asking questions in a 1-on-1 meeting**

<!-- -->

- Asking useful questions is a skill that needs to be practiced.

- To ask useful questions you need to know something about the subject.

  - Before meeting someone learn about the person and their field. Prepare your questions based on what you know and what you need to find out.

- Prepare enough questions for the time you have for the meeting.

- Your questions should be brief and to the point so there is more time for the responses.

- Decide the importance of your questions: which ones <u>must</u> you ask and which ones <u>would you like to</u> ask?

- Organization your list of questions according to importance:

  - Your first question should be a general question that needs only a brief response to create rapport between you and your counterparty.

  - Then ask the more important questions. Make sure you ask these questions before your time ends.

  - Have some more general questions to ask at the end if there is still time.

- **Replying to responses**

<!-- -->

- Reply to your counterparty’s responses with a confirmation, *I see. / I understand,* before asking your next question.

- Be prepared to ask follow up questions for clarification, confirmation, or more information
