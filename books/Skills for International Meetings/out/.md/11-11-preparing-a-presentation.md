# 11 Preparing a Presentation

> *How to organize & explain your ideas*

<img src="assets/media/image14.png" style="width:2.14055in;height:1.8685in" />

- **Good presentations don’t just happen**

> A good presentation that grabs the audience’s attention doesn’t just happen. It must be planned and practiced.

- Good presentations begin with the content and purpose: What do you want to say and why?

- The next step is to organize your ideas. Use the speech structure to organize your ideas, information, and message.

  - **Speech structure**

> Speeches have three essential parts. Each part helps to deliver a clear message.

- **Common Errors**

> Flag expressions are familiar, simple and useful. However, they are often used incorrectly through misunderstanding or carelessness.
>
> Use the flag expressions correctly and at the right time.

1.  ***My name is ...***

> *Thank you for the introduction, Mr Jones. <u>My name is</u> Hiroshi Tanaka*. *<u>Wrong!</u>*

- If the chairperson introduces you, <u>never</u> give your name. There is no need and it is rude to the chairperson.

  1.  ***At first …***

> *I have three points. <u>At first</u> I will talk about… <u>Wrong</u>!*
>
> *I have three points. <u>First</u> I will talk about… <u>Correct</u>!*
>
> Do not use *at first* to count or to introduce your first point.

- Count your points with *first, second, third.*

- *At first* is not used for counting. It is a different grammatical structure from counting. *At first* introduces a condition that is going to change. For example: *When I went to Canada, <u>at first</u> it was very difficult for me to speak English; <u>however</u>, after a while it became much easier for me.*

  1.  ***Let’s …***

> Do not use “Let’s” to introduce a proposal.

- *<u>Let’s</u> improve our products and advertising.* *<u>Wrong!</u>*

- *<u>I think we need to</u> improve our products and advertising.* *<u>Correct!</u>*

> “Let’s” is suitable only for informal proposals such as -

- *<u>Let’s</u> get started.*

- *<u>Let’s</u> take a short break.*

  1.  ***That’s all.***

> Never end your formal speech with “That’s all.” It is much too informal.

- Say, “Thank you very much.”

  1.  **Never use these expressions:**

- *… and so on / etc.*

- *By the way, …*

  1.  **Use numbers to count points. Use expressions to introduce ideas.**

<!-- -->

- In speeches we use the numbers *first, second, third*; or the expressions *first, then, next, finally* to count points in a list. For example:

<!-- -->

- *I have three points: one …; two …; and three …*

> This is different from introducing the ideas we are going to explain

- To introduce the ideas we are going to explain we use transition expressions, not numbers. For example, introduce ideas with these transition expressions:

<!-- -->

- *Let me begin with my first point.*

- *Now, I’ll move on to my second point.*

- *Now, that brings me to my third poin*
