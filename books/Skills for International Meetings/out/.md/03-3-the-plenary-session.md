> *Model Meeting 1: Opening & Key note speaker*

- **Plenary Sessions**

<!-- -->

- The opening plenary session includes:

<!-- -->

- **Opening Remarks** by the conference chairperson to welcome the dignitaries and all participants; state the purpose and goals of the conference, review the agenda, and make necessary administrative announcements.

- **Key Note Speech** to discuss the theme and issues of concern for the conference. The key note speaker is usually a recognized expert in the field.

<!-- -->

- The closing plenary session includes:

<!-- -->

- **Summary and Reports** by the chairperson and designated speakers from the small group discussions report on the accomplishments of the conference.

- **Closing Remarks** by the chairperson to thank the speakers and participants for their contributions and officially close the conference.

<!-- -->

- The length of the plenary sessions depends on the length of the conference itself.

<!-- -->

- For a one day conference the plenary sessions would probably last just one hour each or even less.

- For longer conferences of several days or a week, the plenary sessions could last several hours or all day. There could be several plenary sessions during longer conferences.

<!-- -->

- **Model Plenary Session Transcript**

> Below is a transcript for the plenary session at an international conference on high seas piracy. This transcript illustrates the five tasks, 6-step structure, and formal language of plenary sessions.

- There are six players in the transcript: the chair, Ms. Pieta Pinar; the speaker: Dr. Beatrice Matheson; plus four participants.

<!-- -->

- Read the transcript carefully and note how the language and structure work together.

- Use this transcript as a pattern for your chairing, speeches, and questions for your conference simulations.

**<u>Conference Simulations</u>**

- **First Conference Simulation**

  1.  For your first conference simulation prepare a brief report on your experience with international conferences. You will present your report in a conference simulation during the training.

  2.  Your report should be brief and mention the following points:

<!-- -->

- Frequency of the conferences.

- The length of the conferences.

- Where they were held.

- Themes or issues of the conferences.

- Your task at the conferences.

- What conference skills you need to improve.

  1.  If you have not yet participated in an international meeting, you can write your report based on your work experience and imagination. You can begin your report with a statement such as:

*Unfortunately, I have not yet participated in an international conference however, from my work experience and imagination I think this would be my experience. The conference would probably be overseas in Europe or the US. I would probably…*

- **Other Simulations**

> For the other simulations in the training program you will make brief reports describing some aspect of your work and proposals related to your work. You will receive the details about these reports from your instructor.
