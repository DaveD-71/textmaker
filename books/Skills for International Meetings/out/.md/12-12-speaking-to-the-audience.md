- **Delivery Skills are Important**

> Whether you are the chairperson, speaker, or a participant, delivery skills are very important. Good delivery skills build your confidence and win the attention of your audience.

- **Essential Delivery Skills**

> Your instructor will model each of these skills for you one-by-one.
>
> Stand up and practice the skills with your instructor until they are easy to do.

- **Voice\[\[LINEBREAK\]\]**Speak well. If your audience cannot hear you or understand you they will not receive your message.

<!-- -->

- Speak with a strong voice: clearly, smoothly, and without hesitation.

- Don't rush or speak too quickly.

- Speak with enthusiasm: let your audience know that you care about your topic.

- Emphasize important words with your voice and with gestures. Pause slightly before and after names, dates, numbers, and important words. For example: \[\[LINEBREAK\]\]*In ^ 2012, ^ there were ^ 247 ^ reported pirate attacks ^ worldwide.*

- Eliminate unnecessary pauses and *Umms*.

<!-- -->

- **Eye contact**

> Speak to your audience. Eye contact connects the speaker and the audience.

- Look at your audience, not at your notes.

- Look at the whole audience. Move your head so you can make eye contact with everyone.

- Memorize key parts of your speech so you can look at your audience. Memorize your greetings, introduction, the beginning of each main point, your conclusion, and closing thoughts.

<!-- -->

- **Posture**

> Good posture helps your breathing and makes you appear more confident.

- Stand straight with your feet slightly apart and your weight evenly on both feet.

- Don't lean on the table or lectern – look at your audience.

<!-- -->

- **Notes**

> Hold your notes in one hand just above your waist where you can easily see them and look at your audience.

- Don't put your notes on a table or lectern – if you're looking down at your notes, you can't look at your audience.

- Hold the page in the middle so it won’t fold over.

- Don’t move your notes up and down: it’s very distracting.

<!-- -->

- **Gesturing**

> Your hands are an important part of your delivery.
>
> Your hands should be holding your notes, by your side or gesturing. Gestures keep your audience’s attention and emphasize important points.

- Gestures should be firm and clear.

- Simple small gestures are best. Big gestures are not necessary.

- Gestures and eye contact go together. When you gesture, look at your audience: If you are not looking at your audience, do not gesture.

- When you count (1<sup>st</sup>, 2<sup>nd</sup>, 3<sup>rd</sup>), gesture with one finger, two fingers, three fingers. Don’t count with your fingers if you say, *"My next point is…"*

- Don't scratch your face, play with your hair, or cover your face with your hand if you adjust your glasses.

<!-- -->

- **Clothing – Neatness counts!**

> Make sure your clothing is appropriate to the situation and neat. Inappropriate or messy clothing distracts your audience from your message.
>
> Before you speak, check your jacket collar, buttons, and tie. Make sure your hair is neat and not falling over your face or eyes.

- **Before you begin**

Your presentation begins when you are called to speak and your audience can see you.

Before you begin to speak -

1.  Pause.

2.  Look at your audience.

3.  Get their attention.

4.  Take a deep breath.

5.  Begin.
