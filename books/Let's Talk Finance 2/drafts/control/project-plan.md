# Let's Talk Finance 2 (Book B) — Project Plan

*A new 20-topic finance discussion resource — companion volume to* Let's Talk Finance *(Book A), same format and standard.*

Companion volume: **Let's Talk Finance** (Book A) at `../../Let's Talk Finance/`. Shared rules: `../../_lets-talk-finance-shared/`. Overarching two-book plan and decision log: `../../Let's Talk Finance/PROJECT-PLAN.md`.

Created 2026-08-28. Phase 1 complete (all decisions D1–D8 resolved). Topic list locked.

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

Produce a second **reading-and-discussion resource** of 20 finance topics, built new to the same standard as Book A. Where Book A covers **systemic / regulatory / public-policy** finance, Book B covers **personal finance, corporate finance, and how markets actually work** — the money questions an educated adult meets in their own life and reads about in the news.

- Same 2-page-per-topic format, same 6-subsection template, same B1+/B2 level, same citation discipline.
- Written for a Japan-based reader (`house-style.md` §1a); company-neutral; real examples rotate across sectors and regions, with ≥1 labelled non-Japan example per topic.
- Reads as a companion to Book A, not a different product. No subject overlap (see `../../_lets-talk-finance-shared/topic-ledger.md`).

## 2. Target learner and level

Identical to Book A: educated adult **based in Japan, primarily a Japanese first-language speaker**, interested in finance/economics for professional or general reasons, **CEFR B1+/B2**. See `../../_lets-talk-finance-shared/house-style.md` §1 and **§1a (write from the Japanese reader's vantage point)** — this is the key calibration point. Book B's more personal-finance angle is, if anything, more accessible — but the level and framing rules are the same.

Book B's personal-finance topics have strong Japanese baselines to reflect: high household savings held mostly in cash and bank deposits (and the low return that has meant), the NISA tax-free investment accounts and the 2024 expansion, a housing market where new homes lose value quickly, the rise of consumer and card credit, an ageing society and worry about pension adequacy, and the recent return of noticeable inflation after decades without it. State these where they matter, and still give a labelled non-Japan comparison.

## 3. Scale and format

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Topics | 20, in 4 Parts (5/5/5/5) |
| Reading length | ~440–475 words; fills one A4 page as a 2-column article under the Goal block, no overflow (`house-style.md` §5) |
| Topic length | 2 A4 pages |
| Citations | inline `[N]` → numbered Source Notes with live public URLs; ≥2 factual anchors per Reading |
| Core resource | ~44 content pages + front matter + glossary |

### Overlap watch (vs Book A) — see `topic-ledger.md` for detail

- **B 1.3 Saving & Investing** vs A 3.4 Pension Reform — B is the *individual's* decision; A is the *public/occupational system*.
- **B 2.4 Executive Pay** vs A 4.3 Wealth Inequality — B is *inside one firm*; A is *society-wide*.
- **B 2.5 Banks & how they fail** vs A 2.4 Stress Testing — B is what a bank *is and does* + deposit-run mechanics; A is the *supervisory tool*.
- **B 3.1 What Moves Stock Markets** vs A 2.1 Market Volatility — B is the *mechanics*; A is *regulation*.
- **B 4.2 Government Debt & Deficits** vs A 2.5 Sovereign Debt Management — B is *political economy of deficits*; A is *issuer debt-management operations*.
- **B 4.5 The Future of Money** vs A 1.2 CBDCs — B is the *broad shift* (cash decline, tokenisation, privacy); A is *one instrument*.

If a Book B topic drifts toward a Book A topic during drafting, it is the **Book B** treatment that adjusts — Book A's 20 are fixed.
