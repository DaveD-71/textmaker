# 1 Meeting for the First Time

Meeting people at an international conference

> <u>Situation: At an international conference</u>
>
> Tomoya Ishii works for the Japan Industrial Safety Research Agency. He is attending an international conference on industrial safety in the US. Mr. Ishii approaches someone he would like to meet.

1.  Introducing yourself

> Ishii Hello. My name is Tomoya Ishii (Holds out his hand to shake).
>
> Marks (Shaking hands) It’s nice to meet you, Mr. Ishii. My name is Ted Marks.
>
> Ishii It’s nice to meet you, too, Mr. Marks. I work for the Japan Industrial Safety Research Agency. We call it ISRA. This is my card (Offers his business card). Who are you with?
>
> Marks Thank you. I’m with Industrial Safety Advisory Canada, ISA for short. This is my card (Offers his business card).
>
> Ishii Thank you.

2.  Exchanging information

> Ishii What does ISA do?
>
> Marks ISA surveys the current research on industrial safety, publishes reports and advises the government on industrial safety.
>
> Ishii And what do you do at ISA?
>
> Marks I’m the head of the editorial department. And what does ISRA do?
>
> Ishii Mainly we collect data from Japanese companies but we also conduct training programs, and research visits overseas. And we also publish our reports for the government and industry.
>
> Marks And what do you do at ISRA?
>
> Ishii I’m in the consumer products division. We focus on consumer products such as textiles and home furnishings.
>
> Marks I see. That’s very interesting. Tell me, what do you think about …?
>
> … the conversation continues …

3.  Saying Good-bye

> Ishii Well, it was very nice to meet you, Mr. Marks, but I’m sorry, I must be going now. I have to meet a colleague in a few minutes.
>
> Marks I understand. It was very nice meeting you too, Mr. Ishii (Holds out his hand to shake).
>
> Ishii (Shaking hands) Thank you Mr. Marks. Good-bye.
>
> Marks Good-bye, Mr. Ishii.
>
> <u>Language Check Points</u>

1.  **Hello. ~~Nice to meet you~~. My name is…**

> *Hello. Nice to meet you. My name is Hiroshi Tanaka* is English; however, it is <u>not correct English</u>.
>
> *Nice to meet you* is said <u>only</u> <u>after</u> hearing the other person’s name – <u>not before</u>.

2.  **My name is … Vs. I’m …**

> *My name is …* is the correct formal expression for business introductions.
>
> *I’m …* is appropriate for when people are expecting to meet each other (Please see Unit 3) and for informal social introductions.

3.  **Belong**

> *Belong* is not used for companies or departments. It is used for teams, clubs, political parties and religions. For example:

> <u>The Hand Shake</u>

- **There are four simple steps to a good handshake:**

> 1\. Make eye contact and extend your open hand.
>
> 2\. Let your hands meet at the base of the thumbs.
>
> 3\. Close your hand firmly and shake three times, – 1, 2, 3 – and smile!
>
> 4\. Open your hand and bring it back to your side.

- **The two handed shake? - Don’t do it!**

> The two-handed shake is for politicians: it is not generally used by business people. Avoid the two handed shake!
>
> Shake hands with your right hand only. Leave your left hand by your side. Don’t add your left hand to the shake by putting it on the other person’s hand or arm.

- **Culture Note: Different styles of handshake**

> **Firmness**: Your handshake should be firm but not too strong. Avoid giving a weak handshake: it’s considered unprofessional.
>
> North Americans tend to have firm handshakes. Europeans and Asians prefer a gentler grip.
>
> **Women**: In North America, both men and women generally offer to shake hands but in Britain, Europe, and Asia, men usually wait for the woman to offer her hand.

- **Worried about sweaty hands?**

> Don’t wipe your hand on your suit before shaking hands! Hold a handkerchief in your right hand and switch it to your left hand just before the handshake.
