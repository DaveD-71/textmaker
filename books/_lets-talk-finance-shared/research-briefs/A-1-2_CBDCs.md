# Research Brief — Book A 1.2 Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs)

Prepared 2026-08-28. Research only — not a draft. All URLs opened and marked below.

## What the topic covers (2-3 sentences, plain)
A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's official money, issued as a direct liability of the central bank rather than of a commercial bank. Governments are exploring two broad types: "retail" CBDCs for everyday public use and "wholesale" CBDCs for settlement between financial institutions. As of 2026 a large majority of the world's central banks are researching or piloting a CBDC, but only three small economies have fully launched one for the public, and adoption everywhere has been slow; several major economies (euro area, China, India, Japan) are in advanced pilots, while the United States has legally barred one.

## Regional spread available
Strong, verifiable material exists for well more than four regions:
- **East Asia — China (e-CNY):** world's largest pilot; 7tn yuan cumulative transactions by mid-2024, 17 provincial-level regions; PBOC/government sources. Contrasts with entrenched Alipay/WeChat Pay dominance.
- **Euro area — digital euro:** ECB, preparation phase 2023–25, next phase from Oct 2025, possible issuance 2029; clear primary sources and dates.
- **Caribbean — Bahamas (Sand Dollar, 2020) and Jamaica (JAM-DEX, 2022):** the world's first live CBDCs; central-bank primary sources; low-adoption story is well documented.
- **West Africa — Nigeria (eNaira, 2021):** first in Africa; IMF documented very low usage.
- **South Asia — India (digital rupee):** RBI wholesale + retail pilots from late 2022; PIB primary source; 1-million-transactions-a-day milestone Dec 2023.
- **East Asia — Japan (BOJ pilot):** proof-of-concept 2021–23, pilot since April 2023, no issuance decision.
- **Northern Europe — Sweden (e-krona):** Riksbank pilot concluded 2024, no current need — a useful "stepped back" case.
- **North America — United States:** Executive Order 14178 (Jan 2025) prohibits a CBDC — a clean contrast case.

## The Japanese angle (1 short paragraph)
Japan is a mid-pack explorer, not a leader. The Bank of Japan ran a two-phase "proof of concept" from April 2021 to early 2023 (Phase 1 April 2021–March 2022; Phase 2 from April 2022), then began a **pilot program in April 2023** that simulates how the public would use a digital yen through banks and other intermediaries — no real retail transactions take place. The BOJ has repeatedly said **no decision has been made to issue a CBDC**, and that any such decision is for the Japanese public and the Diet, not the central bank alone; senior officials restated this "no plan to issue" position as recently as 2025. The stated motivation is readiness — keeping a public-money option available as cash use declines — rather than an intention to launch. ## Notes / cautions
- **Fast-moving numbers.** The e-CNY figure to anchor on is the PBOC's own: **7 trillion yuan cumulative by end-June 2024** (government source, verified). Later, larger figures circulate (e.g. ~16.7 trillion yuan / ~230 million wallets attributed to PBOC data around end-2025), but those trace to news write-ups and secondary trackers, not a page I could open on a PBOC/BIS/IMF domain. Use the mid-2024 official figure unless the writer can open a primary end-2025 PBOC release.
- **"Cumulative" vs "annual."** China's headline number is *cumulative* transaction value since the pilot began — it is not annual turnover and should be worded carefully. Much reported e-CNY "use" is also policy-driven (government disbursements, staff payments) rather than organic; the same caveat applies to India's Dec 2023 one-million-transactions-in-a-day milestone, which banks engineered by routing employee payments through the pilot.
- **Adoption is the real story.** All three live retail CBDCs (Sand Dollar, JAM-DEX, eNaira) have struggled: Sand Dollar is well under 1% of Bahamian currency in circulation; the IMF found the vast majority of eNaira wallets never used. A balanced reading should not imply CBDCs are widespread in daily life anywhere.
- **The digital euro is not decided.** It depends on EU legislation still in progress (assumed 2026). "Possible pilot mid-2027," "potential first issuance 2029," and cost estimates (~EUR 1.3bn development; ~EUR 320m/year from 2029; tested holding limits up to EUR 3,000) are all conditional — phrase as plans, not facts on the ground.
- **US position.** Executive Order 14178 (23 January 2025) bars a US CBDC; a Federal Reserve "digital dollar" is therefore off the table for now. This is a policy choice that could change with a future administration — attribute it and date it.
- **BOJ pilot start date.** Secondary sources vary between "February 2023" and "April 2023." The BOJ's own framing: the pilot was *announced* in February 2023 (Uchida speech) and *launched* in April 2023. Use April 2023.
- **Overlap guard (topic ledger):** Book B 4.5 "The Future of Money" covers the broader money-futures theme. Keep A 1.2 tightly on the CBDC instrument itself — definition, the retail/wholesale split, who has launched/piloted, and the adoption record — not on cash decline, crypto, or stablecoins except as contrast terms.
- **Tracker attribution.** Two different "CBDC trackers" exist: the Atlantic Council one (atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker) named in the brief, and a separate Human Rights Foundation one (cbdctracker.hrf.org). Cite the Atlantic Council tracker as the secondary index; if using an HRF page, label it correctly.
- As of August 2026 the euro-area timeline (legislation expected "in 2026") is the item most likely to have moved — check the ECB digital euro pages for any 2026 legislative progress before finalising.
