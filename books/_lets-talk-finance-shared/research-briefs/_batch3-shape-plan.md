## Book A — Let's Talk Finance, Part 3 (Capital, Investment and Public Policy)

| Topic | Article shape | Opening style | Lead region(s) | Japan placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Green Finance Initiatives | data-led explainer (what a green bond/label is, market scale) | short comparison (an ordinary bond vs a labelled green bond funding the same kind of project) | EU (Green Bond Standard/taxonomy), global market-size data, Japan (GX transition bonds), one EM green-bond issuer | mid: Japan's sovereign "GX transition bonds" (first mover among sovereigns); FSA/TSE green-bond guidance |
| 3.2 Infrastructure Investment | problem → responses (funding gap → who fills it: public budgets, PPPs, multilateral banks, private capital) | misconception ("infrastructure is built by government alone") | global funding-gap estimate (G20/GI Hub or OECD), US, EU, Japan (public works spending, ageing infrastructure), one EM PPP case | short para: Japan's public-works tradition and ageing-infrastructure maintenance bill |
| 3.3 Trade Policies and Tariffs | two-case comparison (a tariff imposed vs a negotiated exemption/deal) | direct question ("Who actually pays a tariff?") | US (recent tariff actions), China, EU, Japan (export exposure, a trade agreement), one EM | mid: Japan as an export-exposed economy and a concluded trade agreement (e.g. Japan-US or CPTPP) |
| 3.4 Pension Reform | short chronology (retirement-age and contribution changes over recent decades) | concrete case (a country raising its pension age / a specific reform date) | France (2023 pension-age reform), Japan (ageing population, pension-age history), one Asia/EM case, US Social Security context | LEAD-ish: Japan's ageing population and pension-age/contribution history is a natural anchor, alongside France as the concrete opening case |
| 3.5 Global Economic Recovery Post-COVID | scenario → principle → trade-off | dated event (the 2020 shock, told briefly, then the recovery data) | global (IMF recovery data), US, EU, Japan (slower reopening, later recovery), one EM/lower-income-country divergence | mid: Japan's later reopening and comparatively muted post-COVID inflation/growth path vs the US/EU |

## Book B — Let's Talk Finance 2, Part 3 (Markets, Risk and the Global Economy)

| Topic | Article shape | Opening style | Lead region(s) | Japan placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 What Moves Stock Markets | data-led explainer (earnings, rates, sentiment, index concentration) | direct question ("Why did the market fall today?") | US (index concentration, e.g. Magnificent Seven weighting), Japan (Nikkei/TOPIX, BOJ rate effect), Europe, one EM index | mid: Nikkei 225 sensitivity to BOJ policy and the yen |
| 3.2 Bubbles, Crashes and Manias | short chronology (a sequence of historical/recent bubbles) | dated event (a specific crash date told briefly) | Netherlands (tulip mania, historical), US (dot-com, 1929), Japan (late-1980s asset bubble), one recent case (crypto) | mid-to-lead: Japan's late-1980s asset-price bubble and its long aftermath — a strong, well-documented case |
| 3.3 Commodities: Oil, Metals and Food | two-case comparison (an oil-supply shock vs a food/metals price story) | scene / concrete situation (a farmer, refinery, or import-dependent buyer facing a price move) | Middle East/OPEC+ (oil), one metals case (e.g. copper/critical minerals), global food-price index, Japan (import dependence) | mid: Japan's near-total dependence on imported energy and food, and the weak-yen effect on import costs |
| 3.4 Currencies and Exchange Rates | scenario → principle → trade-off | misconception ("a weak currency is always bad for a country") | Japan (weak yen, BOJ intervention), US (dollar's global role), one EM currency crisis, euro area | LEAD-ish: the weak yen (2022-24) as the opening scenario, then widen to general exchange-rate mechanics |
| 3.5 Emerging Markets and Development Finance | problem → responses (capital flight/debt distress → IMF/World Bank/other responses) | short comparison (an EM growth success vs one in debt distress) | one fast-growing EM (e.g. India/Vietnam), one debt-distress EM, IMF/World Bank (global), Japan (as a bilateral/multilateral lender via JICA) | short para: Japan's role as a bilateral and multilateral development lender (JICA, ADB) rather than as the article's subject |
