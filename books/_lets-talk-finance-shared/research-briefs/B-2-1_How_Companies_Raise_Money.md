# Research Brief — Book B 2.1 How Companies Raise Money

Researched August 2026. Current month for "as of" phrasing: August 2026.

## What the topic covers (2-3 sentences, plain)

Companies fund themselves in two basic ways: by selling **equity** (shares of ownership, which never has to be repaid but dilutes existing owners) or by taking on **debt** (bank loans or bonds, which must be repaid with interest but leaves ownership intact). A firm can raise equity privately — from founders, venture capital, or private-equity funds — or publicly, by "going public" through an **initial public offering (IPO)** and listing its shares on a stock exchange. Over the last thirty years the balance has shifted: in the United States and parts of Europe far fewer companies list publicly than in the 1990s, while a fast-growing pool of **private capital** (private equity and, more recently, private credit) now lets large companies raise billions and stay private for years; bank lending still dominates corporate finance in Japan and much of continental Europe and Asia.

## The Japanese angle (1 short paragraph)

Japan's big companies have traditionally borrowed rather than issued shares: under the postwar **"main bank" system** each large firm had a lead bank that lent to it, held its shares, and watched over management, with the big horizontal **keiretsu** groups (Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo and others) built around such a bank. That model is unwinding slowly — companies and their banks are selling down the **cross-shareholdings** that once locked ownership in place, pushed by investors and by the exchange itself. On **4 April 2022 the Tokyo Stock Exchange restructured into three segments, Prime, Standard and Growth** (Japan Exchange Group, "Overview of Market Restructuring," https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/equities/improvements/market-structure/01.html — open in a browser; jpx.co.jp blocks bots), leaving roughly 3,800 listed companies. Then in **March 2023 the TSE asked companies — especially the ~43% trading below book value, against about 5% in the S&P 500 — to publish concrete plans to raise their capital efficiency and share price**; by March 2025 more than 90% of Prime Market companies had complied and the average Prime price-to-book had risen from about 1.1 to 1.4 (Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance, "Tokyo Stock Exchange Initiative on Cost of Capital and Stock Price Conscious Management," 21 Oct 2025, https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/10/21/tokyo-stock-exchange-initiative-on-cost-of-capital-and-stock-price-conscious-management/ ).

## Terminology likely needed (candidate New terms)

- **equity** — an ownership stake in a company; equity finance means raising money by selling shares
- **debt** — money a company borrows and must repay with interest, through bank loans or bonds
- **share / stock** — a unit of ownership in a company
- **bond** — a tradable IOU: the company borrows from investors and promises fixed interest plus repayment on a set date
- **initial public offering (IPO)** — the first sale of a company's shares to the public, when it "goes public"
- **listing** — having a company's shares quoted and traded on a stock exchange
- **public company vs private company** — one whose shares trade on an open market and who must report publicly, versus one whose shares are held by founders and private investors
- **stock exchange** — a regulated marketplace where shares and bonds are bought and sold (e.g. NYSE, Nasdaq, Tokyo Stock Exchange)
- **venture capital (VC)** — money invested in young, high-risk companies in exchange for equity
- **private equity (PE)** — funds that buy whole companies (often with borrowed money) and hold them privately for several years
- **private credit** — lending to companies by investment funds rather than by banks
- **market capitalisation** — the total market value of a company's shares (share price × number of shares)
- **cross-shareholding** — the practice of two companies each holding blocks of the other's shares to cement a business relationship (relevant to the Japan angle)
- **main bank** — in Japan, the lead bank that historically provided a company's loans, held its shares and monitored it

## Notes / cautions

- **"Number of listed companies" figures depend on the definition** — domestic-incorporated only vs all listings; operating companies only vs including funds, trusts and REITs; one exchange vs a whole country vs all WFE members. The US peak is "more than 8,000" domestically incorporated firms in 1996; "now" is quoted anywhere from ~3,700 (operating companies, CRSP) to ~4,500 (broader). Always give the source and year and use a range.
- **The US-listings-decline story is well established but exact counts vary by source**, and there is a live academic debate about *why*: the traditional explanation (fewer IPOs, more buyouts, regulatory cost) versus Eckbo & Lithell's argument that once mergers are properly counted the "decline" is smaller than it looks. The reading can note the decline as fact and mention that economists disagree on the causes.
- **Private-capital and private-credit AUM are not official statistics.** The US$22tn figure is Bank of America Research's; Preqin, McKinsey and others give different totals, and "private credit" ranges from ~US$1.7tn to ~US$3.5tn depending on what is counted. Name the source and date.
- **Bond figures**: distinguish the *outstanding stock* of corporate bond debt (~US$35tn globally, end-2024, OECD) from *annual issuance* (e.g. ~US$2tn in the US in 2024, SIFMA). Don't conflate them.
- **Several primary sites block automated fetching (HTTP 403):** world-exchanges.org, oecd.org, jpx.co.jp, sifma.org, reuters.com. The figures cited from them here are corroborated by established secondary coverage, but the writer/editor should open each in a normal browser to lift exact current numbers before the reading is finalised.
- **TSE segment counts drift monthly** as companies list, delist and move between segments; cite the JPX "Number of Listed Companies" page with its as-of date.
