# Research Brief — Book A 1.5 Financial Inclusion Initiatives

_Researched 2026-08-28. All URLs opened unless marked otherwise._

## What the topic covers (plain)

Financial inclusion means giving people who are shut out of the formal financial
system — the "unbanked" and "underbanked" — practical access to an account,
payments, savings, credit and insurance. In low- and middle-income countries the
big drivers have been mobile money (a payment account held on a phone rather than
at a bank), networks of local shop agents who handle cash-in and cash-out, and
government-built "rails" such as instant-payment systems and mass account-opening
programmes. Governments and central banks matter twice over: as regulators (KYC
rules, licensing non-banks, consumer protection) and increasingly as builders and
operators of the infrastructure itself.

  (SSA 33% mobile money 2021; 58% account ownership 2024), CBK FinAccess 2024
  (Kenya 84.8%), M-Pesa launch 2007 and 34m customers 2024, GSMA global mobile

  Nov 2020; 67% of adults in a year; 71.5m previously-inactive people brought in;

- **East / Southeast Asia** — Findex 2025 regional spread: Thailand 92%, China
  89%, contrasted with Cambodia 39% and Lao PDR 38%; East Asia & Pacific overall
  ~83%. Shows wide intra-regional variation. (Indonesia is one of the eight
  countries holding most of the world's unbanked.)
- **High-income / Japan** — see below; a different meaning of "inclusion."

## The Japanese angle

Japan is high-income and effectively fully banked, so "financial inclusion" here
is not about opening first accounts but about *how people pay and who gets left
behind by change*. Japan has been strikingly cash-dependent: the government set a
target to lift the cashless share of consumer spending to 40% by 2025, and METI
reported it hit 42.8% in 2024, a year early, with an 80% long-term goal (interim
~65% by 2030) — meaning well over half of spending was still cash as recently as
2024 (METI, 31 March 2025, https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2025/0331_001.html;
Bank of Japan Review 2025-E-9 on banknote circulation,
https://www.boj.or.jp/en/research/wps_rev/rev_2025/data/rev25e09.pdf). Inclusion
concerns focus on elderly and rural users as bank branches and ATMs close, and on
foreign residents who face documentation and language barriers to opening
accounts. Historically the inclusion tool was the postal network: Japan Post Bank,
with roots in an 1875 postal-savings system and about 24,000 outlets under a
universal-service obligation, long guaranteed that even remote communities had a
place to save (Japan Post Bank,
https://www.jp-bank.japanpost.jp/en/aboutus/company/en_aboutus_company_history.html).

## Notes / cautions

- **Be precise about the Findex edition.** Editions: 2011, 2014, 2017, 2021, and
  now **2025** (fieldwork in 2024, released July 2025). "51% → 76%" is the
  2011→2021 story from the *2021* edition. The *2025* edition gives 79% for 2024
  and ~1.3 billion unbanked, and says roughly "50% in 2011 → 80% in 2024" when
  rounding. Do not mix the 76%/1.4bn (2021) and 79%/1.3bn (2024) pairs.
- The Findex 2025 press release rounds global 2024 account ownership to "80%" in
  some sentences and states "79%" in others; both come from the same release.
  Prefer "nearly 80% (79%)."
- **M-Pesa launch:** March 2007 commercial launch by Safaricom; a pilot ran from
  2005. Vodafone was the technology partner. Say "launched by Safaricom in 2007."
- **Pix operator:** Banco Central do Brasil both operates the settlement system
  (SPI) and regulates participation — a genuinely unusual central-bank role worth
  flagging accurately. Launched 16 November 2020 for the general public (a limited
  trial ran from 3 November 2020).
- **PMJDY numbers move monthly.** Safest framing: "launched August 2014; over
  500 million accounts by the mid-2020s, most held by women and rural
  residents." If a precise number is wanted, use "about 56 crore (560 million) as
  of August 2025 (PIB)."
- Several primary sites (Safaricom, PIB, METI English, bcb.gov.br, gsma.com)
  block automated fetching but load normally in a browser — the writer should
  open these directly to confirm quotes before publication. Every figure above is
  corroborated by at least one source that did open.
- The GSMA "State of the Industry" mobile money report is an industry body, not a
  regulator — reliable and widely cited, but note it if a stricter primary-source
  standard is applied; the Findex numbers cover the same ground from the World
  Bank.
- Japan's "57% still cash" is the arithmetic complement of METI's 42.8% cashless
  figure for 2024; state it as "the cashless ratio was 42.8%" rather than
  inventing a cash figure METI does not publish directly.
