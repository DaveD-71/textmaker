| 4.3 Wealth Inequality and Redistribution Policies | data-led explainer (how inequality is measured — Gini, top-1%/top-10% shares, wealth vs income — then the redistribution toolkit: progressive tax, transfers, wealth/inheritance tax, and their limits) | short comparison (top 1% wealth share vs bottom 50% wealth share, one country or global) | global (World Inequality Lab / Credit Suisse-UBS wealth report), US (rising top shares), Nordics (transfers-heavy model), one Latin American high-inequality case, Japan | mid: Japan as a relatively low-income-inequality but ageing, asset-concentrated society; the "1 oku en no kabe" and the 2025 push on taxing high earners' financial income |
| 4.4 Corporate Governance and Accountability | short chronology / timeline (governance failure → code → hard law, across decades: Cadbury 1992 → Sarbanes-Oxley 2002 → post-2008 → stewardship/ESG era) | common misconception ("shareholders run the company" / "the board works for the CEO") | UK (Cadbury Code, "comply or explain"), US (Sarbanes-Oxley 2002, post-Enron), Japan (Corporate Governance Code 2015, revised 2018/2021; TSE 2023 cost-of-capital push), one EM | LEAD-ish: Japan's decade of governance reform — the 2014 Stewardship Code, 2015 Corporate Governance Code, unwinding of cross-shareholdings, TSE's 2023 "P/B below 1" campaign — is a strong, well-documented arc |
| 4.5 Economic Diplomacy and International Cooperation | scenario → principle → trade-off (a sanctions episode or a coordinated rescue → the principle that financial and trade ties are leverage → the trade-off: measures hurt the sender too, and push targets toward alternatives) | plain definition / short statement ("Economic diplomacy is the use of trade, investment, aid, and financial ties — including cutting them off — as tools of foreign policy") | G7/EU (Russia sanctions 2022, ~US$300bn CBR reserves frozen), US (dollar-system leverage, secondary sanctions), IMF/World Bank & G20 (coordinated crisis response), China (alternative institutions: AIIB, CIPS), Japan | short para: Japan's participation in G7 Russia sanctions despite its energy-import exposure (Sakhalin projects); Japan's ODA and "economic security" law (2022) |
