# Research Brief — Book B 2.2 Startups, Venture Capital and Failure

Researched August 2026. Current month for "as of" phrasing: August 2026.

## What the topic covers (2-3 sentences, plain)

Startups usually raise money in stages as they grow and prove themselves — first small amounts from founders, friends and "angel" investors, then a "seed" round, then larger institutional rounds labelled Series A, B, C and so on, each at a higher "valuation" (the price investors put on the whole company). Venture-capital (VC) firms run this as a portfolio of many small bets: they expect most investments to lose money or barely return the cash, and rely on a handful of huge winners to carry the fund — the "power law." When money is cheap, valuations inflate and the number of "unicorns" (private firms valued at US$1bn or more) balloons; when conditions tighten, firms are forced into "down rounds" (raising new money at a lower valuation than before), and the large majority of startups still end up shutting down, merging cheaply, or never returning investor capital.

## The Japanese angle (1 short paragraph)

Japan has the world's fourth-largest economy but a small venture-capital market and, on the IMF's count, just **7 unicorns worth a combined US$9.8 billion in 2023 — against 653 in the United States worth around US$2 trillion** (IMF Working Paper WP/24/246, "Riding Unicorns," Dec 2024). Venture capital as a share of GDP sits below the OECD average, and exits have historically meant a modest domestic IPO rather than a big trade sale. To change this the government made a **Cabinet decision in November 2022 on a "Startup Development Five-Year Plan"** that aims to raise annual startup investment roughly tenfold to **¥10 trillion and to produce 100 unicorns and 100,000 startups by around FY2027**, backed by tax incentives for angel investors, university-startup programmes and a record startup budget (JETRO summary: https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/investment_environment/ijre/report2023/ch3/sec10.html ; plan text: https://www.cas.go.jp/jp/seisaku/atarashii_sihonsyugi/pdf/sdfyplan2022en.pdf ). Progress is partial — the number of startups has risen from about 16,000 to roughly 25,000 since 2022 and university spin-outs hit a record ~5,074 in 2024 — but investment is still far short of the ¥10tn goal, and more graduates now joining or founding startups is as much a cultural shift as a financial one (Japan Times, 25 Dec 2025: https://www.japantimes.co.jp/2025/12/25/special-supplements/japans-startup-ecosystem-gains-global-attention/ ).

## Terminology likely needed (candidate New terms)

- **startup** — a young company built to grow fast, usually in tech, and usually not yet profitable
- **venture capital (VC)** — money invested in early, high-risk companies in exchange for a share of ownership
- **angel investor** — a wealthy individual who puts their own money into a company at a very early stage
- **seed round** — the first formal funding round, to build a product and find early customers
- **Series A / B / C** — successive, larger funding rounds as a company grows, each usually at a higher valuation
- **valuation** — the total value investors place on a company when they put money in
- **unicorn** — a privately held startup valued at US$1 billion or more
- **down round** — a funding round in which the company's valuation is lower than in its previous round
- **exit** — the point when investors turn their shares into cash, through an IPO or a sale of the company
- **portfolio** — the whole set of companies a VC fund has invested in, some expected to fail and a few to pay for the rest
- **power law** — the pattern where a tiny number of investments produce almost all the returns
- **burn rate** — how fast a startup spends its cash (optional extra)
- **IPO (initial public offering)** — the first sale of a company's shares to the public on a stock market (may already be a known term)

## Notes / cautions

- **VC funding totals are not "primary" data.** PitchBook, CB Insights and Crunchbase each report a different number for the same year (2025: Crunchbase ~US$425bn, PitchBook ~US$513bn) because they define "venture," "stage" and "geography" differently. Always name the tracker and the date. Do not present any single figure as *the* number.
- **Unicorn counts drift** as private valuations rise and fall and as trackers add or drop companies. The CB Insights list showed ~1,200 in early 2025, ~1,249 at end-2024 in its report, WIPO says 1,290 for 2025, and CB Insights' live page showed a higher figure again in 2026. Give a rounded range ("about 1,250–1,300") and attribute.
- **Failure-rate figures depend on the definition.** The BLS BED data measures establishment *survival* (an establishment "dies" when it stops reporting employment — which includes sales, mergers and relocations, not just failure), so it slightly overstates outright failure. The "~75% fail" figure is Shikhar Ghosh's HBS study and uses a broad definition (never returned investor cash); the NVCA's estimate of *complete* failure is ~25–30%. Present both framings.
- **bls.gov, imf.org, hbs.edu, oecd.org, pib.gov.in and pitchbook.com all block automated fetching** (HTTP 403) and several returned only via search-engine summaries in this research. Every one of these should be opened in a normal browser by the writer/editor to lift exact current numbers before the reading is finalised. The figures cited here are consistent across multiple independent reports but the primary pages must be eyeball-verified.
- **Japan's Five-Year Plan target year** is stated as "March 2028" (i.e. end of FY2027) in the JETRO summary; some sources loosely say "by 2027." Use "by around FY2027 (March 2028)."
- Current month is August 2026 — the 2025 year-end VC data (published Jan 2026) is the most recent full-year set available; do not expect 2026 full-year figures yet.
