# 1. Self-Study

To develop an effective self-study plan, you need to:

1.  Understand your strong and weak points and manage your development by recognising the opportunities and threats.

2.  Set effective and meaningful goals.

3.  Make a flexible study plan and follow it.

## 1. The Self-Study SWOT

For learners near the beginning of their study, or even mid-way and wanting to reassess their situation, a SWOT assessment can help set you on the right path to achieving your learning goals.\[\[SHAPE:1\]\]\[\[SHAPE:2\]\]\[\[SHAPE:3\]\]\[\[SHAPE:4\]\]

|  O  | pportunities |      What chances do you have?      |
|  T  | hreats       | What outside forces could stop you? |

The SWOT requires you to look internally at your own Strengths and Weaknesses, and to look externally at the Opportunities and Threats that surround you. It helps you identify and build on what you do well, address what is lacking, minimize risks, and take the greatest possible advantage of chances for success.

Think about your own ability to study and achieve your learning goals and complete the SWOT on the next page.

It is good practice to set multiple goals. Having multiple goals creates more opportunities for success and is more motivating. It also adds variety to your study and in that way makes it interesting.

Goals for language learning often fall into three main categories:

##### <u>Learning a skill</u>

> This can include participating in business meetings in English, giving presentations, having successful discussion, achieving a successful negotiation, etc.

##### <u>Learning knowledge</u>

> This can include increasing your vocabulary and your understanding of grammar. These are very important when there is a gap in your knowledge.

##### <u>Testing to measure progress</u>

> When learning English in Japan, students often take a TOEIC, TOEFL, Eiken or IELTS test to measure their ability and progress. Testing is never the goal of language learning; it is just a way of measuring your progress. Therefore, testing should never be a long-term goal.

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

### Goal Types and Duration

Goals can be long-, mid- or short-term.

1.  Typically, a long-term goal is three years or more. Long-term goals should be aspirational. This means they should reflect an ambition or dream you may have. Just be careful of being too ambitious, and not realistic. At the same time, a long-term goal for language learning should reflect how you want to practically use the language.

2.  A mid-term goal can be anywhere from six months to one year. Mid-term goals should be easier and take less resources to achieve than a long-term goal, simply because they take less time to achieve them.

3.  A short-term goal is typically from three to six months. Short-term goals are often motivating because they can be achieved quickly.

By the end of next year, I will be able to participate in simple conversations about general and common topics with a native speaker.

I will increase my TOEIC<sup>®</sup> LR score by 35 points for Listening and 25 points for Reading by next February.

I will learn 30 new words each week for the next six months.

## 4. Planning

\[\[SECTIONBREAK\]\]

Your study plan must be strategic. This means your plan must clearly connect to both your goals and your SWOT. When you make your plan, choose activities which strengthen your weaknesses, help you avoid threats, maximise your advantages, and use your opportunities to achieve your goals.

#### Study Activities

To achieve your goal, you must act. This means you need to decide the activities you will do which will help you achieve your goals. You can do more than one activity to achieve a goal, and a single activity can help you achieve multiple goals.

Think carefully about the ‘Who’, ‘When’ and ‘Where’, and ‘How’ for each activity (the ‘What’ is the activity itself, and the ‘Why’ is to help you achieve your goal!).

On the next two pages, **write four self-study activities**. Answer the following questions for each one:

1.  **What** is the activity? Give a detailed explanation.

2.  **Which goal** is it for? You can choose more than one.

3.  **How often** will you do this activity? Commit to a schedule.

4.  **How long** will you do it each time? Be realistic and practical.

5.  **Where** will you do the activity?

Each time you do the activity, **record your progress** by crossing off one of the stars.
