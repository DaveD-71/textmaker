# 4. Business Presentations

## 1. Presentation Structure

Speeches and presentations have three standard parts, and each part has a specific purpose.\[\[SHAPE:149\]\]\[\[SHAPE:150\]\]\[\[SHAPE:151\]\]\[\[SHAPE:152\]\]

#### The Introduction

1.  This is the basis of your speech. Here you say what you are going to say.

2.  Greet your audience. Introduce your theme and your main points.

3.  Get your audience interested\![\[SHAPE:153\]\]\[\[SHAPE:154\]\]

#### The Body

1.  This is the longest part of your speech. This is where you say what you want to say.

2.  Tell your audience everything you want them to know.

3.  You must select what you say. You cannot say everything about your theme, so choose the most important ideas.

4.  Keep your audience interested\![\[SHAPE:155\]\]\[\[SHAPE:156\]\]

#### The Conclusion

1.  Here you say what you said and deliver your message.

2.  The conclusion is very important. Review your theme and summarise your main points and the key learning points.

3.  Do not introduce any new ideas or new information.

4.  Deliver your closing message clearly. It is a vital part of your conclusion.

5.  Clearly express your opinion or state what action is needed.

6.  Leave your audience thinking about your closing message!

## 3. Presentation Delivery

![](../assets/media/image2.png)Good delivery skills are essential for a good presentation. They build your confidence and win the attention of your audience.\[\[SHAPE:157\]\]\[\[SHAPE:158\]\]

In a presentation, the most important element is the speaker. Use delivery skills to keep the audience’s attention on you.

\[\[SECTIONBREAK\]\]

#### Voice

Your voice is your most important delivery tool. A clear strong voice delivers your message to your audience. If they cannot hear you they will not receive your message.

1)  Focus on speaking fluently (without hesitation and restarts). Speak clearly and smoothly.

2)  Don't rush or speak too quickly.

3)  Emphasize the important words and phrases.

4)  Use pausing effectively.

#### \[\[LINEBREAK\]\]Eye Contact

Eye contact connects the speaker and the audience.

1)  Look at your audience, not at your notes.

2)  Look at the whole audience. Move your head so you can make eye contact with everyone.

#### Gestures

If you are giving a face-to-face presentation, one of your hands will probably be holding your notes. There are three possible positions for your other hand—by your side, behind your back, or gesturing. Gesturing is the best thing to do with your free hand.

1)  Gestures attract your audience’s attention and emphasize important points.

2)  Gestures should be simple, firm and clear.

3)  Gestures and eye contact go together. When you gesture, look at your audience. If you are not looking at your audience, do not gesture.

4)  When you count (1st, 2nd, 3rd), gesture with one finger, two fingers, three fingers.

5)  Don't scratch your face or play with your hair.

6)  Don’t cover your face with your hand if you adjust your glasses.

#### Clothing

Make sure your clothing is appropriate for the situation and then wear it neatly.

1)  Messy clothing and hair will distract your audience from your message.

2)  Before you speak make sure your clothing is neat: check your tie, your jacket collar, and your hair.

#### Posture

Good posture helps your breathing and makes you appear more confident.

1)  Stand straight with your feet slightly apart and your weight evenly on both feet.

2)  Don't lean on the table or lectern – look at your audience.

#### Notes

Hold your notes just above your waist where you can easily see them and look at your audience.

1)  Hold the page in the middle so it won’t fold over.

2)  Don’t move your notes up and down: it’s very distracting.

3)  Don't put your notes on a table or lectern – If you're looking down at your notes, you can't look at your audience.

## 4. Model—My Introduction

Hiroshi Tanaka is a new employee at Nippon Taiyo Bank (NTB), a large bank in Tokyo. His self-introduction speech is below. The speech is very short. However, it follows the basic speech structure of introduction, body, and conclusion.

#### Introduction

| Greetings and purpose | Good afternoon everyone. It’s a pleasure to be here. My name is Hiroshi Tanaka. I’d like to tell you a little about myself and why I decided to join the Nippon Taiyo Bank. |
|----|----|

#### Body

| Background and interests | I was born in Hokkaido, but I grew up and went to school in Kyoto. In high school I always liked mathematics and the sciences, so when I went to university I decided to study physics. I also took some courses in economics which were very interesting. |
|----|----|
| Reason for joining NTB | Since I enjoyed both physics and economics I decided I wanted to combine them in my career, so I looked for a job in banking. That’s why I decided to join the Nippon Taiyo Bank. |
| Goals at NTB | In my career at NTB I know I will work in many different departments, but I really want to use my degree in physics to develop useful new quantitative tools to analyze market trends and risk. This kind of analysis is very important for the bank and for the businesses that it serves. I think this will be very interesting and useful work. This is my personal goal for my career at the Nippon Taiyo Bank. |

#### Conclusion

| Gratitude | I am very happy to be here today, and I am glad to be working with you all. |
|----|----|
| Close | Thank you very much. |

## 5. Model—My Company

Hiroshi Tanaka had to make a short speech to introduce Nippon Taiyo Bank. He described a little about the history and activities of NTB. Below is his speech.

#### Introduction

| Greetings and courtesies. | Good morning everyone. Thank you for inviting me to talk about The Nippon Taiyo Bank. Today, I will tell you a little about the bank’s history and services. |
|----|----|

#### Body

| Main point \#1 | The Nippon Taiyo Bank is a large bank based in Tokyo. It was created in 2004 with the merger of two banks, the Marunouchi Bank and the Musashi Bank. The Marunouchi Bank was a mid-sized, domestic retail bank. They mostly provided banking services to individual customers and small businesses. The Musashi Bank was a large corporate bank. They provided financial services to mid-sized and large corporate clients. |
|----|----|
| Main point \#2 | The Nippon Taiyo Bank is a well-capitalized institution with retail and corporate branches across Japan. Currently, we have 257 branches. We provide financial services to individuals as well as small- and medium-sized businesses. Our large corporate clients are managed by our head office and our four regional offices. We also have seven overseas branches in Asia, North America, and Europe providing services to our corporate clients. |
| Main point \#3 | NTB’s corporate clients come from all sectors of the economy. Our traditional strength has been providing asset finance, project finance and M&A advisory services to the manufacturing and heavy industry sectors however, in recent years we have also been growing in the IT, communication, and service sectors. |

#### Conclusion

| Message, thanks & Q&A | We are a dynamic bank. We are always looking at future opportunities. I hope my introduction helps you understand our business. Thank you very much for listening. Are there any questions? |
|----|----|
