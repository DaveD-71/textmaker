1.  **Vocabulary for the Chairperson**

**Getting people’s attention**

1.  May I have your attention, please? (Formal: for a large meeting)

2.  Alright, everybody. / OK, everybody (informal)

3.  Sorry to interrupt but I think we should get started now.

4.  I'd like to get started now.

5.  I think we should start now.

6.  Shall we start now?

7.  Can we start now?

8.  Shall we get down to business?

9.  I think we should get down to business now.

10. Could we get down to business now?

**Reviewing the agenda**

1.  Let’s take a look at the agenda.

2.  We have three items on the agenda this evening.

3.  Is there anything else for the agenda?

4.  Are there any other items for the agenda?

5.  Does anyone have anything else for the agenda?

6.  Does anyone want to add anything to the agenda?

**Organizing the agenda**

1.  Let’s start with … and then …

2.  I think we should start with … and then …

3.  I think our first item should be …

4.  I’d suggest we start with … and then …

5.  Let’s take a break after we finish …

6.  Mr. Ono, about how much time do you need for your presentation?

**Introducing the speaker**

1.  Our first / next speaker is Mr. Ono.

2.  Mr. Ono, would you give us your report, please?

3.  Mr. Ono?

**Speaker’s response to the chairperson**

1.  Thank you, Mr. Gerrard.

2.  It’s my pleasure.

3.  My pleasure. (Informal)

4.  … Good evening, everyone.

**Conducting the discussion and moving to a decision**

1.  Thank you, Mr. Ono. We have time for some questions.

2.  Are there any questions for Mr. Ono?

3.  Are there any more questions for Mr. Ono?

4.  Mr. Suzuki, do you have any questions for Mr. Ono?

5.  What do you think about this, Mr. Suzuki?

6.  Do you have any comments on this, Mr. Suzuki?

7.  Mr. Ono, what do you think about this?

8.  How do you feel about this?

> (Note: “<u>How</u> do you <u>think</u> about …?” is not correct.)

9.  I’d like to get your opinion on…

10. What do you think we should do?

11. Do you all agree with this proposal?

**Taking a break**

1.  It’s time for a break. We’ll begin again in 10 minutes.

2.  Why don’t we take a short break now? Let’s start again in 10 minutes.

3.  I think we should take a short break now.

4.  I think it’s time for a short break.

5.  We’ll start again at …

**Starting after a break**

1.  It’s time to start again.

2.  Shall we start again?

3.  Shall we continue?

4.  We’ll continue with …

**Wrapping up the discussion**

1.  We have time for one more question.

2.  If there are no more questions then we’ll move on to our next item.

3.  I’m sorry that’s all the time we have for questions.

4.  I’m sorry we’re all out of time.

5.  I’m sorry we have to bring this to a close.

6.  I’m sorry. This is very interesting but we have to move on to our next item / the next item on our agenda.

7.  Thank you everyone.

8.  Thank you for a very interesting presentation, Mr. Ono.

9.  That was a very informative report, Mr. Ono. Thank you very much.

2.  **Questions and Discussion**

**Introducing your question**

1.  I have a question.

2.  Could I ask (you) a question about …?

3.  Could I ask (you) about …?

4.  I’d like to ask (you) about ...

5.  I have two questions. First … Thank you, and my second question is …

**Note:** *“May I <u>have</u> a question?” is <u>never</u> correct.*

**Questions for clarification**

1.  I think you said … Is that correct?

2.  I think you told us that … Am I right?

3.  Did you say …?

4.  I'm sorry. You said something about … but I didn't understand it.

5.  Did you say something about …?

**More information**

1.  Could you tell me more about …?

2.  I’d like to get more information about …

3.  I'm sorry. You told us about … Could you give us some more details?

**Questions to challenge and test**

1.  You said … Could you tell us what you mean?

2.  You said … Could you be more specific?

3.  What’s the connection between A and B?

4.  I’m sorry. I don’t see the connection between A and B.

5.  I think … What's your opinion?

6.  You said … but don’t you think that ...?

3.  **Responding to Questions**

**Pausing to think**

> If you need time to think about a response you must say something: Silence is rude.

1.  That's an interesting question.

2.  Let me see.

3.  Let me think about that.

**Clarifying a question**

> Don’t try to answer a question if you don’t understand it. Always ask for clarification.

1.  I’m sorry. Could you repeat your question, please?

2.  Could you say that again, please?

3.  I'm sorry. Could you rephrase your question, please?

4.  I'm sorry. I don't understand your question.

**Giving a response**

> Avoid answering questions with just one word, “Yes” or “No”. Short blunt answers do not create an interactive atmosphere. Always expand on a simple answer.

1.  Yes, that's right.

2.  No. I think you may have misunderstood.

3.  Let me explain.

4.  I'd like to explain.

5.  I'm sorry. I don't have any more information about that.

4.  **Responding to answers**

    1.  I understand, thank you.

    2.  I see, thank you

    3.  I’m sorry. I’m not sure what you mean.

**Offering opinions**

1.  I think that …

2.  I feel that …

3.  Personally, I feel / believe / think that …

4.  It seems to me that …

5.  **Making Proposals**

<!-- -->

1.  I think we should …

2.  Can we …?

3.  Could we …?

4.  What about this idea? …

6.  **Agreeing** (See the “Two Points of Grammar” for agree + with/ to/on etc.)

When you agree you should summarize the points of agreement and disagreement, and explain why.

1.  I agree with you because …

2.  I agree with what you are saying because …

3.  I agree that we should …

4.  I see what you mean.

5.  You've got a point.

6.  You're right about that.

<!-- -->

7.  **Disagreeing**

<!-- -->

1.  I see what you mean, but I (still) can't agree with you. I think …

2.  I agree that something should be done, however, …

3.  I agree with your point that … but …

4.  That's true, but …

5.  I'm afraid I'm not entirely convinced.

6.  No, I don't think that's right. I think …

7.  I'm sorry, I don't agree because …

8.  I'm afraid I can't agree with you. I think …

8.  **Vocabulary for Discussion**

**Interrupting a discussion**

Sometimes it is necessary to join a conversation that you have been listening to. Be careful not to interrupt while someone is speaking. Wait until there is a pause in the conversation and then say …

1.  Excuse me. May I say something?

2.  I’m sorry, may I interrupt?

3.  Sorry to interrupt you but …

4.  I'd just like to say that …

5.  I’d like to add something.

6.  Could I add something?

**Accepting an interruption**

1.  Yes?

2.  Certainly, go ahead.

**Rejecting an interruption**

1.  I'm sorry, could I finish first? Then I'll answer your question.

2.  Could we discuss that after I finish?

3.  Could we take that up later?

4.  I’m sorry, I’d like to just finish what I’m saying.
