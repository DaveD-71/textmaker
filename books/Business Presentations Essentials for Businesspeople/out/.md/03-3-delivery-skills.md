# 3: Delivery Skills

- **Good delivery skills are essential for a good presentation.**

Good delivery skills build your confidence and win the attention of your audience.

- Your teacher will model each of these skills for you one-by-one.

- Stand up and practice the skills with your teacher until they are easy to do.

> **Speak to your audience. Deliver your message.**

- **Voice**

> Your voice is your most important delivery tool. \[\[LINEBREAK\]\]A clear strong voice delivers your message to your audience. If they cannot hear you they will not receive your message.

- Speak clearly, smoothly, and without hesitation.

- Don't rush or speak too quickly.\[\[LINEBREAK\]\]Emphasize the important words and phrases.

- Eliminate the pauses and *Ummmms*.

<!-- -->

- **Eye Contact**

> Eye contact connects the speaker and the audience.

- Look at your audience, not at your notes.

- Look at the whole audience. Move your head so you can make eye contact with everyone.

<!-- -->

- **Notes**

> Hold your notes just above your waist where you can easily see them and look at your audience.

- Hold the page in the middle so it won’t fold over.

- Don’t move your notes up and down: it’s very distracting.

- Don't put your notes on a table or lectern – If you're looking down at your notes, you can't look at your audience.

<!-- -->

- **Posture**

> Good posture helps your breathing and makes you appear more confident.

- Stand straight with your feet slightly apart and your weight evenly on both feet.

- Don't lean on the table or lectern – look at your audience.

<!-- -->

- **Gestures**

> There are three positions for your other hand: by your side, behind your back, or gesturing.
>
> Gesturing is the best thing to do with your free hand:

- Gestures attract your audience’s attention and emphasize important points.

- Gestures should be simple, firm and clear.

- Gestures and eye contact go together. When you gesture, look at your audience. If you are not looking at your audience, <u>do not</u> gesture.

- When you count (1st, 2nd, 3rd), gesture with one finger, two fingers, three fingers.

- Don’t count with your fingers if you say, "My next point is…" or "My last point is…"

- Don't scratch your face or play with your hair!

- Don’t cover your face with your hand if you adjust your glasses.

<!-- -->

- **Clothing – Neatness counts!**

> Make sure your clothing is appropriate for the situation and then wear it neatly.

- Messy clothing and hair will distract your audience from your message.

- Before you speak make sure your clothing is neat: check your tie, your jacket collar, and your hair.

<!-- -->

- **Tips for Effective Speaking**

1.  **Starting Your Speech**

> Your speech begins when you are called to speak.
>
> Before you speak, pause, look at your audience, and get their attention. Take a deep breath and begin.

2.  **Speak to Your Audience**

> If you memorize parts of your speech, you can look at your audience. Memorize –

1.  your greetings

2.  your introduction

3.  the beginning of each main point

4.  your conclusion and closing thoughts

<!-- -->

3.  **Speak Clearly and with Passion**

> Let your audience know that you care about your topic: Speak with energy and enthusiasm in your voice.

- Emphasize important words with your voice and with gestures.

- Pause slightly before and after names, dates, numbers, and important words.

> For example: “In^**2012**,^**Protech**^sold^**187,000** units of ^**PX-Y40**.”
