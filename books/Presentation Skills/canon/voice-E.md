# Presentation Skills — "Essentials" Tier Style Guide

## 1. Voice Description

Essentials speaks like a coach standing next to you before you walk on stage: short, flat, imperative statements that tell the learner what to physically do, in the order they'll do it. Average sentence length is 6–12 words; never exceed roughly 15. Most sentences are single-clause commands (verb first or subject-you-implied). Formality is plain and direct — contractions are fine, jargon is not. Skip the "why" unless it fits in three words or fewer; if a rule needs justifying, justify it with a consequence ("or you'll lose the room"), not a theory. Frameworks are introduced as numbered steps or a fixed list, never as prose narrative — name it, give the steps, move on. There is no throat-clearing: no "in this unit we will explore," no scene-setting, no acknowledgment that the topic is hard. Assume the learner has five minutes and needs to be doing the skill, not reading about it.

## 2. Sample Sentences (pattern-match anchors)

- **Eye contact:** "Pick three people in the room. Look at each one for a full sentence, not a glance. Rotate. Never scan the whole crowd like a lighthouse."
- **Introducing a framework:** "Use the PREP structure: Point, Reason, Example, Point. State your point first. Give one reason. Give one example. Restate the point. Done."
- **Opening a talk:** "Cut the small talk. Say your first sentence before you reach the lectern. If you need notes for your opening line, you haven't practiced enough."

## 3. Avoid List

- **Hedging language** — no "might," "could consider," "in some cases." Say what to do.
- **Multi-clause sentences** — no sentences built on "which," "although," or three commas. One idea, one sentence.
- **Rhetorical questions** — no "Why does this matter?" Just state the instruction.
- **Theory/backstory** — no research citations, no "studies show," no history of the technique.
- **Passive voice** — no "eye contact should be maintained." Say "hold eye contact."
- **Qualifiers and softeners** — cut "just," "simply," "a little," "try to."
- **Scene-setting intros** — no "In this section, we'll cover…" Start with the instruction.
- **Metaphor stacking or flourish** — one plain image max, only if it clarifies an action; no extended analogies.
- **Options without a default** — don't list five ways to do something with no pick; give the one to use.
- **Empathy padding** — no "we know this can feel scary." Acknowledge nothing; instruct.

This guide is intended to be pasted verbatim into each unit-drafting agent's prompt for the Essentials tier.
