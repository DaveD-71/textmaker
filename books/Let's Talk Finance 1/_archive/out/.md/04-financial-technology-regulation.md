## 4. Financial Technology (Fintech) Regulation

### Reading

Financial market fragmentation refers to the segmentation and divergence of global financial markets into separate and distinct segments characterized by differences in regulations, trading venues, and market structures. Financial market fragmentation can arise from various factors such as regulatory divergence, jurisdictional boundaries, technological barriers, and market fragmentation regulations such as MiFID II in the European Union.

Fragmentation can lead to inefficiencies, increased costs, reduced liquidity, and market fragmentation risks such as arbitrage opportunities, market abuse, and regulatory arbitrage. Moreover, fragmentation can hinder the integration and interoperability of financial markets, impeding cross-border capital flows, and hindering market efficiency and resilience.

Regulators and policymakers are seeking to address financial market fragmentation through initiatives such as regulatory harmonization, equivalence agreements, mutual recognition frameworks, and regulatory sandboxes to promote consistency, convergence, and interoperability across jurisdictions. Moreover, technological innovations such as distributed ledger technology (DLT) and digital assets have the potential to mitigate market fragmentation by enabling decentralized and interoperable financial infrastructure that transcends traditional market boundaries.
