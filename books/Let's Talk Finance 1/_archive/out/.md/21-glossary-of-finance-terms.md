## Glossary of Finance Terms

This glossary compiles key finance-related terms found across all 20 articles in *Let’s Talk: Finance*. Each definition is written in plain English, with short references to the article(s) where the term appears.

A

Adverse Selection. When insurers end up with riskier customers because safer people opt out. *(Art. 18)*

AI in Finance. Use of machine learning/automation for risk, fraud detection, trading and service. *(Art. 5)*

AML (Anti‑Money Laundering). Rules and tools that stop criminals from hiding illegal money. *(Art. 1, 5)*

Amortization. Gradually spreading the cost of an asset or loan over time. *(Art. 3)*

Annuity. A contract that pays a steady stream of income, often for retirement. *(Art. 12)*

Asset. Something valuable a person or company owns. *(Art. 3, 9)*

AUC (Auction) / Price Discovery. How markets find fair prices via supply and demand. *(Art. 11)*

B

Balance Sheet. A snapshot of what a company owns and owes at one point in time. *(Art. 3)*

Basel / BCBS (Basel Committee on Banking Supervision). International bank‑safety standards set by the Basel Committee. *(Art. 5)*

Basis Point (bp). One‑hundredth of a percentage point (0.01%). *(Art. 13)*

Bearer Bond. A bond owned by whoever physically holds it (historical/illustrative). *(Art. 9)*

Beta. How much an investment tends to move compared with the market. *(Art. 11)*

Bid‑Ask Spread. The gap between the buying (bid) and selling (ask) price; a measure of market liquidity. *(Art. 11)*

Biometric Authentication. Proving identity with physical traits (fingerprint/face). *(Art. 6)*

Bitcoin. A well‑known cryptocurrency. *(Art. 1)*

Blockchain. A shared digital ledger used to record transactions. *(Art. 1, 3, 4)*

Bond. A loan to a government or company that pays interest and returns principal. *(Art. 9, 17)*

C

Capital Controls. Rules that restrict money moving in/out of a country. *(Art. 20)*

Capital Flows. Money crossing borders for trade or investment. *(Art. 20)*

Carbon Credit. A tradable permit to emit a set amount of greenhouse gases. *(Art. 2)*

Cash Flow. Money entering and leaving a business over time. *(Art. 3)*

CBDC (Central Bank Digital Currency). Digital cash issued by a central bank. *(Art. 3, 4)*

CDS (Credit Default Swap). A contract that protects against a borrower failing to pay. *(Art. 12)*

Collateral. Property pledged to secure a loan. *(Art. 10)*

Corporate Governance. How a company is directed and controlled. *(Art. 13)*

Counterparty Risk. The risk the other party in a contract can’t meet obligations. *(Art. 12)*

Coupon (Bond). The periodic interest payment on a bond. *(Art. 9)*

Credit Rating Agency. Firm that grades how risky borrowers or securities are. *(Art. 17)*

Credit Risk. The chance a borrower won’t repay on time or in full. *(Art. 9, 17)*

Cryptocurrency. Digital money that uses cryptography and runs without a central authority. *(Art. 1)*

D

Data Breach. Unauthorized access to personal/financial data. *(Art. 6)*

DeFi (Decentralized Finance). Financial services built on blockchains without traditional intermediaries. *(Art. 1)*

Defined Benefit Plan. Pension that pays a set benefit in retirement. *(Art. 12)*

Defined Contribution Plan. Pension where you and/or employer contribute; benefit depends on investments. *(Art. 12)*

Devaluation. When a government lowers its currency’s official value. *(Art. 20)*

Derivatives. Contracts whose value comes from other assets (stocks, bonds, commodities). *(Art. 12, 18)*

Discount Rate. An interest rate used to convert future money into today’s value. *(Art. 14)*

Diversification. Spreading investments to reduce risk. *(Art. 11)*

DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology). Tech for shared databases; includes blockchain. *(Art. 3, 4)*

Duration (Bond). How sensitive a bond is to interest‑rate changes. *(Art. 9)*

E

Encryption. Protecting data so only authorized people can read it. *(Art. 6)*

ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance). Standards investors use to judge sustainability and responsibility. *(Art. 2)*

ETF (Exchange‑Traded Fund). A fund that trades like a stock but holds a basket of assets. *(Art. 11)*

Ethereum. A blockchain that enables smart contracts and apps (and its currency, Ether). *(Art. 1)*

Exchange Rate. How much one currency is worth in another currency. *(Art. 20)*

FDI (Foreign Direct Investment). Investing to control or significantly influence a business abroad. *(Art. 20)*

Fintech. Technology that improves or automates financial services. *(Art. 6)*

Fiscal Policy. Government taxing and spending decisions. *(Art. 14, 20)*

Flash Crash. A sudden, deep, and short‑lived market drop. *(Art. 11)*

G

GDP (Gross Domestic Product). The value of all goods and services produced in a country. *(Art. 13, 20)*

GDPR / CCPA. Privacy laws governing personal data use (EU/California). *(Art. 6)*

Green Bond. A bond where the proceeds fund environmental projects. *(Art. 2)*

Green Finance. Financing that supports sustainability goals. *(Art. 2)*

H

Hedge. An offsetting position to reduce risk. *(Art. 11)*

Hedge Fund. An investment fund using advanced strategies. *(Art. 11)*

High‑Frequency Trading (HFT). Ultra‑fast trading using algorithms and low‑latency tech. *(Art. 11)*

I

Impact Investing. Investing for returns and measurable social/environmental benefits. *(Art. 2, 15)*

Income Statement. A report of revenues, expenses, and profit for a period. *(Art. 3)*

Inflation. A general rise in prices over time. *(Art. 13, 20)*

Insurance Solvency. An insurer’s ability to pay claims. *(Art. 18)*

Interest Rate. The price of borrowing money. *(Art. 13, 20)*

IPO (Initial Public Offering). A company’s first sale of stock to the public. *(Art. 11)*

K

KYC (Know Your Customer). Banks verifying identity to reduce fraud and money‑laundering. *(Art. 1)*

L

Leverage. Using borrowed money to amplify gains (and losses). *(Art. 11)*

LIBOR. A benchmark interest rate used globally (being phased out). *(Art. 13)*

Liquidity. How quickly and easily an asset can be converted to cash. *(Art. 11)*

M

Macroprudential Policy. System‑wide rules to reduce financial‑system risks. *(Art. 11)*

Market Index. A basket of securities that tracks market performance. *(Art. 11)*

Market Liquidity. How quickly assets can be bought/sold without moving the price much. *(Art. 11)*

Market Maker. A firm that quotes buy/sell prices and provides liquidity. *(Art. 11)*

Market Risk. Risk of losses due to market‑wide moves. *(Art. 11)*

Microfinance. Small loans and services for people lacking traditional banking. *(Art. 10)*

MiFID II. EU market rules that protect investors and improve transparency. *(Art. 4)*

Monetary Policy. Central‑bank actions (interest rates, asset purchases). *(Art. 14, 20)*

Mutual Fund. A pooled fund that invests in a range of assets. *(Art. 11)*

N

NFT (Non‑Fungible Token). A unique blockchain token representing ownership of a digital item. *(Art. 1)*

Non‑Performing Loan (NPL). A loan on which the borrower has stopped paying. *(Art. 9)*

O

Operational Risk. Losses from processes, systems, people, or external events. *(Art. 11)*

Option. A contract giving the right, not obligation, to buy/sell at a set price. *(Art. 12)*

Order Book. A list of buy and sell orders at different prices. *(Art. 11)*

P

Portfolio. All the investments an investor owns. *(Art. 11)*

Principal. The face amount of a loan or bond to be repaid at maturity. *(Art. 9)*

Private Equity. Investment in private, non‑listed companies. *(Art. 9)*

Protectionism. Policies that protect domestic industries (tariffs/quotas). *(Art. 8)*

Q

Quantitative Easing (QE). Central bank buying assets to add money to the economy. *(Art. 14)*

Quota. A limit on the quantity of goods that can be imported. *(Art. 8)*

R

Rating Shopping. Issuers seeking the best (most favorable) credit rating. *(Art. 17)*

Reinsurance. Insurance purchased by insurers to share risk. *(Art. 18)*

Remittance. Money migrants send back to family in their home country. *(Art. 20)*

Return. The gain or loss on an investment. *(Art. 11)*

Rollover Risk. Risk a borrower cannot refinance maturing debt. *(Art. 9)*

S

Secondary Market. Where existing securities are bought and sold. *(Art. 11)*

Securities / Security. Tradable financial assets (e.g., stocks, bonds, derivatives). *(Art. 7, 11, 12)*

Smart Contract. Code on a blockchain that runs automatically when conditions are met. *(Art. 3)*

Sovereign Bonds / Sovereign Debt. Government borrowing via bonds/bills. *(Art. 9)*

Stablecoin. Crypto designed to hold a steady value (often pegged to USD). *(Art. 1)*

Stress Test. Scenario test to see if banks/markets can withstand shocks. *(Art. 11, 19)*

T

Tariff. Tax on imported goods; also referenced in “tariff wars.” *(Art. 8, 20)*

Tokenization. Replacing sensitive data with non‑sensitive “tokens.” *(Art. 6)*

Trade Imbalance / Deficit. When a country imports more than it exports. *(Art. 20)*

Trading Halt. A temporary pause in trading for a security or the market. *(Art. 11)*

U

UBI (Universal Basic Income). Regular payments to all citizens to support basic needs. *(Art. 15)*

Unbanked / Underbanked. People without access to traditional banking. *(Art. 10)*

W

WTO (World Trade Organization). Global body that sets trade rules and resolves disputes. *(Art. 8)*

Yield. Income return on an investment (interest/dividends), expressed annually. *(Art. 9, 13)*

Yield Curve. A graph of bond yields across maturities. *(Art. 9, 13)*
