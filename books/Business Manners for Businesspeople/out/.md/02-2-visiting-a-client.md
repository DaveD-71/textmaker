# 2 Visiting a Client

> <img src="assets/media/image4.png" style="width:0.80625in;height:0.43194in" /> *Introductions, greetings, & courtesies*

- Business Situation: Visiting a client - Atlas Construction

> Takashi Ikeda visits a client, Amanda Jones, for the first time. Ms Jones works in the purchasing department of Atlas Construction. Mr. Ikeda and Ms Jones have talked on the phone and exchanged e-mail but they have not met before.
>
> Mr. Ikeda arrives at the client’s office and goes to the reception desk.

1.  At the reception desk

> Mr. Ikeda gives his name, his company’s name and appointment information.
>
> Recep’t. Good morning. How may I help you?
>
> Ikeda Good morning. My name is Takashi Ikeda. I’m with Nippon Taiyo. I have an appointment with Amanda Jones at 10:30.
>
> Recep’t. One moment please. I’ll call Ms Jones (Calls Ms Jones). Mr. Ikeda?
>
> Ikeda Yes?
>
> Recep’t. Ms. Jones will come down to meet you.
>
> Ikeda Thank you.
>
> Recep’t. Here is a visitor’s badge. Please wear it in the building and return it when you leave.
>
> Ikeda Thank you. I’ll do that.
>
> Recep’t. Ms Jones will meet you over there in the waiting area (Gesturing).
>
> Ikeda Thank you very much.

2.  Meeting for the first time

> Ms Jones is expecting to meet Mr. Ikeda; however, they have not met before so she begins by confirming his identity.
>
> Jones Good morning, Mr. Ikeda?
>
> Ikeda Yes that’s right. I’m Takashi Ikeda from Nippon Taiyo.
>
> Jones (Holds out her hand to shake) I’m Amanda Jones. It’s a pleasure to meet you, Mr. Ikeda.
>
> Ikeda Good morning, Ms Jones. It’s a pleasure to meet you, too. This is my card.
>
> Jones Thank you. And here’s mine.
>
> Ikeda Thank you very much.
>
> Jones Please come this way. The meeting room is over here.
>
> Ikeda Thank you.
>
> Jones (In the meeting room) Please have a seat here. (Gesturing)
>
> Ikeda (Sitting down) Thank you.

3.  Meeting <u>again</u>

> The next time Ms Jones and Mr. Ikeda meet they will use different greetings.
>
> Jones Good morning, Mr. Ikeda (Holds out her hand to shake).
>
> Ikeda Good morning, Ms Jones. It’s nice to see you again.
>
> Jones It’s nice to see you again, too. How have you been?
>
> Ikeda I’ve been very well, thank you. And you?
>
> Jones Very well, too, thank you. The meeting room is this way.

- Notes

1.  **At the reception desk**

> You can use the same expressions when you use a reception phone.

2.  **First meetings**

> Ms Jones and Mr. Ikeda introduce themselves with *I’m…　*not *My name is…*
>
> *I’m …* is used to identify yourself when the other person is expecting to meet you, as in the case of Ms Jones and Mr. Ikeda. They had an appointment so they were expecting to see each other.

3.  **Meeting again**

> Ms Jones and Mr. Ikeda greet each other with *It’s nice to <u>see</u> you again.*
>
> *It’s nice to <u>meet</u> you again.* is not a correct greeting. Don’t say this.

4.  ***How are you? / How have you been?***

> The expression, *How are you?*, is a standard part of courteous greetings. It is not a question about health. <u>Never</u> respond, *I’m a little sick. I’m tired.*
>
> Correct responses are:

- *Fine thank you, and you?*

- *Very well, thank you. And you?*

- *Fine thanks, and you?* (Informal.)

<!-- -->

- *How have you been?* means, *since the last time we met*. The appropriate response is *I<u>’ve</u> been very well, thank you. And you?*

5.  **Business Cards**

> Exchanging business cards at introductions is standard business practice throughout the world, but the protocol differs from culture to culture:

- In Asia, business cards are a key part of the introduction: they are presented and received with two hands and are treated with great respect.

- In North America, the key part of the introduction is the person to person contact, not the business card. The business card is an additional useful element. Cards are usually presented and received with one hand and treated more casually.

- Don’t be offended by casual handling of business cards and don’t imitate it.

- <img src="assets/media/image5.png" style="width:0.87708in;height:0.44306in" />Maintain the Japanese style of presenting and receiving business cards. It’s a sign of respect for the other person that is easily understood across cultures.
