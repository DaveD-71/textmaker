- **What is Logic?**

> The way we think and organize our ideas is part of the culture in which we are raised and educated. Thinking is a skill, which is learned.
>
> Western logic is based largely on the ideas of Greek philosophers who borrowed some of their ideas from other people such as Egyptian and Hebrew scholars.
>
> Scholars spend years studying and working with logic. It is not a simple subject, however, the most basic form of Western logic is:

**A + B = C**

> **A** is a fact or observation.
>
> **B** is another fact or observation.
>
> **C** is a clearly stated new idea or conclusion based on connecting A and B.

- **The Goal of Logic**

> The goal of western logic is to analyze information systematically and present ideas clearly so that they can be easily understood and discussed.
>
> The first goal of logic is understanding. The second goal is agreement.

- **Samples of A + B = C**

**Sample 1: Observation**

Using observations to understand a situation:

**A** Mr. Jones is carrying an umbrella.

**B** The umbrella is wet.

**C** Therefore, it is probably raining.

**Sample 2: Ideas**

Using ideas to make or support an opinion:

**A** Smoking cigarettes is unhealthy.

**B** Smoke hurts everyone's eyes and throats.

**C** Therefore, people should not smoke in public areas.

- **Reasoning Practice 2: Why? / Because - An Interactive Logic Game**

1.  Write a statement such as -

> *I am going to the store.*
>
> *I have to work overtime.*

2.  The students write down 2 or 3 reasons why.

> *I have to work overtime because…*

3.  Ask one student to read his or her statement. The other students ask in turn-

> *Why did you…?*
>
> The first student responds with one of the reasons.

4.  After the students have all had a chance, make it more challenging. After the first student answers why he did something, the next students ask about that reason.

> For example:
>
> A. I went to the store.
>
> B. <u>Why</u> did you go to the store?
>
> A. I went to the store to buy some bread.
>
> B. <u>Why</u> did you buy bread?
>
> A. I bought bread <u>because</u> there wasn't any at home.
>
> B. <u>Why</u> wasn't there any bread at home?
>
> A. I didn’t have any bread at home <u>because</u>…
>
> And continue…

- **  
  How to Organize Information**

\[\[PAGEBREAK\]\]

> Information must be organized to be useful.
>
> You have to decide how to organize your information in an effective way. Organize your information to support the purpose of your speech.

- **You can organize information according to -**

1.  **Time**

    - From past to present to future.

> For example: What happened in the past? What is happening now? What will happen next?

2.  **Importance**

    - From least important to most important.

    - Usually, the most important item is last; however, the speaker must decide the order.

3.  **Type**

    - Keep the same type of information together.
